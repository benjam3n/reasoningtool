## Phase 1: EXPLORATION

### U0: Ground in Exemplars

**What does "good" look like for thinking toolkits that persisted through technology shifts?**

1. **Spreadsheets (VisiCalc -> Lotus -> Excel -> Google Sheets -> AI-augmented):** The concept survived but the interface transformed completely. The underlying model (grid of cells with formulas) proved durable. The procedures people used changed every generation.
2. **Programming IDEs (vi -> Emacs -> Visual Studio -> VSCode -> AI-assisted coding):** Each generation made explicit procedures less necessary (autocomplete replaced memorized syntax), but new procedural knowledge emerged at higher abstraction levels.
3. **Decision frameworks (Ben Franklin's pros/cons -> decision matrices -> Bayesian reasoning -> AI-assisted decision tools):** The core insight (decompose to decide) persisted. The execution layer changed entirely. Most specific frameworks were forgotten; a few became cultural defaults.
4. **Checklists (aviation -> surgery -> software deployment):** Atul Gawande's work showed that simple procedural thinking tools persist when the domain has high stakes and high complexity. They survived despite increasing automation.

**What they share (likely fundamental):** Decomposition of complex tasks. Forcing structure on unstructured thinking. Compensating for known cognitive failure modes.

**What they differ on (likely stylistic):** Interface, level of detail, who executes (human vs machine), how invoked.

**Felt impression:** The concept of structured thinking assistance will persist. The specific form of "563 markdown files executed by an AI" is an implementation detail that may not survive. Something deeper will.

### U1: Map the Space

**Unpacking the input:**

[U1] EXPLICIT: The question asks what a thinking toolkit would look like in 5 years (2031).

[U5] META: This is a futures question. It admits of scenarios, not answers. The temptation is to predict; the useful move is to map the scenario space.

**Technique 1: STATE SPACE — What states could a thinking toolkit be in by 2031?**

[U6] State: EVOLVED — The toolkit concept persists but the form transforms. Skills become adaptive algorithms, not static markdown. AI models internalize the procedures and apply them without explicit invocation.
[U7] State: ABSORBED — AI models become so capable that structured procedures add zero value. The toolkit is obsolete because the AI already reasons better than any procedure can guide it.
[U8] State: ESSENTIAL — As AI gets more capable, the value of structured thinking INCREASES because the space of possible AI actions grows and users need more guidance, not less, to direct that capability.
[U9] State: NICHE — The toolkit survives but only for specific edge cases: high-stakes decisions, regulated domains, educational contexts. Most users never need it.
[U10] State: WRONG QUESTION — "Thinking toolkit" is a transitional concept. The real question is about the interface between human judgment and AI capability. By 2031 this looks nothing like a "toolkit."

**Technique 2: INSTANCE-TO-CATEGORY — What is this an instance of?**

[U11] A "thinking toolkit for AI" is an instance of HUMAN-MACHINE INTERFACE DESIGN — specifically, the design of how humans direct and constrain machine reasoning.
[U12] Sibling: Prompt engineering frameworks (emerged ~2023, already declining in explicit form by 2026 as models improve).
[U13] Sibling: Programming languages themselves — structured procedures that humans write for machines to execute. The abstraction level keeps rising.
[U14] Sibling: Management consulting frameworks (McKinsey's 7S, BCG matrix) — structured thinking templates that humans apply to business problems. These have persisted for 50+ years despite being "obviously" something a smart person could do without.

**Technique 3: PARAMETER VARIATION**

[U15] Parameter: AI capability level — current: GPT-4/Claude-class (strong but imperfect reasoning) — range: current to superhuman general reasoning.
[U16] Parameter: User sophistication — current: mostly power users who know CLI — range: complete novices to expert reasoners.
[U17] Parameter: Interface modality — current: text-based CLI — range: text to voice to brain-computer to ambient.
[U18] Parameter: Procedure explicitness — current: fully explicit markdown — range: fully explicit to fully internalized/implicit.

**Technique 4: PERSPECTIVE ROTATION**

[U19] AI RESEARCHER sees: "These are training data. The best skills will be distilled into model behavior. The toolkit is a stepping stone."
[U20] ENTERPRISE USER sees: "I need auditable reasoning trails. The toolkit is compliance infrastructure, not a thinking aid."
[U21] COGNITIVE SCIENTIST sees: "Structured reasoning procedures are cognitive prosthetics. The question is whether the prosthetic gets built into the body (the AI) or stays external."
[U22] SKEPTIC sees: "Prompt engineering was a fad. Thinking toolkits are prompt engineering with extra steps. They'll follow the same trajectory."

**Technique 5: ASSUMPTION EXTRACTION**

[U23] LOAD-BEARING: AI models will continue to improve at reasoning tasks — if false: toolkits become MORE important as compensating structures for weak AI.
[U24] LOAD-BEARING: Users will still want to DIRECT AI reasoning, not just consume AI outputs — if false: toolkits are irrelevant because users just say "solve this" and trust the result.
[U25] LOAD-BEARING: The value of structured thinking procedures is separable from the current implementation — if false: the toolkit dies with markdown-as-interface.
[U26] BACKGROUND: AI companies will continue to offer API/CLI access, not just consumer chat interfaces.

**Technique 6: DIMENSION DISCOVERY**

[U27] Dimension: Explicitness of procedure — toolkit currently sits at: maximally explicit. The trend may be toward implicit.
[U28] HIDDEN dimension: Trust calibration — how much should a user trust AI reasoning without procedural guardrails? This is the real product.
[U29] HIDDEN dimension: Reasoning auditability — can you trace HOW the AI reached a conclusion? Procedures create an audit trail that "just asking" does not.

**Technique 7: TEMPORAL VARIATION**

[U30] Short-term (1-2 years): Toolkits gain value. AI is good enough to execute procedures but not good enough to replace them. Sweet spot.
[U31] Medium-term (3-5 years): Fork point. Either AI reasoning becomes reliable enough that explicit procedures add friction, or the complexity of tasks grows to keep procedures valuable.
[U32] Long-term (5-10 years): If procedures survive, they look nothing like markdown files. They're embedded in model training, or they're dynamic protocols, or they're trust-verification systems.
