# /reframe Reframe "I have 592 skills and no users" into something that reveals what to do next
**Date:** 2026-03-09
**Skill:** /reframe (Reframe)

---

**Interpretation selected:** Interpretation 3 — Generate multiple framings for strategic choice. Ben is not stuck on a mystery. He knows the situation. He wants to see it through enough lenses that the right next action becomes obvious.

**Depth:** 2x (default). Minimum 3 lenses, 5 alternative frames, 2 constraints questioned, 2 stakeholder perspectives.

---

## Phase 1: CAPTURE — Understand the Current Frame

### Step 1: Articulate the Current Frame

```
CURRENT FRAME:

[F1] Problem as stated: "I have 592 skills and no users."

[F2] Implied cause: The project has not been marketed or distributed
     effectively. The work is done; the audience is missing.

[F3] Implied solution direction: Find users. Market the product. Get
     the word out. Distribution activities — subreddit, posts, outreach.

[F4] Success criteria: Users appear. Download numbers, website traffic,
     community members. The gap between "592 skills" and "0 users" closes.

[F5] Who owns it: Ben, as sole creator and distributor.

[F6] Constraints assumed:
     - The 592 skills are the product (finished, valuable as-is)
     - Users are the missing ingredient
     - Ben must find/attract users himself
     - Unemployment means this needs to work (financial pressure)

[F7] Time horizon: Immediate. The juxtaposition of "592" and "0" frames
     this as an urgent gap to close now.

[F8] What this frame makes INVISIBLE:
     - Whether anyone needs 592 skills (vs. 5 that work perfectly)
     - Whether "users" is even the right next milestone
     - Whether the product is actually finished or actually started
     - What a single user would DO with this if they found it today
     - Whether Ben is the first user, and whether it's working for him
```

### Step 2: Diagnose Frame Limitations

```
FRAME DIAGNOSIS:

[F9] Is this a SOLUTION frame disguised as a problem?
  YES. "I have 592 skills and no users" implies the solution is "get users."
  The underlying problem is: "I don't know if what I've built creates value
  for anyone, and I have no feedback signal to tell me."

[F10] Is this framed at the right level of abstraction?
  Too concrete: "592 skills" treats the inventory count as the asset.
     The count is not the asset. The transformation a user undergoes is.
  Too abstract: No — the frame is actually fairly specific, but it
     conflates "having skills" with "having a product."

[F11] Whose perspective dominates this frame?
  The builder's. Ben sees inputs (skills written, hours spent, website built)
  and outputs (user count = 0). This is a production frame. It is blind to
  the consumption experience — what it feels like to arrive, try one skill,
  and either leave or stay.

[F12] What constraint is doing the most work in this frame?
  The assumption that the 592 skills ARE the product — that the work is done
  and only distribution remains. If this assumption is wrong, the entire
  solution space shifts from "find users" to "find the product inside the
  592 skills."
```

## Phase 2: GENERATE — Create Genuinely Different Frames

### Lens 1: Change the Level of Abstraction

```
[F13] ONE LEVEL UP (more abstract):
  Problem becomes: "I've built a system for procedural thinking but
  have no evidence it helps anyone think better."
  Now the solution space includes: Running the skills on real problems
  with real people and measuring whether outcomes improve. Validation
  before distribution.
  But loses: Urgency. Validation takes time Ben may not have.

[F14] ONE LEVEL DOWN (more concrete):
  Problem becomes: "No single person has completed a single skill and
  reported what happened."
  Now the solution space includes: Get ONE person to use ONE skill on
  ONE real problem and document the result. That's it. Not 592 skills
  to the world — one skill to one person.
  But loses: The grand vision. But what it gains is a feedback signal,
  which is worth more than a vision right now.
```

### Lens 2: Change the Stakeholder Perspective

```
[F15] From a potential user's perspective:
  Problem becomes: "I have a thinking problem right now. I google it,
  I find nothing from this project. If I somehow land on the website,
  I see 592 skills and have no idea which one to use or why I should
  trust any of them. There is no proof this works. No story. No result.
  Just a big menu."
  What they see that the original frame misses: The product is not 592
  skills — the product is the answer to "will this help me with my
  specific problem RIGHT NOW?" 592 is a liability from their perspective,
  not an asset. It signals "this person built an encyclopedia, not a tool."
  What they miss: The depth and interconnection of the system.

[F16] From a hiring manager's perspective (person most affected by Ben's
  unemployment, least consulted about this project):
  Problem becomes: "This candidate spent months building 592 markdown
  files. What does that demonstrate? Obsessive productivity? Product
  sense? Engineering skill? I can't tell."
  What they see: The project as a portfolio piece, a signal of
  capability. They would want to see: clear problem statement, user
  research, iteration based on feedback, measurable outcomes.
  What they miss: The intellectual depth of the system.
```

### Lens 3: Change the Time Horizon

```
[F18] SHORT-TERM frame (this week):
  Problem becomes: "What is the single most valuable thing I can do
  in the next 7 days to get a feedback signal from a real person?"
  Solutions available: Post one skill solving one real problem in a
  place where that problem is being discussed (a subreddit, a forum,
  a Discord). Not "here's my project" — "here's a structured way to
  think about the thing you're struggling with." One skill. One context.
  One response.
  Risk: Optimizes for speed over strategy. But the real risk is
  continuing to build without feedback.

[F19] LONG-TERM frame (1-5 years):
  Problem becomes: "How does procedural thinking become a category
  that people seek out, and how does this project become the
  reference implementation?"
  Solutions available: Category creation through content, education,
  community. Writing about WHY procedural thinking matters, not just
  offering the tools. Becoming the person who articulates the idea,
  not just the person who built the tools.
  Risk: Ignores that Ben is unemployed and needs near-term signal.

[F20] RETROSPECTIVE frame (looking back from success):
  "What was the real problem?" becomes: "The real problem was that I
  treated building skills as the work, when the work was finding out
  which skills actually change outcomes for people. I had to stop
  building and start testing. The first 10 users taught me more than
  skills 100 through 592 ever did."
  Key insight: The 592 skills are not the product. They're the raw
  material. The product hasn't been built yet — it gets built through
  contact with real users and real problems.
```

### Lens 4: Change the Constraint Set

```
[F21] Remove the biggest constraint:
  If "592 skills are the product" were not assumed, the problem becomes:
  "I have deep expertise in procedural thinking and 592 prototypes.
  What is the actual product?"
  Is this constraint real? NO. It is an assumption born from sunk cost.
  The skills are raw material, not finished goods.
  What would it take to remove it? Accept that the valuable thing might
  be 5 skills, or a different format entirely, or Ben's ability to
  create skills live for people — not the existing library.

[F22] Add a constraint:
  If you could only keep 5 skills, which 5? The problem becomes:
  "What is the minimum viable toolkit that demonstrably helps one
  person with one type of problem?" This forces curation, which
  forces understanding of who the user is and what they actually need.
  Does the added constraint force a more creative solution? Yes.
  It shifts from "distribute the library" to "find the greatest hits."

[F23] Invert a constraint:
  "No users" as a resource: Having zero users means zero legacy, zero
  expectations, zero backward compatibility. Ben can pivot the entire
  product, rename it, restructure it, pick a niche, change the format,
  and no one is inconvenienced. Zero users is maximum freedom.
  This is the one moment where radical change costs nothing.
```

### Lens 5: Change the Success Criteria

```
[F24] Different metric:
  If success were measured by "depth of transformation in one person"
  instead of "number of users":
  Problem becomes: "How do I find one person, help them use one skill
  on a real problem, and document whether their thinking actually
  improved?"
  Different actions suggested: Stop all distribution work. Find one
  person. Run one skill. Measure the result. That's the product
  research Ben has never done.

[F25] Different beneficiary:
  If we were solving this for Ben himself:
  Problem becomes: "Am I using these 592 skills? Are they making my
  own thinking better? Can I point to decisions I made better because
  of them?"
  What insight does this yield? If Ben can't demonstrate that the
  skills work on his own problems (like "how do I get users" — which
  is itself a thinking problem), that's a red flag. If he CAN, those
  demonstrations ARE the marketing.
```

## Phase 3: SELECT — Choose the Most Useful Frame

### Step 4: Compare Frames

```
FRAME COMPARISON:

| Frame | Problem Statement | Solution Space | Actionability | What It Reveals | What It Hides |
|-------|------------------|----------------|---------------|-----------------|---------------|
| Original [F1] | "592 skills, 0 users" | Marketing, outreach, distribution | Medium | The gap exists | Whether the gap is the right one to close |
| Abstraction up [F13] | "No evidence it helps anyone" | Validation, testing, measurement | Medium | Need for proof | Specific next step |
| Abstraction down [F14] | "Zero people have completed one skill" | Get one person through one skill | HIGH | The atomic unit of progress | The scale question |
| User perspective [F15] | "592 skills is overwhelming, not inviting" | Curation, entry points, proof | HIGH | The consumption experience | The builder's investment |
| Time short [F18] | "What gets feedback this week?" | One skill, one context, one post | HIGH | The urgency of signal | Long-term positioning |
| Constraint removed [F21] | "What is the actual product?" | Rebuild around what works | Medium | Skills are raw material, not product | The value of the existing library |
| Constraint added [F22] | "Which 5 skills?" | Curation, niche focus | HIGH | Forced prioritization | Breadth of the system |
| Constraint inverted [F23] | "Zero users = maximum freedom" | Radical pivot, niche down, restructure | Medium | Freedom to change everything | That freedom doesn't pay rent |
| Success shift [F24] | "Transform one person deeply" | Find one person, run one skill | HIGH | Depth over breadth | Scale path |
| Beneficiary shift [F25] | "Is Ben using his own skills?" | Dogfooding, demonstration | HIGH | Eat-your-own-cooking test | External validation |
```

### Step 5: Evaluate and Recommend

```
REFRAME EVALUATION:

[F26] MOST PRODUCTIVE REFRAME: The combination of [F14] + [F25].

  Reframed problem: "I have 592 prototypes and zero test results.
  The next step is not finding users — it's finding out if this works.
  And the fastest test subject is me."

  This frame is best because:
  - It redefines the 592 skills as prototypes (raw material), not
    product — which removes the sunk-cost pressure to distribute them all
  - It makes the next action crystal clear: use the skills on Ben's own
    real problems, document the results, and let those results become
    the proof that attracts the first users
  - It turns the "no users" problem into a "no data" problem, which
    is solvable immediately without anyone else's cooperation
  - Ben IS using the skills (this session is proof) — he just needs
    to document and share the results

[F27] COMPLEMENTARY FRAMES:
  Use [F25] (dogfooding) for immediate action — Ben uses his own skills
  on his real problems and documents the before/after.
  Use [F22] (which 5 skills?) for product focus — identify the
  highest-value skills and make those the entry point.
  Use [F18] (this week) for urgency — post one demonstration, not
  an announcement, in a relevant community.

[F28] DANGEROUS FRAMES:
  - The ORIGINAL frame ("592 skills, no users") is the most dangerous.
    It implies the work is done and only marketing remains. This leads
    to premature distribution of an untested product, which will yield
    silence, which will feel like rejection, which will be demoralizing.
    The silence won't mean the skills are bad — it will mean no one
    knew what to do with 592 unfamiliar things. But it will FEEL like
    failure.
  - The LONG-TERM frame [F19] ("category creation") is seductive because
    it feeds the vision, but it's premature. You can't create a category
    without existence proofs. Get the proofs first.

[F29] KEY INSIGHT FROM REFRAMING:
  The original frame assumed that the inventory (592 skills) is the asset
  and that users are the missing ingredient. By shifting frames, we can see
  that the real problem is: Ben has never tested whether the skills produce
  better outcomes, and therefore has no evidence to show anyone.

  The 592 skills are not the product — they're the laboratory. The product
  is demonstrated transformation: "Here is a real problem. Here is how this
  skill changed my thinking about it. Here is what I did differently."

  This opens up three specific next actions:
  1. DOGFOOD PUBLICLY: Ben is already using the skills (this prompt series
     is evidence). Package the best results as demonstrations, not
     documentation. "I used /reframe on my own stuckness, and here's
     what it revealed" is more compelling than "here are 592 skills."
  2. CURATE TO 5: Identify the 5 skills with the strongest demonstrated
     results. Make those the front door. 592 is the library; 5 is the
     product.
  3. ONE PERSON, ONE SKILL: Find one real person with a real problem that
     matches one of the 5 curated skills. Walk them through it. Document
     the result. That's the first user story, and user stories are what
     attract the second user.
```

## The Reframe, Stated Simply

**Before:** "I have 592 skills and no users" --> go find users.

**After:** "I have 592 prototypes and no test results" --> go run tests.

The first framing sends you outward to market an unproven thing. The second sends you inward to prove the thing works — and the proof IS the marketing.
