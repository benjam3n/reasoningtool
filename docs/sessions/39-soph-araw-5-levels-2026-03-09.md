## The Five Levels

---

### Level 1: Child (ages 8-12)

**What ARAW does, in one idea:**

When you have an idea, your brain wants to believe it's right. ARAW makes you play a game with two rounds.

**Round 1: Pretend it's right.** If your idea is true, what else has to be true? Follow the chain. "If dogs can talk, then they could tell us when they're sick, and then vets would need different tools, and then..." Keep going until you hit something you can actually check.

**Round 2: Pretend it's wrong.** If your idea is wrong, why? Not just "because" -- find a real reason. Then ask: if it's wrong, what's the *right* answer instead?

**The rule:** You have to try just as hard in both rounds. No cheating by making Round 2 easy.

**Why it works:** Your brain is like a lawyer -- it argues for whatever you already believe. ARAW makes your brain be the lawyer for *both sides*. When both sides have argued their best, you can actually see which one is stronger.

**What you get:** Sometimes your idea wins. Sometimes it loses. Sometimes you find out it's a different question than you thought. All three are good answers.

---

### Level 2: Teenager (ages 14-18)

**What ARAW does, and why you'd want it:**

ARAW stands for Assume Right / Assume Wrong. It's a structured way to stress-test any belief, decision, or argument before you commit to it.

**The problem it solves:** Confirmation bias. When you believe something, your brain automatically filters evidence to support it and dismisses evidence against it. You don't even notice you're doing it. ARAW forces you to do what your brain won't do naturally: build the strongest possible case *against* your own position.

**How it works:**

1. **Break it down.** Take your idea and split it into the individual claims it depends on. "I should drop AP Chemistry" actually contains hidden claims: the class is too hard, dropping it won't hurt college apps, my time is better spent elsewhere, and I can't improve my performance. Each of those might be true or false independently.

2. **Assume Right (AR).** For each claim, assume it's true and trace the consequences. What follows? What doors open? What doors *close*? (That last one matters -- ARAW tracks what you give up, not just what you gain.)

3. **Assume Wrong (AW).** For each claim, assume it's false and find out why it would be false. Not a vague "maybe not" -- a specific reason. Then: if it IS wrong, what's the actual answer?

4. **Keep going deeper.** Each answer you find generates new claims to test. You build a tree, not a list.

5. **Only stop at bedrock.** You stop a branch when you hit something you can actually test, observe, or prove logically. "It feels true" is not a stopping point.

**The key discipline:** AR and AW get equal effort. If your "Assume Wrong" section is shorter and weaker than your "Assume Right" section, you're not doing ARAW -- you're just confirming what you already think with extra steps.

**What changes:** You stop having opinions and start having *positions* -- beliefs you've tested from both directions and can defend because you already know where they're weakest.

### Level 3: College Student (undergraduate level)

**ARAW as an epistemic method:**

ARAW is a structured adversarial reasoning protocol. It operationalizes a core epistemological principle: a belief is only as strong as the strongest attack it has survived.

**Architecture:**

The procedure has three strictly separated phases:

**Phase 1 -- Exploration.** Claims are identified, typed (explicit, implicit, bundled, presupposed, meta), and ranked by Value of Information (VOI) -- how much knowing the truth of this claim would change your action. High-VOI claims are explored first. For each claim, you build a branching tree:

- **AR branch:** Trace implications forward. Each implication is classified as Necessary (must follow), Probable (likely follows), or Possible (could follow under stated conditions). Track foreclosures -- what becomes impossible if this claim is true.
- **AW branch:** Identify independent reasons the claim could be wrong. Each is classified as Fatal (kills the claim alone), Serious (significantly undermines), or Conditional (kills under specific circumstances). For each wrongness reason, derive alternatives -- not asserted from intuition, but logically pointed to by the specific failure mode.

Every node is numbered (F1, F2, F3...). Every branch continues until reaching bedrock: empirically testable, logically necessary, directly observable, or in tension with another finding. "Probably true" is not bedrock.

**Phase 2 -- Registry.** A complete compilation of every claim and finding. No new analysis. Claims receive verdicts: Validated (AR reaches bedrock, AW doesn't reach fatal bedrock), Rejected (fatal AW reason reaches bedrock), Damaged (serious AW but not fatal at bedrock), Conditional (right under some conditions, wrong under others), or Uncertain (neither side reached bedrock). Crux points are identified: the specific questions that, if answered, would resolve the most uncertainty.

**Phase 3 -- Synthesis.** Conclusions derived exclusively from the registry. No new findings introduced. Identifies the overall pattern, key tensions between findings, weakest links in reasoning chains, and testable predictions.

**The methodological insight:** ARAW enforces what good reasoning requires but humans resist: symmetric rigor. The procedure's corruption safeguards are as important as its analytical steps. If a user praises the analysis mid-session, the protocol prescribes *increasing* adversarial pressure, because positive feedback creates a gradient toward agreement rather than truth. If more than 80% of claims are validated, the procedure flags this as likely confirmation bias and requires harder testing.

**Structural feature this level reveals:** ARAW is not just a thinking tool -- it's a debiasing architecture. The numbered findings, strict phase separation, and bedrock requirements exist specifically to prevent the analyst from drifting toward comfortable conclusions.

### Level 4: Expert (professional analyst / experienced reasoner)

**ARAW as formal adversarial epistemology:**

ARAW implements a specific theory of justified belief: **claims earn epistemic status through survival of structured attack, not through accumulation of supporting evidence.** This places it closer to Popperian falsificationism than Bayesian updating, though with important modifications.

**Key design decisions and their rationale:**

1. **Three-phase separation is an anti-corruption measure.** By prohibiting conclusions during exploration and prohibiting new findings during synthesis, ARAW prevents narrative-driven reasoning. Most analytical frameworks fail here: the analyst forms a conclusion mid-exploration and unconsciously steers remaining analysis to support it. ARAW's phase separation is a structural intervention against this.

2. **Bedrock types encode different epistemic groundings.** BEDROCK-TEST (empirically testable), BEDROCK-LOGIC (logically necessary), BEDROCK-OBSERVE (directly observable), and BEDROCK-TENSION (contradicts another finding) are not interchangeable. Each represents a different kind of stopping criterion. TEST and OBSERVE ground out in the world. LOGIC grounds out in formal structure. TENSION grounds out in internal coherence. A thorough ARAW analysis should reach multiple bedrock types, because over-reliance on one type creates systematic blind spots.

3. **Multi-valued AW is the critical innovation.** Naive adversarial reasoning treats "wrong" as binary: NOT-X. ARAW requires expanding the state space: alternatives Y and Z, hybrids, modifications, and reframes. Each alternative must be *derived from* a specific wrongness finding, not asserted independently. This prevents the common failure of adversarial reasoning where "assume wrong" collapses into "assume the opposite," which is still a binary frame.

4. **The foreclosure tracker is where AR does its real work.** Most frameworks track what AR implies (what becomes true). ARAW also tracks what AR forecloses (what becomes impossible). This is structurally important: every commitment has costs, and costs that are invisible at decision time become regrets later. The foreclosure tracker makes opportunity costs explicit.

5. **VOI-ordered exploration prevents completionism.** Not all claims deserve equal analysis. Value of Information ranking ensures that analytical resources concentrate on the claims that would most change action if resolved. This is a pragmatic concession: perfect analysis of everything is impossible, so the procedure optimizes for decision-relevance.

6. **The corruption pre-inoculation is not politeness -- it's structural.** ARAW explicitly warns that user praise degrades output quality. This isn't a social nicety; it addresses a measured failure mode of interactive reasoning. When an analyst receives positive feedback, subsequent analysis demonstrably shifts toward agreement. ARAW prescribes the opposite response: increase adversarial rigor after praise.

**Where ARAW succeeds:** Problems where confirmation bias is the primary threat. Decisions where the cost of being wrong is high and the commitment is hard to reverse. Situations where the obvious answer is so compelling that nobody has seriously tested it.

**Where ARAW is expensive:** Problems requiring speed. Situations where the answer is genuinely simple (ARAW's depth floors can force unnecessary exploration of straightforward claims). Domains where empirical bedrock is unreachable (pure value questions, aesthetic judgments) -- here, ARAW's bedrock requirement becomes a liability because the tree never terminates naturally.

**Structural feature this level reveals:** ARAW is not just a procedure but an *incentive architecture* -- it structures the analyst's attention to counteract known cognitive failure modes. Each design decision addresses a specific, documented bias or reasoning error.

### Level 5: Researcher (epistemology / cognitive science / decision theory)

**ARAW as a contribution to applied epistemology, and its open problems:**

ARAW occupies an interesting position in the landscape of formal reasoning methods. It is not a calculus (it doesn't compute probabilities), not a logic (it doesn't derive theorems), and not a heuristic (it's too structured). It is best understood as a **procedural epistemology** -- a fixed protocol that, when followed, produces a specific kind of epistemic object: a *tested claim with an explicit attack surface.*

**Theoretical placement:**

ARAW shares DNA with several traditions but is reducible to none:

- **Popperian falsificationism:** The emphasis on AW as the primary epistemic move, and the principle that claims earn status through surviving attack, is directly Popperian. However, ARAW departs from Popper by also requiring positive AR exploration -- it doesn't treat confirmation as worthless, but rather demands that it be balanced by equally rigorous disconfirmation.

- **Adversarial collaboration (Kahneman):** ARAW internalizes what adversarial collaboration externalizes. Instead of two people arguing opposite sides, a single analyst plays both roles under structural constraints. The corruption pre-inoculation is an explicit acknowledgment that this internalization is fragile.

- **Red teaming (military/security):** ARAW generalizes red teaming from "attack this plan" to "attack this belief." The multi-valued AW (not just "this fails" but "here's what's actually true instead, and here's why") goes beyond traditional red teaming.

- **Bayesian reasoning:** ARAW is conspicuously non-Bayesian. It doesn't assign or update probabilities. Instead, it traces implications to bedrock. This is a deliberate design choice: probability assignments in complex domains are often pseudo-precision, and ARAW replaces them with structural classifications (Necessary/Probable/Possible, Fatal/Serious/Conditional) that are arguably more honest about the actual state of knowledge.

**Open problems and research questions:**

1. **The bedrock problem.** ARAW requires every branch to terminate at bedrock (testable, logical, observable, or tension). In practice, many real-world reasoning chains enter domains where bedrock is unreachable within any practical time horizon. The procedure doesn't have a principled way to handle "provisionally grounded" claims -- beliefs that rest on well-established but technically non-bedrock foundations. This creates a tension: either you accept softer stopping points (violating the procedure) or you mark everything UNCERTAIN (making the procedure uninformative). A more sophisticated treatment would introduce a typology of grounding strengths rather than a binary bedrock/not-bedrock distinction.

2. **The symmetry assumption.** ARAW mandates equal rigor for AR and AW. But is this always epistemically optimal? In domains with strong prior evidence, asymmetric exploration (deeper AW for well-established claims, deeper AR for novel claims) might be more efficient. The symmetry requirement is a debiasing heuristic, not a theorem -- and like all heuristics, it has failure modes. Specifically, it may lead to false balance: the appearance that "both sides" of a well-settled question deserve equal weight.

3. **The single-analyst limitation.** ARAW's corruption pre-inoculation acknowledges that a single analyst playing both sides is vulnerable to self-deception. But the structural safeguards (phase separation, numbered findings, bedrock requirements) are designed to mitigate this. An empirical question remains: how effective are these structural safeguards compared to actual adversarial collaboration between multiple analysts? The cognitive science literature on "consider the opposite" interventions suggests significant but incomplete debiasing -- ARAW goes further, but quantifying "how much further" is an open measurement problem.

4. **The VOI circularity.** ARAW ranks claims by Value of Information before exploring them. But VOI assessment itself requires some understanding of the problem's structure -- understanding that the exploration is supposed to produce. This is a bootstrap problem: you need to know what matters before you've investigated, but investigation is how you learn what matters. ARAW handles this pragmatically (initial VOI estimates, revised during exploration), but a more formal treatment of sequential VOI updating within the procedure would strengthen it.

5. **Registry completeness vs. cognitive load.** The finding registry requires every claim and every finding to be tracked. At higher depth levels (8x+: 18 claims, 55+ findings), this becomes a significant cognitive management task. There is a tension between completeness (nothing lost) and comprehensibility (the analyst can actually use the registry to derive synthesis). Whether this is a practical limitation or a fundamental design tension is worth investigating.

6. **Verdict derivation formalism.** ARAW verdicts (Validated, Rejected, Damaged, Conditional, Uncertain) are "derived from the tree, not asserted." But the derivation rules are informal: "AR evidence reaches bedrock, AW reasons don't reach fatal bedrock" for Validated. A more formal treatment would specify aggregation rules for mixed evidence -- what happens when AR reaches BEDROCK-LOGIC but AW reaches BEDROCK-TEST? When a claim has three Serious AW reasons, at what point does accumulation of serious (non-fatal) concerns constitute effective rejection?

7. **Meta-ARAW stability.** ARAW can be applied to itself: "The claim that ARAW produces reliable epistemic assessments." A self-application would reveal whether the procedure's own assumptions survive its own testing method. Initial observation: ARAW's assumption that adversarial exploration is epistemically superior would face a Serious AW challenge from domains where cooperative/constructive reasoning outperforms adversarial reasoning (e.g., mathematical proof, where building up is more productive than tearing down). This suggests ARAW's domain of applicability is empirical and strategic claims, not formal or constructive domains -- a boundary condition the procedure itself doesn't explicitly state.

**Structural feature this level reveals:** ARAW is a *first-generation* procedural epistemology with identifiable boundary conditions, unresolved design tensions, and measurable open questions. Its contribution is not that it is optimal, but that it is *specified enough to be criticizable* -- which, by its own standards, is the precondition for improvement.

## Meta-Observation

The five levels above are not translations of each other. Each introduces a structural feature of ARAW that the previous level could not represent:

| Level | Structural Feature Introduced |
|-------|-------------------------------|
| Child | The core mechanism: forced symmetric exploration |
| Teenager | The debiasing function and the discipline of equal rigor |
| College Student | The formal architecture: phases, bedrock types, numbered findings |
| Expert | The incentive design: each structural choice counters a specific bias |
| Researcher | The boundary conditions: where ARAW's own assumptions are untested |

This progression -- behavior, purpose, formalism, design rationale, limits -- is the natural decomposition of any method. The child sees what it does. The researcher sees where it breaks.
