**Name the emotion:** Fear. Specifically, fear of irrelevance. Fear that 592 skills and months of work will meet the world and the world will shrug.

**What's the worst thing that happens if you distribute and it goes badly?**

Let's be precise:

1. You post on Reddit. It gets 0 upvotes and 0 comments. **Outcome:** You have exactly the same number of users as today (zero), but now you know something you didn't know before.

2. You post on Reddit. Someone comments "this is pointless, LLMs already do this." **Outcome:** You get data. Maybe they're right and you need to adjust. Maybe they're wrong and you sharpen your pitch. Either way: information you don't currently have.

**The fear is maximally disproportionate to the actual risk.** The worst case of distribution (being ignored) is identical to the current state (being unknown). You cannot lose what you don't have.

## The Honest Diagnosis

Here's what's actually happening, stated without cushioning:

**You are using skill-building as a coping mechanism for the fear of finding out whether your work matters.**

Building feels like progress. It has the texture of productivity. You get the dopamine of completion (skill #592! skill #593!). And critically, building can never reject you. A new skill always "works" in the sense that it exists when you're done. Distribution can fail. Distribution can reveal that the thing you've spent months on doesn't resonate. So you build another skill instead.

This is the displacement cycle:
```
Feel anxiety about whether the toolkit matters
    --> Build another skill (anxiety temporarily relieved)
        --> Toolkit grows but stays untested
            --> Anxiety returns, slightly worse
                --> Build another skill...
```

The way out:
```
Feel anxiety about whether the toolkit matters
    --> Send it to one person (anxiety spikes briefly)
        --> Get real feedback (anxiety replaced by information)
            --> Iterate based on reality, not imagination
```

**The competing commitment** (Step E2) deserves naming: *"I'm committed to distributing the toolkit, but I'm also committed to not finding out it doesn't matter."* As long as you only build, the toolkit is both potentially world-changing and safely untested. Distribution collapses the wavefunction. That's what you're avoiding.
