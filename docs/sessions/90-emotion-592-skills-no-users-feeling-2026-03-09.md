# /emotion What does it feel like to have built 592 skills and not know if anyone will use them?
**Date:** 2026-03-09
**Skill:** /emotion (Process a Feeling)

---

## Emotion Identification

This isn't cleanly one emotion. The question "what does it feel like" is itself an invitation to map the terrain rather than diagnose a disorder. But the terrain has identifiable features:

**Primary emotion: Uncertainty** -- not about the work's quality, but about whether it will find its people. You built 592 skills. That's not a weekend project. That's months of solo sustained effort by someone who kept going when nobody was watching. The uncertainty isn't "did I build something good?" -- it's "will the thing I built reach anyone?"

**Secondary emotion: A particular kind of exposure.** Shipping work into silence is different from shipping work into criticism. Criticism at least means contact. Silence means you don't know if you're shouting into a void or if the sound just hasn't arrived yet.

**What this is NOT (per context):** This is not frustration. Not burnout. Not self-doubt about capability. You built the thing. You built the website. You planned the subreddit. The execution is not in question.

---

## Implicit Request

The question isn't "make me feel better about this." The question is closer to: **"What is this experience, and what does it tell me?"**

This is an emotion about the future -- about an outcome that hasn't resolved yet. That makes it closest to **anxiety** in the skill's taxonomy, but it's a specific subspecies: the anxiety of having shipped something real into a world that hasn't responded yet.

The emotion carries information. The information is: **the outcome that matters most to you is still undetermined, and you can't force it to resolve.**

### Primary Weight Test

Removing A ("I don't know if anyone will use them" alone) loses the weight of what's been invested. Removing B ("I built 592 skills" alone) loses the stakes. Both clauses are load-bearing. This is a genuine tension, not setup-and-punchline.

```
[B5] TENSION_TYPE: Tradeoff (with elements of emotional resistance)
[B6] CLASSIFICATION_EVIDENCE: This is not a contradiction -- both are true simultaneously.
     It's not a risk (the "bad thing" isn't hypothetical -- the silence is current).
     It's not an exception or scope limit.
     It's a tradeoff between two true things: the work IS substantial AND
     the outcome IS unknown. The tension is that having done the work
     doesn't purchase certainty about reception. Effort and adoption are
     decoupled, and that decoupling is the source of the feeling.
```

### Hidden Objection Check

```
[B7] HIDDEN_OBJECTION_CHECK:
  ENERGY_MATCH: No -- the stated objection ("don't know if anyone will use them")
    is milder than the real weight here.
  POSSIBLE_HIDDEN: The deeper tension may be: "I don't know if this was the right
    use of my time while unemployed" or "I don't know if this work constitutes
    proof of my value to the world."
  EVIDENCE: An employed person building 592 skills as a hobby would frame this
    differently. The unemployment context means the work carries double duty --
    it's both the creative output AND the implicit argument for employability/worth.
    The silence from the world isn't just "nobody used my tool yet" -- it's
    "the thing I poured myself into hasn't yet validated the bet."

[B8] LIKELY_HIDDEN_OBJECTION: "I built something I believe in during a vulnerable time,
     and the world hasn't told me yet whether it was the right call."
[B9] SURFACE_QUESTION: "When you imagine someone using these skills and finding them
     genuinely useful -- what does that give you beyond the knowledge that the skills
     work?"
```

### Resolution

```
TENSION TYPE: Tradeoff (effort invested vs. outcome unknown)
EVIDENCE: Both sides are true. The work is real. The uncertainty is real.
          Neither invalidates the other.

HIDDEN OBJECTION: The silence carries more weight because of the unemployment context.
The work isn't just a project -- it's a bet placed during a period when the stakes
of "was this worth it" are higher than usual.

IF BOTH TRUE (this is a tension, not a contradiction):
NAVIGATE: The work's quality is independent of its adoption timeline.
These are two separate variables that feel fused but aren't:
  1. "Is this good work?" -- answerable by examining the work itself
  2. "Will people find it?" -- answerable only by time and distribution
The feeling comes from treating (2) as the answer to (1).
They are decoupled. The silence is information about distribution,
not about quality.
```

## /nsa Analysis: The Uncertainty Itself

```
UNCERTAINTY ANALYSIS
====================

ORIGINAL: "I don't know if anyone will use the 592 skills I built"

UNCERTAINTIES:

1. Will anyone discover this work?
   TYPE: Epistemic (partially) + Aleatory (partially)
   CONFIDENCE: 20% to 70%
   STAKES: Whether the work has impact beyond its creator
   REASONING: Epistemic because distribution strategy can be improved (subreddit,
     website, word of mouth, content marketing -- these are knowable actions).
     Aleatory because even with perfect distribution, adoption involves luck,
     timing, and fit that can't be fully controlled.

   WHAT WOULD MOVE CONFIDENCE:
     UPWARD: One person using a skill and reporting value. A post that gets traction.
       A community forming. An employer noticing the work. Any signal from outside.
     DOWNWARD: Extended silence after active distribution. Negative feedback
       on the concept. Discovery that the problem space is already saturated.

   DECISION_SENSITIVITY: Yes -- if you knew nobody would ever use them, you'd
     stop building and redirect effort. If you knew thousands would, you'd double down.
   COST_OF_BEING_WRONG (if you assume nobody will use them and stop):
     Medium-high -- you'd abandon real work based on absence of evidence
   COST_OF_BEING_WRONG (if you assume people will use them and keep going):
     Medium -- you'd invest more time in something that might not land
   COST_OF_DELAY (waiting to find out before acting): Low -- the work exists
     regardless; distribution actions are cheap
   REVERSIBILITY: High -- you can always resume or redirect

   RECOMMENDATION: Partial action -- continue distributing (cheap) while
     also pursuing parallel paths (employment, other projects). Don't treat
     silence as verdict. Don't treat hope as evidence.

2. Is the work good enough to be useful?
   TYPE: Epistemic -- answerable by testing
   CONFIDENCE: 55% to 85%
   STAKES: Whether the skills actually help people think better
   REASONING: You've used them yourself. You've iterated on them. You've
     built 592 of them, which means you've seen what works and what doesn't
     across hundreds of iterations. This is not zero-evidence territory.
     But self-assessment has known biases, and you haven't had external
     validation yet.

   DECISION_SENSITIVITY: Yes -- if the work isn't good, distribution won't help
   COST_OF_BEING_WRONG: Low -- fixable through iteration
   COST_OF_DELAY: Low
   REVERSIBILITY: High

   RECOMMENDATION: Investigate cheaply -- get the work in front of 5-10 people
     and observe what happens. Not surveys. Observation. Do they use the skills?
     Do they come back? Do they recommend them? This is cheap evidence that
     resolves the uncertainty far better than continued solo building.

3. Was this the right use of time while unemployed?
   TYPE: Model uncertainty -- this depends on what framework you use to evaluate
     "right use of time"
   CONFIDENCE: 30% to 60%
   STAKES: Whether to feel regret or confidence about the investment
   REASONING: By a "maximize immediate employability" model, maybe not optimal.
     By a "build something real that demonstrates capability" model, possibly
     excellent. By a "do the work you believe in" model, clearly yes.
     The uncertainty is about which model to apply, not about the facts.

   DECISION_SENSITIVITY: Partially -- affects emotional state more than next actions
   COST_OF_BEING_WRONG: Low -- the time is spent regardless; the question is
     how to frame it going forward
   COST_OF_DELAY: None

   RECOMMENDATION: This is model uncertainty. The answer depends on the frame.
     Rather than resolving it, notice that the "right use of time" question is
     unanswerable in the present tense. It resolves retroactively -- whether this
     was the right use of time depends on what happens next, which you still
     influence.

STOP INVESTIGATING WHEN: One clear signal from outside -- positive or negative --
  that breaks the silence. The problem isn't uncertainty about quality; it's
  absence of signal about reception.
```

## Synthesis

### The Emotion Acknowledged

The feeling of having built 592 skills and not knowing if anyone will use them is the feeling of **an unresolved bet**. You placed a large, sustained wager -- months of solo work during unemployment -- on the belief that structured thinking tools would be valuable. The work is done. The bet hasn't resolved. You're sitting in the gap between "shipped" and "received," and that gap has no natural end date.

This isn't despair. It isn't frustration. It's the specific discomfort of **having done your part and not being able to do the world's part for it.**

### The Implicit Need Identified

The need isn't reassurance ("your work is great!"). The need is **signal** -- any signal from outside the system. The uncertainty that hurts isn't "is this good?" (you've iterated 592 times; you have internal evidence). The uncertainty is "will this reach anyone?" -- and that's a distribution question, not a quality question.

### What the Analysis Found

1. **The "but" is a tradeoff, not a contradiction.** The work being substantial and the outcome being unknown are both true. They don't cancel each other out. The tension is that effort doesn't purchase certainty of reception, and that decoupling is uncomfortable.

2. **The hidden objection is about the unemployment context.** The silence weighs more because the work carries double duty -- creative output AND implicit proof of worth during a vulnerable period.

3. **The uncertainty is partially epistemic, partially aleatory.** You can improve the epistemic part (distribute, test, get feedback). You can't control the aleatory part (timing, luck, fit). The recommended posture is partial action: distribute what exists while pursuing parallel paths.

4. **The actual want is contact, not scale.** You don't need a thousand users. You need one. One person using one skill and it making a difference. That's the minimum viable signal that closes the loop.

5. **The crux is the shift from building to distributing.** 592 skills is enough. The next marginal unit of progress comes from getting skill #1 to its first user, not from building skill #593.

### Suggested Next Steps

- **Cheapest test (do this first):** Find one person -- a friend, an online stranger, anyone -- and walk them through one skill on a problem they actually have. Watch what happens. This costs almost nothing and resolves the biggest uncertainty.
- **Active distribution:** Post specific skills (not "here's my toolkit of 592 things") in communities where people are already discussing the problems those skills solve. Specific skill for specific problem in specific community.
- **Parallel framing:** The toolkit itself is a portfolio piece. 592 structured procedures built solo demonstrates systems thinking at a level most employers never see. This can serve the employment need simultaneously.
- **Stop condition:** You don't need to resolve all uncertainty before acting. You need one signal. One person. Then iterate from there.
