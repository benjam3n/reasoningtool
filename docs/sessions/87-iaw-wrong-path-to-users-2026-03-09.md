# /iaw What if I've been thinking about reasoningtool's path to users completely wrong?
**Date:** 2026-03-09
**Skill:** /iaw (I Am Wrong)

---

## Phase 1: Intent Extraction

```
[W1] ORIGINAL: "Build quality product → create content → distribute → get users.
     592 skills, website exists, no users. What if the entire approach to
     getting users is wrong?"

[W2] SURFACE_APPROACH: Build-first, content-market, distribute, attract users.
     Classic "if you build it, they will come" pipeline — product → content →
     distribution → users.

[W3] UNDERLYING_INTENT: Get real users who benefit from the reasoning toolkit
     and come back to use it.

[W4] INPUT_TYPE: strategy
```

### Separation Test

"If I could get real, returning users without building 592 skills, creating content, and distributing — would I be satisfied?"

**YES.** The approach (build → content → distribute) is a means, not the goal. The goal is users who benefit. Generate alternatives to the approach.

---

## Phase 2: Alternative Generation

```
[W5] ALTERNATIVE: Build WITH users, not FOR users (co-creation)
  LENS: Temporal shift — move users to the beginning, not the end
  PRESERVES: Quality product, eventual scale
  CHANGES: Users come BEFORE the product is "done" — they shape it.
           Instead of finishing then finding users, find 5 users then
           finish with their input.

[W6] ALTERNATIVE: Embed skills into where people already think (platform integration)
  LENS: Level shift — operate where users already are, not where you want them to be
  PRESERVES: The skill content and procedures
  CHANGES: Instead of bringing users to a website, bring skills to
           existing tools (Slack bots, ChatGPT GPTs, Raycast extensions,
           browser extensions that activate on Google Docs). Users never
           visit your site — they use your skills inside their workflow.

[W7] ALTERNATIVE: Solve one person's real problem publicly (case study first)
  LENS: Inversion — instead of product → content → users, do user → content → product
  PRESERVES: Demonstrating value
  CHANGES: Find someone with a real stuck problem. Use the toolkit to
           solve it. Document the process publicly. The documentation IS
           the content, the solved problem IS the proof, the person IS
           the first user. Repeat.

[W8] ALTERNATIVE: Give away the output, not the tool (results-first)
  LENS: Means substitution — same goal (users see value), different delivery
  PRESERVES: People experiencing better thinking
  CHANGES: Instead of "here's a toolkit, go think better," post the
           RESULTS of skills applied to interesting problems. "Here's a
           root cause analysis of why [trending topic] failed." People
           come for the analysis, discover the tool that produced it.

[W9] ALTERNATIVE: Eliminate the need for "users" entirely (consulting/service model)
  LENS: Elimination — remove the user-acquisition problem
  PRESERVES: Revenue, impact, using the skills
  CHANGES: Don't build a product with users. Be a consultant who uses
           these skills to deliver structured thinking as a service.
           Clients pay for your analysis. The toolkit is your
           competitive advantage, not your product.

[W10] ALTERNATIVE: One killer skill, not 592 (decomposition/focus)
  LENS: Decomposition — achieve the intent with one piece, not the whole
  PRESERVES: The toolkit concept
  CHANGES: Pick the ONE skill that's most demonstrably useful (probably
           /decide or /rca). Build the entire user experience around just
           that. "Paste your decision, get a structured analysis." No
           discovery problem. No overwhelm. One thing, done perfectly.
           592 skills become the long-tail expansion, not the pitch.

[W11] ALTERNATIVE: Let other people teach it (educator/creator partnership)
  LENS: Stakeholder shift — someone else brings the users
  PRESERVES: The skill content
  CHANGES: Instead of you acquiring users, partner with people who
           already have audiences (YouTubers who teach productivity,
           newsletter writers, course creators). They use your skills
           in their content. Their audience becomes your users. You
           provide the engine; they provide the distribution.
```

## Phase 3: Alternative Assessment

```
[W12] ASSESSMENT: W5 — Co-creation with users
  INTENT_PRESERVED: fully
  FEASIBILITY: medium — requires finding 5 willing early users
  CONSEQUENCES: Product evolves based on real needs, not assumptions.
                Slower initial build, but validated. Risk of building for
                a non-representative sample.
  FORECLOSES: The satisfaction of shipping a "complete" product. Speed of
              solo development.
  ADVANTAGE: Every feature is validated. Zero wasted work. Users are
             invested because they shaped it.
  DISADVANTAGE: Finding those first 5 willing people is itself a
                user-acquisition problem (smaller, but real). Slower.

[W13] ASSESSMENT: W6 — Platform integration (bring skills to users)
  INTENT_PRESERVED: mostly — users get value, but may never know it's
                    "reasoningtool"
  FEASIBILITY: medium — requires building integrations for each platform
  CONSEQUENCES: Users get value without changing behavior. Brand
                attribution is weak. Maintenance burden across platforms.
  FORECLOSES: Building a destination. Brand recognition. Direct
              relationship with users.
  ADVANTAGE: Meets users where they are. Zero behavior change required.
             Distribution is built into the platform.
  DISADVANTAGE: You become invisible infrastructure. Hard to monetize.
                Platform dependency.

[W14] ASSESSMENT: W7 — Case study first (solve real problems publicly)
  INTENT_PRESERVED: fully
  FEASIBILITY: high — you can start today with no new infrastructure
  CONSEQUENCES: Each case study is both content and proof. Slow to
                scale but high-trust. Requires finding willing subjects.
  FORECLOSES: Speed. Can't do this at scale.
  ADVANTAGE: Proof before pitch. Every case study is a compelling story.
             SEO-friendly. Shareable.
  DISADVANTAGE: Labor-intensive. Doesn't scale. One case study doesn't
                prove the toolkit — need many.

[W15] ASSESSMENT: W8 — Results-first (give away output)
  INTENT_PRESERVED: mostly — users see value but may not adopt the tool
  FEASIBILITY: high — you can start today
  CONSEQUENCES: Builds audience around your analysis, not your tool.
                Risk of being seen as "an analyst" not "a toolkit."
  FORECLOSES: Users learning to use the skills themselves (initially).
  ADVANTAGE: People can immediately judge value. No adoption barrier.
             Content is inherently interesting. Shareable.
  DISADVANTAGE: Gap between "I like reading this" and "I'll use the
                tool myself." Content treadmill.

[W16] ASSESSMENT: W9 — Consulting/service model
  INTENT_PRESERVED: partially — impact yes, "users of a product" no
  FEASIBILITY: high — requires no product changes
  CONSEQUENCES: Revenue from day one. No scaling. Trading time for money.
                Toolkit stays private competitive advantage.
  FORECLOSES: Scale. Product-market fit learning. Community.
  ADVANTAGE: Immediate revenue. Validates that structured thinking has
             value. No user-acquisition problem.
  DISADVANTAGE: Doesn't build toward a product with users. You become
                a consultant, not a product builder.

[W17] ASSESSMENT: W10 — One killer skill
  INTENT_PRESERVED: fully
  FEASIBILITY: high — requires focus, not new capabilities
  CONSEQUENCES: Dramatically simpler pitch. Dramatically simpler UX.
                591 skills "wasted" (temporarily). Forces quality over
                quantity.
  FORECLOSES: Showcasing breadth. The "592 skills" story.
  ADVANTAGE: Solves the discovery problem completely. Solves the
             overwhelm problem completely. Possible to make one thing
             excellent. Easy to explain. Easy to share.
  DISADVANTAGE: Choosing wrong. Losing the "comprehensive toolkit"
                positioning. 591 skills sitting unused.

[W18] ASSESSMENT: W11 — Educator/creator partnerships
  INTENT_PRESERVED: fully
  FEASIBILITY: medium — requires finding and convincing partners
  CONSEQUENCES: Leverages existing audiences. Others explain your value
                proposition (often better than you can). Dependent on
                partners' enthusiasm.
  FORECLOSES: Full control of messaging. Speed (partnership takes time).
  ADVANTAGE: Borrowed audience. Third-party credibility. Educators are
             GOOD at explaining value — better than product pages.
  DISADVANTAGE: Finding partners who care. Loss of control. Revenue
                sharing. Their audience may not be your audience.
```

==============

ORIGINAL: "Build quality product → create content → distribute → get users"
          (592 skills, website exists, zero users)

INTENT: Get real users who benefit from structured thinking skills and
        return to use them.

ALTERNATIVES (ranked by fit):

     PRESERVES: The toolkit concept, quality, user acquisition goal
     CHANGES: Lead with ONE skill instead of 592. Build the entire
              experience around "paste your decision, get structured

     ADVANTAGE: Solves discovery, overwhelm, and explanation in one

     DISADVANTAGE: Choosing the wrong skill. Losing the "comprehensive"
                   positioning. 591 skills sit unused initially.

  2. RESULTS-FIRST
     PRESERVES: People experiencing better thinking, content creation
     CHANGES: Instead of teaching the tool, publish the OUTPUT of the
              tool applied to interesting problems. People come for the

     ADVANTAGE: Zero adoption barrier to see value. Content is
                inherently interesting and shareable. Proves the tool
                works before asking anyone to use it.
     DISADVANTAGE: Gap between reading and adopting. Content treadmill.

  3. CASE STUDY FIRST
     PRESERVES: Demonstrating real value, content creation
     CHANGES: Find someone with a real stuck problem. Solve it with the
              toolkit. Document publicly. The solved problem is the
              proof. The person is the first user. Repeat.
     ADVANTAGE: Proof before pitch. Every case study is content,
                evidence, and a testimonial simultaneously.
     DISADVANTAGE: Labor-intensive. Doesn't scale. Requires finding

  4. CO-CREATION (build WITH users)
     PRESERVES: Product quality, eventual scale
     CHANGES: Move users to the BEGINNING. Find 5 people. Build with
              them, not for them. Every feature is validated before it

     ADVANTAGE: Zero wasted work. Users are invested. Product-market
                fit is built in, not discovered after.
     DISADVANTAGE: Finding those first 5 is itself a mini acquisition
                   problem. Slower development.

  5. EDUCATOR PARTNERSHIPS
     PRESERVES: Skill content, user acquisition goal
     CHANGES: Partner with people who already have audiences.

              creators. They explain your tool to their people.
     ADVANTAGE: Borrowed audience. Third-party credibility. Educators

     DISADVANTAGE: Finding willing partners. Loss of control. Their

ORIGINAL RANK: 6th of 7. The original approach front-loads building and

at any stage.

REFRAME: The problem isn't "how do I distribute a 592-skill toolkit."
The problem is "how do I prove that ONE skill helps ONE person, then
repeat." You don't have a distribution problem. You have a unit-economics-
of-proof problem. One skill, one person, one solved problem, documented.
That's the atomic unit of growth. Everything else — the 592 skills, the
website, the content — is premature scaling of something that hasn't yet
proven its smallest unit works.
