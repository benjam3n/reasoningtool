# /stl Write the tagline for reasoningtool in 3 different styles: academic, casual, provocative
**Date:** 2026-03-09
**Skill:** /stl (Style)

---

## Step 1: Define the Story's Purpose and Message

**The ONE point:** reasoningtool makes AI thinking structured, reliable, and deep -- not generic.

**Target emotion:** Confidence that this tool changes how AI works for you.

**Desired action:** Install it. Try a skill. See the difference.

**Why a tagline matters here:** A tagline is a story compressed to its smallest possible unit. It must do the work of a hook, a conflict, and a resolution in a single breath.

**What would be lost without a story:** A feature list. "592 skills for Claude Code" is a fact. A tagline is a promise.

---

## Step 2: Story Source Selection

For taglines, the strongest source is **the user's own latent experience** -- the frustration they already have with AI but haven't named. Each style will tap that source differently:

- **Academic** draws on the gap between AI capability and AI methodology
- **Casual** draws on the "that was... fine, I guess" feeling of generic AI output
- **Provocative** draws on the uncomfortable truth that most AI use is understructured

---

## Step 3: Narrative Arc (Compressed to Tagline Scale)

Each tagline must contain:
- An implicit **conflict** (AI thinking is currently unstructured)
- An implicit **resolution** (reasoningtool fixes it)
- A **hook** that makes you stop scrolling

---

## Step 4: The Three Taglines

### Academic

> **"Structured analytical procedures for systematic AI-assisted reasoning."**

**Why it works in this register:** It signals rigor. The audience hearing this is someone who distrusts AI hype and wants methodology. Every word earns its place: "structured" (not ad hoc), "analytical procedures" (not vibes), "systematic" (not random), "AI-assisted" (the human is still in charge), "reasoning" (not just generation).

**Sensory beat:** Reading this should feel like opening a well-organized research paper -- clean, credible, precise.

---

### Casual

> **"592 thinking skills so your AI actually thinks."**

**Why it works in this register:** It lands the core value proposition in plain language. The word "actually" does all the heavy lifting -- it names the gap everyone has felt. Your AI generates. Your AI responds. But does it *think*? The number 592 is specific enough to be credible and large enough to be impressive without explanation.

**Sensory beat:** Reading this should feel like a friend leaning over and saying "no, seriously, try this one."

---

### Provocative

> **"You've been prompting. You could have been reasoning."**

**Why it works in this register:** It creates a before/after in a single sentence. It reframes everything the reader has done with AI as the lesser version. "Prompting" becomes the past tense, the thing you used to do. "Reasoning" becomes the upgrade you didn't know existed. The implicit challenge: are you going to keep doing it the old way?

**Sensory beat:** Reading this should feel like a slight sting followed by curiosity. The sting is the point.

---

## Step 5: Hook and Landing Analysis

| Style | Hook mechanism | Landing |
|---|---|---|
| Academic | Credibility through precision | "reasoning" as the final word anchors the purpose |
| Casual | The word "actually" creates a gap | "thinks" lands the promise |
| Provocative | Second-person address ("You've been...") | "reasoning" reframes the entire category |

All three taglines end on the concept of **reasoning** or **thinking** -- the core identity of the product. This is intentional. The last word is the one that sticks.

## Step 7: Refinement Notes

**What to test:** Put all three in front of different audiences and watch which one makes people click. The academic version will perform best on Hacker News. The casual version will perform best on Twitter/X. The provocative version will perform best in direct outreach and landing pages.

**What to cut if needed:**
- Academic can drop "AI-assisted" if context makes it obvious
- Casual can drop "592" if the number changes frequently
- Provocative works as-is; every word is load-bearing
