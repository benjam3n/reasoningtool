## SECTION B: Familiar-Seeming Situation

### B.1 — Name the pattern you are matching to.

"This is the sunk-cost-identity-fusion pattern. A person invests so much time and self into a project that the project becomes their identity. They continue not because the project is working, but because stopping would mean confronting who they are without it. I have seen this in myself before with past projects, and I have seen it in every founder who says 'I just need six more months.'"

### B.2 — Force three differences between this situation and the pattern.

1. **The project is genuinely large and functional.** 592 skills actually exist and work. This is not vaporware or a half-built prototype. Past identity-fusion projects I have seen were often more aspiration than substance. This one has real, tested output.

2. **I am unemployed, not choosing this over a job.** In the classic pattern, the person has alternatives they are ignoring. My situation is different — I do not currently have a competing job offer I am refusing. The opportunity cost calculus is different when the alternative is not "a good job" but "job searching."

3. **I am asking the question.** In the pattern I am matching to, the person does NOT ask whether identity is affecting their decisions. They defend. The fact that I am running this procedure on myself is structurally different from the pattern where someone else has to point it out.

**Binary check:** Is any of these differences structural?

Yes — all three are structural. Difference #1 changes whether the project has real value independent of my identity. Difference #2 changes the opportunity cost analysis. Difference #3 changes whether the pattern-match is even applicable (self-aware identity fusion behaves differently from blind identity fusion).

**Proceed to B.3 with heightened caution.**

### B.3 — What would I see if this were NOT the pattern I think it is?

If this is NOT identity-fusion-driving-decisions, I would expect to see:

1. **I would be willing to radically change the project's form if evidence demanded it.** If I could genuinely say "I would convert reasoningtool into a 10-skill product tomorrow if user testing showed that was better," and mean it, then the project is not fused to my identity — I am holding it loosely.

2. **I would have a clear answer to "what would make me stop."** Someone whose identity is fused to a project cannot articulate stopping conditions. If I can name a concrete, falsifiable condition under which I would walk away, the fusion is not total.

3. **I would be spending time on distribution and validation, not just building.** Identity-fused builders build because building feels like progress and avoids the vulnerable act of showing work to strangers. If I am actively seeking users, feedback, and rejection, identity is not driving the bus.

**Binary check:** Can I check for each indicator right now?

- **Indicator 1 (willingness to radically change form):** Honestly? The 592-skill count appears in my thinking regularly. The website showcases breadth. If someone said "cut it to 15 skills," I would feel a sharp resistance — not because 15 is wrong, but because 592 represents months of work and a kind of completeness I value. **This indicator is partially present.** I am not fully willing to radically change form. The resistance is there.

I have continued through zero users, zero traction, and zero external validation. **This indicator is absent.** I cannot articulate my stopping conditions, which is a hallmark of the fusion pattern.

- **Indicator 3 (time on distribution vs. building):** The vast majority of my time has gone into building skills, improving the website, and refining the system. Today alone I have run 85+ analytical prompts — all of which are building and refining, not distributing or validating with real users. **This indicator is absent.** Building dominates.

**Result: Two of three indicators suggest the pattern match is at least partially correct.** The identity-fusion pattern is operating here, even if the differences I noted in B.2 mean it is not the full story.

5. If someone offered me a job tomorrow, would I stop or continue the project? (Tests commitment type.)
6. Is the resistance to cutting skills about quality judgment or about protecting the investment? (Tests motivation.)

### A.3 — Three competing descriptions of the situation.

**Description 1: Identity protection is the primary driver.**
"I have fused my identity with being a builder of a comprehensive reasoning toolkit. Continuing to build protects me from the vulnerability of job searching, the pain of admitting zero traction, and the emptiness of not having a defining project. The project is primarily a psychological shelter."

**Description 2: The project is genuinely valuable but I lack distribution skills.**
"I have built something real and substantive, and identity is a normal byproduct of deep investment in good work. The problem is not that identity is driving decisions — it is that I am a builder, not a marketer, and I am doing what I know how to do. The identity question is a distraction from the real problem: I do not know how to get users."

**Description 3: The project is a productive form of unemployment.**
"Being unemployed without a project would be psychologically destructive. This project keeps me skilled, structured, and purposeful. Identity attachment is real but functional — it is the thing preventing a worse outcome (depression, stagnation). The question is not 'is identity involved?' but 'is there a better use of this time that I am avoiding?'"

**Binary check:** Would each description lead to a different action?

- Description 1 leads to: Detach identity from the project. Set hard stopping conditions. Start job searching immediately.
- Description 2 leads to: Keep building but shift 50%+ of time to distribution, marketing, user research.
- Description 3 leads to: Keep the project but honestly evaluate whether job searching in parallel would be better.

Yes — three different actions. These are meaningfully distinct.

### A.4 — Which description do I WANT to be true?

I want Description 2 to be true. It is the one where the project is valuable, I am not broken, and the fix is a learnable skill (distribution) rather than a painful identity reckoning.

**Binary check:** Is this also the most comfortable description? Yes. It lets me keep doing what I am doing with a minor adjustment. It does not require confronting whether the project is primarily serving a psychological need.

**Flagged as bias risk. Do not weight Description 2 higher.**

### A.5 — Select the description I can most cheaply test.

**The cheapest test distinguishes Description 1 from Descriptions 2 and 3:**

Ask myself right now: "If I learned tomorrow that AI companies are building this exact capability into their products natively — making reasoningtool redundant — how would I feel?"

- If Description 1 is true (identity protection), I would feel devastated, defensive, and would look for reasons why their version is inferior. The loss would feel personal.
- If Description 2 is true (valuable but poorly distributed), I would feel disappointed but would pivot — maybe join one of those companies, or find the niche they are not covering.
- If Description 3 is true (productive unemployment), I would feel relieved that the market validated the concept and start looking for roles in that space.

### A.6 — Run the test.

Sitting with the hypothetical honestly: If Anthropic or OpenAI shipped built-in structured reasoning procedures tomorrow...

The honest answer is that I would feel a mix. There would be defensiveness ("mine are more comprehensive"), loss ("all that work"), but also — if I am honest — some relief. Relief suggests Description 3 has weight. Defensiveness suggests Description 1 has weight. The mix itself is informative: identity IS involved, but it is not the whole story.

**No single description clearly wins. Refining:**

The most accurate description combines elements: **I have built something real, AND identity has fused with it in ways that are distorting my allocation of time, AND the project is serving a psychological function during unemployment that makes it harder to evaluate objectively.**

## Validation Checkpoint

**V.1 — Classification in one sentence:**
"I am in a situation where genuine project value, identity protection, and unemployment psychology have merged, and the identity component is keeping me in a building loop that avoids the harder, more vulnerable work of distribution and honest evaluation."

**V.2 — What action does this classification imply?**
Three things, in order:
1. Write down concrete stopping/pivot conditions with dates. ("If I do not have 50 active users by [date], I will [specific action].")
2. Shift at least 50% of daily time from building to distribution, user contact, and validation — starting tomorrow, not "soon."
3. Separate job searching from the project. Run them in parallel. The project should not be a reason to avoid the job market, and a job should not be a reason to abandon the project.

**V.3 — If I took that action and the situation got worse, what would that tell me?**
If I set stopping conditions and immediately feel panicked or start finding reasons why the conditions are wrong, that tells me identity fusion is deeper than I think, and I should talk to someone outside the project about it — a friend, a mentor, a therapist. The panic would be the identity protecting itself.

If I shift to distribution and it feels unbearable (not just hard, but existentially threatening), that tells me the project IS primarily a shelter, and I need to address the underlying vulnerability before the project can be evaluated honestly.

**V.4 — Reclassification trigger:**
"I will reclassify if, after two weeks of genuinely spending 50% of my time on distribution and user contact, I find that (a) I cannot sustain it and keep reverting to building, or (b) I get user feedback that fundamentally changes what the project should be. In case (a), identity protection is the dominant factor and I need outside help. In case (b), the project has real value and I was just in the wrong mode."

**V.5 — Next steps:**
The appropriate procedure now is to make the three actions from V.2 concrete and scheduled. Consider running `/ata` on the distribution shift, and `/sdc` again in two weeks to check whether the pattern has changed.

## The Direct Answer

**What identity am I protecting?**
"I am a person who builds comprehensive, rigorous systems. I think deeply. I create things that are thorough and complete. I do not do things halfway."

That identity is real and earned. But it is also why 592 skills exist instead of 15 well-tested ones with 100 users. Thoroughness and completeness serve the identity. Distribution and user feedback threaten it, because users might say "I only need 5 of these" — and that would make the other 587 feel like they were for me, not for them.

**Is it affecting my decisions?**
Yes. Specifically:
- It biases toward building over distributing (building confirms the identity; distributing risks disconfirming it).
- It biases toward breadth over depth (592 skills signals thoroughness; 15 polished skills does not).
- It biases against setting stopping conditions (stopping would end the identity, not just the project).
- It makes the project feel more important than it may be to anyone other than me — which is the hardest sentence in this document to write.

None of this means the project is worthless. It means the project cannot be evaluated clearly until the identity layer is acknowledged and accounted for.
