# Recursive Causal Interrogation: Commitment

## The Binding

What is commitment?

---

Is commitment a decision, a feeling, a disposition, or something that precedes all three?

Does commitment require a single decisive moment, or can one become committed gradually without noticing?

If commitment can arrive without a moment of choice, is it still commitment or merely habit?

Can habit become commitment, or does commitment require something that habit lacks?

What does commitment possess that habit does not — is it awareness, intention, or something else entirely?

If commitment requires intention, must the intention be sustained continuously, or is a single original intention sufficient?

Can a commitment persist after the original intention has been forgotten?

If one has forgotten why one committed, is the commitment diminished or transformed?

Does a commitment that outlasts its reasons become stronger or weaker than one whose reasons remain vivid?

Can commitment be stronger than the reasons that support it?

---

Is commitment always commitment to something, or can one simply be a committed person without a specific object?

If commitment requires an object, what kinds of things can serve as objects of commitment — persons, principles, projects, or something more abstract?

Can one be committed to a contradiction — to two things that cannot both be honored?

If contradictory commitments are possible, does this reveal something about the nature of commitment that logic cannot capture?

Does commitment operate in a space prior to logic, or is genuine commitment necessarily coherent?

If commitment must be coherent, who determines whether a set of commitments is consistent?

Can one discover that one's commitments are inconsistent only after living them?

Is the discovery of inconsistency a failure of commitment or a deepening of understanding?

Does understanding one's commitments require testing them against reality, or can one know one's commitments through reflection alone?

---

What is the relationship between commitment and attachment?

Can one be committed without being attached, or does commitment necessarily involve attachment?

If commitment involves attachment, does it also involve the fear of loss?

Is a commitment that fears no loss a deeper commitment or a shallower one?

Can commitment be entirely free of anxiety, or is anxiety part of what makes commitment genuine?

Does genuine commitment embrace the possibility that it might fail?

If commitment cannot fail, is it commitment or compulsion?

What distinguishes commitment from compulsion when both produce the same observable behavior?

Is the difference between commitment and compulsion purely internal, or does it show up in how one relates to the committed action?

Can an observer distinguish commitment from compulsion, or is this distinction available only to the one who acts?

## What Commitment Is

Is commitment a relationship between a self and a future action, or between a self and a value?

If commitment is to a value, can the value change while the commitment remains?

If the value changes, has the commitment changed too, or does commitment have its own continuity independent of its object?

Can commitment be more stable than the thing one is committed to?

Does commitment create stability, or does it require stability as a precondition?

If commitment creates stability, what was there before commitment — chaos, openness, or mere possibility?

Is mere possibility threatening or liberating to the uncommitted person?

Does the uncommitted person experience freedom or vertigo?

Is commitment a response to vertigo — a way of making the world stable enough to inhabit?

If commitment is a response to vertigo, is it a cure or merely a coping mechanism?

---

Does commitment involve a promise, or can one be committed without having promised?

If commitment does not require a promise, how does it bind?

Can something bind without language, without declaration, without witness?

Does a commitment that no one else knows about have the same force as one that has been publicly declared?

If public declaration strengthens commitment, does this mean commitment is partly social?

Can a purely private commitment be as binding as a social one?

What makes a commitment binding — is it the will of the one who commits, the expectation of others, or something in the structure of the commitment itself?

If commitment derives its force from will alone, what happens when the will weakens?

Is a commitment that requires constant willpower more admirable or more fragile than one that has become effortless?

Does effortless commitment indicate depth or mere automation?

---

Is commitment a form of self-constraint, or does genuine commitment feel like freedom?

If commitment feels like freedom, is this an illusion that obscures its constraining nature, or a genuine experience of liberation through focus?

Can constraint be liberating, or is liberating constraint an oxymoron?

Does the artist who commits to a medium experience the medium's constraints as limits or as the very condition of creative expression?

If constraints enable expression, does commitment enable selfhood?

Is the uncommitted self a possibility or a void?

Can one have a self without commitments, or are commitments constitutive of selfhood?

If commitments constitute the self, what makes the initial commitment — is there a pre-committed self that chooses?

Does the question of what precedes commitment lead to an infinite regress?

Does the act of promising change the moral landscape, or does it merely reveal obligations that already existed?

Does the authority of commitment come from within the committed person, from the relationship, or from some transcendent moral order?

Does the value of commitment depend on its difficulty?

---

Is the distinction between compliance and commitment only visible from within?

If compliance can become commitment, does the origin of a commitment matter for its authenticity?
