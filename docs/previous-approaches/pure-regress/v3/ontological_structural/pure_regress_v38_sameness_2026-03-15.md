# Recursive Causal Interrogation: Sameness

## The Identical

What does it mean for something to be the same?

Is sameness a relation a thing bears to itself, or a relation between two things?

If sameness is a relation a thing bears to itself, is it the most trivial of all relations or the most mysterious?

How can a relation hold between a thing and itself, when relations seem to require two distinct relata?

Does the very concept of self-relation already smuggle in a doubling — a thing considered twice?

If we must consider a thing twice to declare it self-identical, have we not already introduced difference into the heart of sameness?

Can sameness be thought without at least the shadow of difference?

Is the thought of pure sameness — sameness without any contrast — a thought at all, or the absence of thought?

What would it be like to think sameness and nothing else?

Would a mind that grasped only sameness be a mind, or would it have collapsed into the very thing it contemplated?

---

Does every judgment of sameness require a prior act of distinction?

Must we first separate before we can declare identical?

If so, does sameness always arrive too late — after the damage of distinction has already been done?

Is the declaration "A is the same as A" informative, or is it empty?

If it is empty, why does logic place it at the foundation?

If it is informative, what new information does it carry?

Can tautology teach?

Does the law of identity — that everything is identical with itself — state a fact about the world, or lay down a rule for thought?

If it is a rule for thought, could there be minds that operate without it?

What would reasoning look like for a being that did not assume self-identity?

Could such a being navigate the world at all?

---

Is sameness a property, a relation, a structure, or something else entirely?

If sameness is a property, what does a thing possess when it possesses sameness?

Does it possess itself?

Can a thing possess itself without being both possessor and possessed, and therefore doubled?

If sameness is a relation, is it a relation of zero distance — a relation that collapses the space between relata?

Is a relation of zero distance still a relation?

Does the concept of a relation require at least the possibility of distance between its terms?

If sameness eliminates all distance, does it eliminate the very conditions for a relation to hold?

## The Ship of Theseus

Can two things each be identical with a third without being identical with each other?

Does the transitivity of identity forbid this, or does the ship of Theseus show that transitivity fails?

If transitivity fails for identity, is it really identity we are talking about?

Is the concept that survives the failure of transitivity still worthy of the name "sameness"?

If continuity grounds persistence, what kind of continuity — spatial, temporal, causal?

Does a flame that burns continuously through the night maintain its identity despite replacing all its matter moment by moment?

Is the flame a better model for identity than the ship?
