# Recursive Causal Interrogation: Difference

## What Differs

What is difference?

Is difference something that exists in itself, or only as a relation between things that are already given?

If difference is a relation, what are the relata — and must they be established before difference can appear?

If the relata must be established first, does that mean identity is prior to difference?

But how could identity be established without already distinguishing one thing from another — which is to say, without difference?

Does every assertion of identity already presuppose a background of difference against which identity becomes legible?

If identity requires difference to be recognizable, and difference requires identity to have something to differ from, which of the two is more fundamental?

Could it be that neither is more fundamental, and that they co-arise?

What would it mean for identity and difference to co-arise rather than for one to ground the other?

Does co-arising itself presuppose a prior unity from which both emerge, or is co-arising a genuinely primitive notion?

If co-arising is primitive, what prevents us from asking what makes co-arising possible?

Can difference be defined, or does every definition of difference presuppose it?

If I say difference is the relation of non-identity between two terms, have I not used "non-identity" — which already means difference?

Is every attempt to define difference circular?

Does the circularity of defining difference indicate that difference is a transcendental condition — something presupposed by all thought whatsoever?

If difference is a transcendental condition, can it ever be an object of thought, or does it always remain the medium through which thought operates?

Can thought think its own medium without falsifying it?

When we make difference into an object of inquiry, do we domesticate something that is essentially wild?

What would it mean for difference to resist conceptualization even as we attempt to conceptualize it?

---

Is there a difference between difference and differentiation?

Is difference a state and differentiation a process, or are these the same phenomenon described in two registers?

If differentiation is the process by which difference comes about, what exists prior to differentiation?

Is the undifferentiated a coherent concept, or does naming it already introduce a differentiation — between the differentiated and the undifferentiated?

Can the undifferentiated be experienced, or only inferred as a logical presupposition?

If the undifferentiated can be experienced, what is the character of that experience — and how does the experiencer remain differentiated enough to have it?

If the undifferentiated can only be inferred, is it a real origin or merely a theoretical posit?

---

Does difference have a nature, or is it precisely what disrupts the notion of having a nature?

If everything that exists has a nature, and difference exists, then difference must have a nature — but is a "nature of difference" not a contradiction, since difference is what unsettles fixed natures?

Can something exist without having a nature?

Is "existence without nature" what Deleuze means by difference-in-itself?

What did Deleuze mean by insisting that difference must be thought in itself, not through the lens of identity?

Is the history of philosophy, as Deleuze claimed, a history of the subordination of difference to identity?

What would it mean to liberate difference from identity?

## The Echo

Is the question of difference the most fundamental philosophical question?

Does every other philosophical question presuppose the question of difference?

Can the question "What is being?" be asked without already distinguishing being from non-being?

Can the question "What is truth?" be asked without already distinguishing true from false?

Can the question "What is the good?" be asked without already distinguishing good from evil?

Is the act of questioning itself a differentiation — separating what is known from what is not yet known?

Does every question open a difference — a gap between the asker's current state and the answer sought?

Is the gap between question and answer the space in which thinking occurs?

Is thinking the traversal of difference — the movement from not-knowing to knowing?

But does the traversal of difference close the gap, or does it open new ones?

Does every answer generate new questions — new differences between what is now known and what remains unknown?

Is the production of new questions the production of new differences?

Is philosophy the infinite production of difference through questioning?
