# Recursive Causal Interrogation: Similarity

## Similarity

What does it mean for one thing to be similar to another?

Is similarity a relation that holds between things, or a judgment made by minds about things?

If similarity is a relation between things, does it exist independently of anyone noticing it?

Can two things be similar without any observer to recognize their similarity?

If similarity requires an observer, does the observer create the similarity or merely detect it?

What faculty of the mind is responsible for perceiving similarity — and is that faculty itself similar to anything else?

Does recognizing similarity presuppose already knowing what similarity is?

If we must understand similarity before we can recognize instances of it, how did we ever come to understand similarity in the first place?

Is the understanding of similarity itself acquired through noticing similarities?

Does this circle — needing similarity to learn similarity — vitiate the concept, or reveal something fundamental about how concepts work?

Can the circle be broken by grounding similarity in something more basic, or is similarity itself the most basic thing?

If similarity is the most basic thing, does it precede being — or can there be similarity only where there are already beings?

Can something resemble another thing without being similar to it?

Can something be similar to another thing without resembling it?

Does perceptual resemblance ground conceptual similarity, or does conceptual similarity organize perceptual resemblance?

Can we perceive resemblance without concepts, or do concepts shape what we see as resembling?

If a newborn sees two faces as alike, is that similarity or resemblance or neither?

Is pre-conceptual resemblance a myth invented by empiricists?

Does the distinction between resemblance and similarity dissolve under pressure, or does it sharpen?

If the distinction dissolves, does that mean we have been using two words for one thing?

If two words for one thing, is that itself a case of similarity between words?

What makes two things similar rather than different?

Is similarity defined by shared properties, or is it something over and above shared properties?

If similarity is just the sharing of properties, is every pair of things similar in some respect?

Are any two things in the universe entirely without shared properties?

If every pair of things shares at least one property — such as the property of being a thing — is universal similarity trivial?

Does trivial similarity undermine the concept, or does it show that similarity admits of degrees?

If similarity admits of degrees, what determines the degree of similarity between two things?

Is the degree of similarity proportional to the number of shared properties?

Should some shared properties count more than others in determining degree of similarity?

What determines which properties are more important for similarity — the things themselves, or the purposes of the one comparing?

If the importance of properties varies with purpose, is similarity always relative to a context?

Can there be absolute or context-independent similarity?

Would absolute similarity be identity in disguise?

If absolute similarity is identity in disguise, is all talk of similarity really talk of partial identity?

Is partial identity coherent — can a thing be partially the same as another?

If partial identity is coherent, does it require that things have parts — and is the partial identity a sharing of parts?

Is property-sharing a form of part-sharing — and if so, are properties parts of things?

If properties are parts, is the similarity between two red things a matter of both having redness as a part?

Is redness detachable from the things that have it, or is it inseparable from them?

If inseparable, how can two things share what cannot be detached?

Is the paradox of shared inseparable properties the deepest paradox of similarity?

Is similarity a matter of sharing properties, or a matter of lacking differences?

Are these the same thing stated differently, or genuinely distinct perspectives?

If similarity is the absence of difference, does difference come first and similarity derive from it?

If similarity is the presence of shared features, does similarity come first and difference derive from its absence?

Which is more fundamental — what two things have in common, or what sets them apart?

Can we specify what two things share without implicitly specifying what they do not share?

Does every statement of similarity carry an unstated complement of dissimilarity?

Is similarity always partial, or can it be total?

If similarity is total, does it become identity?

Is identity the limiting case of similarity, or something categorically different from similarity?

Does the limiting case of a concept still belong to that concept, or does it mark the concept's boundary?

If identity is the boundary of similarity, is identity itself similar or dissimilar to similarity?

Where does similarity end and identity begin?

Is there a sharp boundary between being very similar and being the same thing?

If two things are similar in every possible respect, are they still two things?

Does the identity of indiscernibles apply to similarity — if there is nothing dissimilar about them, must they be identical?

Is the difference between similarity and identity a difference of degree or a difference of kind?

Is similarity one relation or many?

Does shared biology explain shared judgments of likeness, or does it merely relocate the problem?

If our brains are wired to detect likeness, what is it about the world that our brains are detecting?

Is there a structure in things that corresponds to our perception of likeness, or does our perception impose structure?

Does the question of whether likeness is found or imposed itself rest on a similarity — the similarity between mind and world?

Is representation a form of similarity, or does representation work through some other mechanism?

Does the direction of a similarity judgment depend on which thing is taken as the reference point?

Does the transitivity of similarity depend on whether the same property is shared across each link?

## The Problem of Respects

Does every similarity judgment require specification of a respect in which the things are similar?

If similarity always requires a respect, is unqualified similarity meaningless?

Can we ever say two things are just similar, full stop, without specifying how?

If overall similarity is a weighted sum of specific similarities, who assigns the weights?

Can the weights be determined objectively, or are they always relative to interests, purposes, contexts?

If the weights are interest-relative, is overall similarity interest-relative too?

Does interest-relative similarity undermine realism about natural kinds?

If natural kinds are supposed to be classes of genuinely similar things, but similarity is interest-relative, are natural kinds interest-relative?

Can nature itself determine which respects of similarity matter?

Is the notion that nature carves at its joints a metaphor, and if so, what is it a metaphor for?

Does nature have joints, or do we project joints onto nature?

If we project joints, is the projection guided by similarities we find or similarities we impose?
