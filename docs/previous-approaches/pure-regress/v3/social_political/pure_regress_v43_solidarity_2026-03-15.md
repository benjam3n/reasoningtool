# Recursive Causal Interrogation: Solidarity

## Solidarity

What binds people together who have no obligation to be bound?

---

Is solidarity a feeling, a commitment, a practice, or a structure?

If solidarity is a feeling, can it be willed into existence or must it arise spontaneously?

If it arises spontaneously, what triggers it — shared suffering, shared identity, shared threat?

If solidarity must be triggered, does it exist before the trigger or only after?

Can you be in solidarity with someone without knowing it?

Can solidarity be one-directional, or must it always be mutual?

If one person stands in solidarity with another who rejects that solidarity, does solidarity still exist?

Is unwanted solidarity a form of imposition?

Can solidarity be an act of aggression disguised as an act of care?

When does standing with someone become standing over them?

---

What is the difference between solidarity and sympathy?

Does sympathy look down while solidarity looks across?

Can you feel sympathy without solidarity, and if so, what is missing?

Can you feel solidarity without sympathy — standing with someone you do not particularly like or pity?

If solidarity does not require affection, what does it require?

Is solidarity possible between people who dislike each other?

If two enemies unite against a common threat, is that solidarity or mere strategic alliance?

What distinguishes genuine solidarity from a temporary coalition of convenience?

Does the distinction matter if the outcome is the same?

If solidarity is judged by outcomes rather than intentions, is performative solidarity indistinguishable from genuine solidarity?

---

What is the difference between solidarity and charity?

Does charity preserve the distance between giver and receiver while solidarity collapses it?

Can an act of charity ever become an act of solidarity, or are they structurally incompatible?

If a wealthy person donates to a workers' strike fund, is that solidarity or philanthropy?

Does the answer depend on whether the wealthy person also risks something?

Is risk essential to solidarity — must you have something to lose?

Can you be in solidarity with someone if your solidarity costs you nothing?

Is costless solidarity a contradiction in terms?

If solidarity requires sacrifice, how much sacrifice is enough?

Who gets to judge whether the sacrifice is sufficient?

Does the demand for sufficient sacrifice become a purity test that undermines solidarity itself?

---

Is solidarity a relationship between individuals or between groups?

Can a single person be in solidarity, or does solidarity require at least a collective on one side?

If solidarity is fundamentally collective, what happens to the individual within it?

Does solidarity demand the subordination of individual interests to collective ones?

If so, how does solidarity differ from conformity?

Can you maintain your individuality while participating in solidarity, or does real solidarity require self-effacement?

Is the person who insists on their individual perspective within a solidarity movement a traitor or a necessary corrective?

When does healthy dissent within solidarity become destructive fragmentation?

Who draws the line between loyal criticism and disloyalty?

Is there a paradox in demanding loyalty as a condition of solidarity — does coerced solidarity cease to be solidarity?

Can forced unity ever produce genuine solidarity, or does solidarity require voluntary commitment?

---

What is class solidarity?

Is class solidarity based on shared economic position, shared exploitation, or shared consciousness of exploitation?

Can people share an economic position without recognizing it?

If they do not recognize it, does class solidarity exist latently or not at all?

Is class consciousness a prerequisite for class solidarity, or can solidarity precede consciousness?

Can workers be in solidarity with each other while competing for the same jobs?

Does labor market competition structurally undermine the very solidarity that workers need?

If competition and solidarity coexist in tension, which is more fundamental to the worker's condition?

Is the history of labor a history of solidarity overcoming competition, or competition undermining solidarity?

Can class solidarity cross national borders, or does national identity always trump class identity?

If a worker in one country benefits from the exploitation of a worker in another, can they truly be in solidarity?

Is international class solidarity a real possibility or a utopian fantasy?

## Worker Solidarity and Its Contradictions

What does it mean for workers to stand together?

Is a picket line a physical manifestation of solidarity, or is it something more — a boundary between loyalty and betrayal?

When a worker crosses a picket line, are they breaking solidarity or exercising individual freedom?

Can individual freedom and collective solidarity be reconciled, or must one always yield to the other?

If a worker crosses a picket line because their family will starve otherwise, is the moral weight on the scab or on the system that created the choice?

Does judging the scab let the system off the hook?

Is the anger directed at strikebreakers a displacement of anger that should be directed at employers?

Or is the anger at strikebreakers precisely the mechanism by which solidarity is enforced?

Does solidarity require enforcement, and if so, does enforced solidarity undermine its own moral foundation?

Can solidarity be simultaneously voluntary and enforced — chosen freely but maintained through social pressure?

Is social pressure a form of coercion or a form of persuasion?

At what point does the community's expectation of solidarity become a form of tyranny?

---

Is the union the institutional form of solidarity, or does institutionalization transform solidarity into something else?

Can solidarity survive bureaucratization?

Does a union leadership that negotiates on behalf of workers embody solidarity or replace it?

When union leaders compromise, are they exercising solidarity wisely or betraying it?

Is the tension between rank-and-file militancy and leadership pragmatism a permanent feature of organized solidarity?

Can solidarity exist without organization, or is spontaneous solidarity always too fragile to endure?

If organization is necessary, does the form of organization determine the quality of solidarity?

Is a horizontal, leaderless movement more solidary than a hierarchical union?

Or does horizontality create the illusion of solidarity while actually enabling domination by the most vocal?

Does structurelessness breed its own tyrannies, as Jo Freeman argued?

If every form of organization distorts solidarity, is pure solidarity impossible in practice?

Is the impossibility of pure solidarity a reason to abandon the concept, or a reason to pursue it asymptotically?

---

What happens to solidarity when the struggle succeeds?

Does victory dissolve solidarity, or does it transform solidarity into something new?

If workers win their demands, does the solidarity that sustained the struggle survive into the new conditions?

Is post-victory solidarity possible, or is solidarity inherently tied to resistance?

Can solidarity exist in conditions of comfort, or does it require adversity?

If solidarity requires adversity, do communities unconsciously preserve or invent adversity to maintain their bonds?

Is the nostalgia for past struggles really nostalgia for the solidarity those struggles produced?

Do movements romanticize hardship because hardship was the price of connection?

Is there something perverse in valuing solidarity so highly that suffering becomes desirable as its precondition?

Can solidarity be built through joy, shared creation, and mutual flourishing rather than through shared suffering?

If joy-based solidarity is possible, why is it so much rarer than suffering-based solidarity?

Is suffering simply a more powerful bonding agent than happiness?

## Political Solidarity

What is political solidarity?

Is political solidarity different from personal solidarity, or is the personal always political?

If all solidarity is political, does the category of political solidarity lose its meaning?

Can you be in political solidarity with a cause you do not fully understand?

Is partial understanding sufficient for solidarity, or does genuine solidarity require deep comprehension of what you are supporting?

If deep comprehension is required, does that restrict solidarity to intellectuals and exclude those who feel solidarity intuitively?

Is intuitive solidarity less valid than theoretically grounded solidarity?

Can a person who cannot articulate why they stand with a movement be more solidary than a theorist who can write books about it?

Does solidarity live in the body or in the mind?

Is showing up more important than understanding why you showed up?

---

What does it mean to be in solidarity with a people you have never met?

Can you be in solidarity with Palestinians, Kurds, Uyghurs, or Rohingya from the comfort of a distant country?

Is distant solidarity real solidarity or a performance of moral identity?

Does the accusation of performative solidarity function to silence genuine solidarity, or does it name a real problem?

Can distant solidarity accomplish anything, or does it serve only the psychological needs of the person expressing it?

If distant solidarity raises awareness, and awareness leads to pressure, and pressure leads to change, is it still merely performative?

At what point does a chain of indirect causation become too attenuated to count as solidarity?

Is solidarity about effect or about stance — about what you accomplish or about where you stand?

If solidarity is about stance, is it fundamentally an ethical position rather than a political one?

Can an ethical stance that produces no political results still be called political solidarity?

---

Is solidarity with the oppressed a duty or a choice?

If it is a duty, who imposes it?

If it is a choice, what motivates it — moral conviction, self-interest, identity, guilt?

Can solidarity motivated by guilt be genuine?

Does guilt-based solidarity serve the guilty or the oppressed?

When privileged people express solidarity with the marginalized, who benefits more — the marginalized or the privileged person's self-image?

Is the criticism that solidarity serves the privileged always valid, or can it become a way of dismissing solidarity altogether?

If every expression of solidarity by the privileged is suspect, is cross-class solidarity impossible?

Does the suspicion of solidarity across difference become a self-fulfilling prophecy that prevents solidarity across difference?

How do you distinguish genuine ally solidarity from self-serving solidarity without reading minds?

Is the demand to prove the purity of one's solidary motives a demand that can never be satisfied?

---

Can solidarity be extended too far — can you try to be in solidarity with everyone?

If you are in solidarity with everyone, are you in solidarity with no one?

Does universal solidarity dilute the concept to meaninglessness?

Is there a necessary tension between solidarity with a particular group and solidarity with humanity as a whole?

If you are in solidarity with workers and with the unemployed, what happens when their interests conflict?

If you are in solidarity with women and with a particular cultural community, what happens when that community's practices oppress women?

Does genuine solidarity require choosing sides, and does choosing sides always mean betraying someone?

Is the anguish of choosing between competing solidarities the authentic experience of political life?

Can a framework of intersectionality resolve competing solidarities, or does it merely name the problem without solving it?

Does naming a problem without solving it have value, or is it merely intellectual comfort?

## Solidarity and Identity

Is solidarity always based on identity — on being the same kind of person?

If solidarity requires shared identity, what counts as a shared identity?

Is shared race a basis for solidarity, or does racial solidarity essentialize race?

Is shared gender a basis for solidarity, or does the diversity within any gender category undermine gender solidarity?

Can there be solidarity among women when the category "women" is contested?

Does the contestation of identity categories destroy the possibility of solidarity, or does it demand a more sophisticated solidarity?

What would solidarity without identity look like?

Is solidarity across difference more admirable than solidarity based on sameness, or is it merely more fragile?

Can solidarity based on shared values substitute for solidarity based on shared identity?

If values are chosen but identities are given, is value-based solidarity more authentic or less rooted?

Does rootedness matter for solidarity, or is solidarity precisely the act of creating roots where none existed?

---

Is identity-based solidarity inherently exclusionary?

If solidarity among Black people excludes non-Black people, is that exclusion justified or problematic?

Can exclusionary solidarity coexist with broader coalitions, or does every exclusion weaken the larger movement?

Is the demand that all solidarity be inclusive a demand made from a position of privilege?

Do those who have never needed exclusionary solidarity have the standing to criticize it?

Is "standing" itself a concept that either enables or restricts solidarity?

Does the question of who has standing to speak reproduce the very hierarchies that solidarity is supposed to overcome?

If everyone has standing to express solidarity, does standing become meaningless?

If only some people have standing, who decides who they are?

Is the gatekeeping of solidarity a necessary protection or an exercise of power disguised as principle?

Can gatekeeping be both — simultaneously protective and authoritarian?

---

What is the relationship between solidarity and nationalism?

Is national solidarity a genuine form of solidarity, or is it solidarity's corruption?

Can a nation be a solidarity community, or does nationalism always depend on defining an enemy to exclude?

If solidarity requires an outside — a them against which the we is defined — is all solidarity ultimately tribal?

Can there be solidarity without an adversary?

Is solidarity against something always stronger than solidarity for something?

If oppositional solidarity is stronger, does every solidarity movement need an enemy to survive?

Does the need for an enemy explain why solidarity movements so often turn inward and begin purging their own members?

Is the purge the shadow side of solidarity — exclusion as the dark twin of inclusion?

Does the history of revolutionary movements confirm that intense solidarity produces intense persecution of internal dissent?

Is there a way to build solidarity that does not eventually devour its own?

## Solidarity Across Difference

How do you build solidarity between people who have nothing obvious in common?

Is the premise of the question wrong — do all humans have something in common by virtue of being human?

If shared humanity is the basis for solidarity, why is solidarity so difficult to achieve?

Is shared humanity too abstract to ground solidarity — do people need more concrete commonalities?

What concrete commonalities are sufficient — shared neighborhood, shared employer, shared oppression, shared language?

Can solidarity cross a language barrier?

If solidarity is partly communicative, does the inability to communicate directly undermine it?

Can gestures, presence, and action communicate solidarity without words?

Is wordless solidarity more honest than spoken solidarity, or is it simply less precise?

Does precision matter in solidarity, or is the vagueness of fellow feeling its strength?

---

Can men be in solidarity with feminism?

If a man benefits from patriarchy, can he genuinely stand against it?

Is the expectation that beneficiaries of oppression dismantle that oppression reasonable or naive?

Can you saw off the branch you are sitting on?

If structural privilege makes genuine solidarity structurally impossible, is the entire project of cross-group solidarity doomed?

Or does the fact that people regularly act against their apparent interests prove that structural analysis is incomplete?

Is solidarity a refutation of rational self-interest, or can solidarity be explained as a form of enlightened self-interest?

If solidarity is enlightened self-interest, does that reduce it to selfishness?

Does the motivation matter if the action is the same?

If a man supports feminism because he believes patriarchy harms him too, is that solidarity or self-help?

Can the same act be both solidarity and self-interest without contradiction?

---

Can solidarity exist between the colonizer and the colonized?

If a citizen of an imperial power stands in solidarity with the people their nation oppresses, is that solidarity or is it a performance that assuages colonial guilt?

Can guilt be transformed into solidarity, or does guilt always contaminate what it produces?

Is the demand for guilt-free solidarity a demand for an impossibility in a world structured by historical injustice?

If everyone carries some historical complicity, is all solidarity necessarily tainted?

Does tainted solidarity have value, or should we hold out for pure solidarity?

Is pure solidarity available to anyone, or is it the fantasy of those who have not examined their own position?

If all solidarity is impure, does the distinction between genuine and performative solidarity collapse?

Or does the effort to act despite impurity constitute its own form of genuineness?

Is solidarity defined not by purity of motive but by willingness to act in the face of acknowledged complicity?

---

What does solidarity look like between the Global North and the Global South?

Is Northern solidarity with the South compatible with Northern consumption patterns?

Can you express solidarity with exploited garment workers while wearing the clothes they made under exploitation?

Does the demand for perfectly consistent solidarity make solidarity impossible for anyone embedded in global capitalism?

If consistency is impossible, is the only honest position hypocrisy — knowing you contradict yourself and acting anyway?

Is acknowledged hypocrisy morally superior to unacknowledged hypocrisy?

Is the person who acts in solidarity while admitting their complicity more honest than the person who avoids solidarity to avoid hypocrisy?

Does purity of position become an excuse for inaction?

Is the demand for purity a luxury of those who can afford to do nothing?

Can imperfect solidarity accomplish more than perfect inaction?

## Solidarity and Sacrifice

Does solidarity require sacrifice?

If solidarity costs nothing, is it solidarity or merely agreement?

Is there a threshold of sacrifice below which solidarity is not genuine?

If so, who sets the threshold — the person sacrificing or the person on whose behalf the sacrifice is made?

Can the demand for sacrifice become a form of exploitation within solidarity movements?

When a movement demands that its members give up comfort, safety, or livelihood, is that solidarity or coercion?

Is the willingness to sacrifice the proof of solidarity, or is it possible to prove solidarity without sacrifice?

Can you demonstrate solidarity through joy, celebration, and mutual care rather than through suffering?

If sacrifice is not required, what distinguishes solidarity from mere agreement or approval?

Is solidarity a disposition — a readiness to sacrifice if called upon — rather than the sacrifice itself?

Can a disposition be tested only by being activated, or can an untested disposition still count as genuine?

---

What kinds of sacrifice does solidarity demand?

Is financial sacrifice sufficient, or does real solidarity require bodily risk?

Is the person who donates money but never shows up at a protest less solidary than the person who shows up but donates nothing?

Can solidarity be measured, or does the attempt to measure it betray its spirit?

Does quantifying solidarity reduce it to an economy of exchange — I gave this much, therefore I am this solidary?

If solidarity cannot be measured, how do we distinguish genuine solidarity from lip service?

Is the impossibility of measuring solidarity the reason that accusations of insufficient solidarity are so potent?

Does the unmeasurability of solidarity make it vulnerable to manipulation by those who claim to be the arbiters of authenticity?

Is every solidarity movement vulnerable to the charismatic individual who claims to embody solidarity more purely than anyone else?

Does the cult of the solidarity hero undermine collective solidarity by centralizing it in a single figure?

Can solidarity survive the hero, or does the hero's fall always shatter the movement?

---

Is the greatest sacrifice in solidarity the sacrifice of one's own perspective?

Does genuine solidarity require that you subordinate your analysis to the analysis of those most affected?

If the most affected have an analysis you believe is wrong, does solidarity require you to suppress your disagreement?

Is silent disagreement within solidarity honest or cowardly?

If you voice your disagreement, do you risk fracturing the solidarity?

If you suppress your disagreement, do you risk enabling a mistake?

Is the tension between honesty and unity the permanent condition of every solidarity movement?

Can this tension be productive, or does it always threaten to become destructive?

Is there a form of disagreement that strengthens solidarity rather than weakening it?

What would loyal opposition within a solidarity movement look like?

Is the concept of loyal opposition inherently liberal, and therefore incompatible with radical solidarity?

Does radical solidarity demand total alignment, and if so, how does it differ from authoritarianism?

## Performative Solidarity

What is performative solidarity?

Is performative solidarity solidarity that is performed for an audience rather than enacted for a cause?

Can solidarity ever not be performed — is there such a thing as purely private solidarity?

If solidarity is inherently public and communicative, is all solidarity performative?

If all solidarity is performative, does the accusation of performative solidarity lose its force?

Or does the accusation distinguish between solidarity performed for the benefit of the cause and solidarity performed for the benefit of the performer?

Can you always tell the difference between these two?

Is the distinction between solidarity-for-the-cause and solidarity-for-the-self even meaningful, given that all action serves multiple purposes?

If posting a black square on social media during a racial justice movement is performative, what would non-performative solidarity look like in the same context?

Is the demand for non-performative solidarity a demand for invisible solidarity, and is invisible solidarity a contradiction?

---

Does the accusation of virtue signaling destroy solidarity before it can form?

If every public expression of solidarity is dismissed as virtue signaling, does that create a world where no one dares express solidarity?

Is the cynicism about performative solidarity more corrosive than performative solidarity itself?

Does the critic of performative solidarity contribute more to justice than the performer of solidarity?

Can criticism of solidarity become a form of anti-solidarity — a way of ensuring that no one acts?

Is the person who does nothing but criticizes those who do something in a morally superior position?

If not, why does the critic of performative solidarity so often assume moral superiority?

Is the critique of performative solidarity itself a performance — a performance of discernment, of being too sophisticated to be fooled?

Is the infinite regress of performance — performing solidarity, performing the critique of performed solidarity, performing the critique of the critique — resolvable?

Or is the recognition that all public action is performance the beginning of political maturity?

---

Can performative solidarity have real effects?

If a corporation's rainbow logo during Pride Month increases visibility of queer people, does the corporate motive matter?

If a politician's expression of solidarity with a marginalized group raises the group's profile, does the politician's insincerity matter?

Can insincere solidarity create the conditions for sincere solidarity?

If hypocrisy is the tribute that vice pays to virtue, is performative solidarity the tribute that indifference pays to justice?

Is it better to live in a world where solidarity is performed insincerely than in a world where no one bothers to perform it at all?

Does the normalization of solidarity performances — however hollow — shift the baseline of acceptable behavior?

If the baseline shifts, does yesterday's performative solidarity become today's genuine expectation?

Can cultural change happen through the accumulation of performances that gradually become sincere?

Or does the accumulation of insincere performances create a culture of cynicism that makes sincerity impossible?

---

Is social media solidarity real solidarity?

Can a hashtag constitute solidarity, or is it merely the appearance of solidarity?

If a hashtag trends and leads to real-world action, was the hashtag solidarity all along?

If a hashtag trends and leads to nothing, was it solidarity that failed or was it never solidarity?

Does the medium determine whether an expression counts as solidarity?

Is solidarity expressed in person more real than solidarity expressed online?

If so, does that privilege those with the physical ability and geographical proximity to show up?

Is the hierarchy of solidarity — street protest over online support, arrest over marching, injury over arrest — a useful guide or a form of gatekeeping?

Does the hierarchy of sacrifice within solidarity movements reproduce the very hierarchies solidarity is supposed to challenge?

Is there a way to honor different forms of contribution without ranking them?

## Global Solidarity

Can you feel solidarity with eight billion people?

Is practiced solidarity without feeling sufficient, or is feeling essential?

If feeling is essential, does global solidarity require a transformation of human emotional capacity?

Is the demand for global solidarity a demand for a kind of person who does not yet exist?

Can institutions substitute for feeling — can a just global order produce the functional equivalent of solidarity without the emotional bond?

Is institutional justice a form of solidarity, or is it solidarity's replacement?

If institutions replace solidarity, have we gained justice and lost community?

---

What would global solidarity require in material terms?

Would it require the redistribution of wealth from rich nations to poor nations?

If global solidarity demands redistribution, and redistribution is resisted, is the resistance a failure of solidarity or a rational defense of self-interest?

Can self-interest and solidarity coexist at the global level?

If citizens of wealthy nations are asked to accept a lower standard of living for the sake of global solidarity, is that a reasonable demand?

Is the demand unreasonable, or is the current distribution unreasonable?

Does framing the question as a demand for sacrifice rather than a demand for justice bias the answer?

If global inequality is unjust, is solidarity with the global poor a matter of charity or of returning what was taken?

Does the history of colonialism transform global solidarity from a generous act into a moral obligation?

If solidarity is obligatory, is it still solidarity or is it restitution?

Can restitution and solidarity coexist, or does the language of debt undermine the language of fellowship?

---

Is solidarity between nations possible, or is solidarity always between peoples despite their nations?

Do nation-states have interests that are structurally incompatible with transnational solidarity?

If the state's purpose is to serve its citizens, can it ever prioritize the welfare of non-citizens?

Is the welfare state a form of national solidarity that excludes outsiders by design?

Does the existence of borders contradict the possibility of global solidarity?

Can you build solidarity while maintaining the structures that divide people into insiders and outsiders?

Is the abolition of borders a prerequisite for global solidarity, or can solidarity be built incrementally within and across existing borders?

If incremental solidarity is possible, does incrementalism always defer justice to a future that never arrives?

Is patience a form of solidarity with future generations, or is impatience the true expression of solidarity with those suffering now?

Is it revealing that the most commonly imagined triggers for global solidarity are external threats?

If solidarity is always reactive — always against — can it be sustained once the threat recedes?

Is peacetime solidarity possible, or does peace dissolve the bonds that crisis forged?

Does the climate crisis function as the solidarity-producing threat that could unite humanity?

If climate change is the common enemy, why has it not produced global solidarity?

Is the failure of climate solidarity evidence that shared threat is insufficient for solidarity?

If solidarity is a process, can it ever be completed?

Is sentimental solidarity dangerous because it is fickle?

Is abstract solidarity dangerous because it is disconnected from lived experience?
