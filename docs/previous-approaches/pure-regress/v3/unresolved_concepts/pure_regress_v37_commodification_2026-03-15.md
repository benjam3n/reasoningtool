# Recursive Causal Interrogation: Commodification

## Everything for Sale

Is there anything that cannot, in principle, be turned into something for sale?

---

What does it mean to say that something has been commodified?

Does commodification name a single process, or does the word conceal a family of distinct transformations that share only a surface resemblance?

Is the commodification of water the same kind of process as the commodification of love?

If they differ, what is the common element that justifies calling both "commodification"?

Could one define commodification without reference to markets, or is the market the necessary condition for anything to become a commodity?

Did commodification exist before the word for it existed, or did naming the process also help produce it?

Is commodification something that happens to things, or something that happens to the way people relate to things?

If it is relational, whose relation counts — the buyer's, the seller's, or the relation between them?

Can a thing be commodified from one person's perspective but not from another's?

---

What did Marx mean by the commodity form, and does his definition still hold under conditions he could not have anticipated?

Is a commodity defined by the fact that it is produced for exchange rather than for use?

If so, does a gift economy contain no commodities, or does it contain commodities that are exchanged through a different medium?

Does the distinction between production for use and production for exchange survive scrutiny, or does every use already imply a kind of exchange?

When a mother cooks a meal for her family, is she producing a use-value free of exchange, or is there an implicit exchange of care for belonging?

If even unpaid domestic labor involves exchange, does that make everything a commodity by default?

Would such an expansive definition drain the concept of all critical force?

---

What is use-value, and can it be separated from exchange-value without remainder?

Does use-value inhere in the object, or is it constituted by the needs, desires, and capacities of the user?

If use-value is subjective, how can Marx treat it as a material property of the commodity?

Is Marx's use-value closer to Aristotle's notion of a thing's proper function, or is it something entirely different?

Can an object have use-value if no one has any use for it?

Does a painting in a locked vault have use-value if no one can see it?

If the vault owner derives satisfaction from mere ownership, is that satisfaction a use-value or an exchange-value in disguise?

Does the collector who never displays art consume use-value or fetishize exchange-value?

---

What is exchange-value, and why did Marx insist that it is not the same as price?

If exchange-value is a ratio between commodities, is it a property of single commodities or only of pairs?

Can a commodity have exchange-value in isolation, or does it require at least one other commodity to be measured against?

Does money create exchange-value or merely represent it?

If money merely represents exchange-value, what does exchange-value look like without money?

Can barter reveal exchange-value in its pure form, or does barter already presuppose some prior notion of equivalence?

What grounds the equivalence between two different commodities — labor time, social convention, power, desire, or something else?

If labor time grounds value, whose labor counts and how is its duration measured across qualitatively different activities?

## The Commodity Fetish

Is fetishism a psychological illusion, a structural feature of market society, or both at once?

Is the academic who critiques commodity fetishism while drawing a salary from a commodified university caught in a performative contradiction?

Does the contradiction invalidate the critique, or does it demonstrate the very inescapability the critique describes?

---

What does it mean to say that commodities appear to have social properties that actually belong to the relations between the people who produce them?

Is this appearance a kind of projection, a misattribution, or a genuine ontological feature of market societies?

If the commodity really does mediate social relations, is the "fetish" not an illusion but a description of how things actually function?

Does money have power because people believe it does, or do people believe it does because it genuinely structures their survival?

Can a starving person choose not to fetishize food by recognizing it as mere matter?

Does the urgency of need dissolve the distinction between real and fetishized value?

---

Is the commodity fetish analogous to religious fetishism, or did Marx use the analogy loosely?

Does the iPhone have power over its user, or does the user merely behave as if it does?

If the user's behavior is indistinguishable from being under the object's power, does the distinction between "real" and "as if" power collapse?

Can one worship a commodity without knowing one is doing so?

Is brand loyalty a secular form of devotion?

---

If the commodity fetish involves a misrecognition of social relations as thing-relations, what would undistorted recognition look like?

Would a fully transparent economy eliminate fetishism, or would new forms of mystification arise?

Does the fantasy of total transparency itself fetishize clarity as a commodity?

Can there be a non-fetishistic relationship to objects, or does all human attachment to things involve some degree of projection?

Did pre-capitalist societies relate to objects without fetishism, or did they merely fetishize differently?

Is the animist who believes a river has a spirit fetishizing the river, or relating to it more honestly than the capitalist who treats it as a resource?

---

Does the concept of commodity fetishism assume a "true" social reality beneath the appearance, and if so, what justifies that assumption?

Is the base-superstructure model itself a fetishization of economic relations as more real than cultural ones?

Can one critique fetishism without positing a privileged standpoint from which the fetish is visible?

Who occupies that standpoint — the proletariat, the intellectual, the revolutionary, or no one?

If no one can fully escape the fetish, is the concept of fetishism itself fetishistic?

---

If a kidney is sold, is the seller selling a part of themselves or a thing that belongs to them?

Does the concept of self-ownership facilitate or resist the commodification of the body?
