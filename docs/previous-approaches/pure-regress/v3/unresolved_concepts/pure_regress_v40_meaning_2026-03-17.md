# Recursive Causal Interrogation: Meaning

## The Signified

- What makes a sign point beyond itself, and is the pointing something the sign does, something a mind does, or something that arises only in the relation between sign and mind?

- If a sign points to something, does the thing pointed to exist independently of the pointing, or does the act of pointing bring the signified into being as a signified?

- Can the signified ever be encountered apart from some sign that delivers it, and if not, is the signified anything other than the residue of signification itself?

- If every encounter with the signified is mediated by a sign, does the signified recede behind every attempt to grasp it, generating an infinite regress of signs pointing to signs?

- Does the signified determine the sign, or does the sign determine the signified, and if the determination runs in both directions, what grounds the circle?

- If the signified is a concept, where does the concept reside — in the mind, in the language, in the practice of using the sign — and does the answer change what the signified is?

- Can two different signs share the same signified, and if so, what makes the signified the same across signs if not another sign that identifies it?

- Is the signified stable, or does it shift each time the sign is used, and if it shifts, can the sign be said to mean anything determinate at all?

- Must there always be a signified, or can a sign point to nothing and still function as a sign, and would such empty pointing be meaning or its annihilation?

- Does the signified have a signified of its own, and if so, does every meaning dissolve into an infinite chain of further meanings with no terminal point?

---

- If we strip away every sign, does the signified remain, and if it does, what kind of being does it have, and if it does not, was there ever anything beyond the sign?

- Is the relation between sign and signified arbitrary, as Saussure claimed, and if so, what force or convention establishes and sustains the arbitrary connection?

- If the sign-signified relation is conventional, does meaning exist before the convention, or does the convention create meaning from nothing?

- Can an arbitrary connection become necessary through use, and if so, does habitual meaning differ in kind from original meaning, or only in degree?

- Does the signified have boundaries, and if so, what determines where one signified ends and another begins, and is the boundary itself a signified?

- If signifieds bleed into one another, is meaning inherently fuzzy, and does the demand for precision distort meaning rather than clarify it?

- Can the signified be exhausted by any enumeration of its instances, or does it always overflow the examples meant to delimit it?

- Is the signified universal or particular — does "dog" signify dogness in general or some prototype or schema, and who or what decides?

- If the signified is universal, where do universals exist, and does the problem of meaning reduce to the problem of universals?

- Does the signified survive translation, and if something is lost in translation, is the loss a loss of meaning or a loss of something else that merely accompanied meaning?

---

- Is the signified present in the sign or absent from it, and does meaning require the signified's absence — a gap between sign and thing — to function as meaning at all?

- If meaning depends on the absence of the signified, does perfect presence destroy meaning, and does this explain why naming God has been thought impossible?

- Can the signified be fully present in any sign, or does every sign necessarily betray what it signifies by fixing it in a form foreign to its nature?

- If signs always betray the signified, is communication inherently unfaithful, and does every act of meaning-making involve a constitutive loss?

- Does the multiplicity of signs for a single signified indicate the signified's richness or the sign's poverty?

- If we could grasp the signified without any sign, would that grasp be a kind of meaning or a transcendence of meaning altogether?

- Is the desire to reach the signified directly — without mediation — the driving force behind all philosophy, and is this desire coherent?

- Can the signified resist the sign, refusing to be captured, and if so, does this resistance itself mean something?

- If the signified is always deferred, as Derrida suggested, is there a signified at the end of the chain, or only the perpetual motion of signification?

---

- Does the signified belong to the same order of being as the sign, or does it exist in a different register, and can entities from different registers truly relate?

- If sign and signified belong to different orders, what bridges the gap, and is the bridge itself a sign or a signified or something else entirely?

- Can the signified be a sign in another context, and if every signified is potentially a sign, does the distinction between sign and signified collapse?

- If the distinction collapses, is there only signification without anything signified, and can signification without a signified be called meaning?

- Does the signified pre-exist the sign temporally, or do they arise together, and does the temporal question matter for the logical question of priority?

- If sign and signified arise together, is their relationship more like creation or more like discovery, and what would each answer imply about the nature of meaning?

- Can the signified be destroyed while the sign persists, and if so, what is a sign without a signified — nonsense, ornament, or a meaning waiting to be born?

- Is the signified one or many — does a single sign have exactly one signified, or a cluster, or a potentially infinite field of signifieds?

## The Hermeneutic Circle

- If understanding a part requires understanding the whole, and understanding the whole requires understanding the parts, is interpretation possible at all, or does the circle render understanding permanently incomplete?

- Does the hermeneutic circle describe a defect in human understanding or a structural feature of meaning itself, and would a perfect knower still encounter the circle?

- If the circle is a structural feature of meaning, does this mean meaning is never fully given but always in the process of being constituted?

- Can the circle be broken by finding a part that is self-interpreting, requiring no reference to the whole, and would such a part be the foundation of all meaning?

- If no self-interpreting part exists, does all interpretation rest on an ungrounded leap — an initial guess that structures all subsequent understanding?

- Is the initial guess arbitrary, and if so, does all meaning rest on an arbitrary foundation, and does this arbitrariness infect everything built upon it?

- Can the circle be entered at any point, or are some entry points more revealing than others, and what determines a good starting place for interpretation?

- Does repeated traversal of the circle deepen understanding or merely entrench initial prejudice, and how could one tell the difference from within the circle?

- Is the hermeneutic circle a circle at all, or is it a spiral, with each traversal reaching a deeper level, and if so, what does the spiral converge toward?

- If the spiral converges, does it converge toward the true meaning, and if truth is itself a hermeneutic concept, does the convergence have any non-circular guarantee?

---

- Does the hermeneutic circle apply to the interpreter as well — must one understand oneself to understand a text, and must one understand texts to understand oneself?

- If self-understanding is itself circular, does the hermeneutic circle extend to all knowledge, making every form of understanding an interpretation without ground?

- Can tradition supply the ground that the circle lacks, and if so, does this mean meaning is always relative to tradition, with no tradition-independent meaning?

- If meaning is tradition-dependent, can members of different traditions understand each other, or does each tradition constitute its own hermetically sealed meaning-world?

- Does the hermeneutic circle entail that there is no meaning in itself — no meaning apart from the act of interpretation — and is this liberating or nihilistic?

- If meaning exists only in interpretation, does the text mean nothing when no one reads it, and does this consign meaning to the contingency of readers?

- Can a text mean something its author did not intend, and if so, where does the surplus meaning come from — the text itself, the reader, or the space between them?

- Is the hermeneutic circle a description of meaning or a prescription for how to seek it, and does it matter which it is?

- Does the circle apply to itself — is the hermeneutic circle itself something that can only be understood hermeneutically, and does this meta-circularity strengthen or undermine it?

---

- If every interpretation generates a new text requiring further interpretation, does the hermeneutic circle produce an infinite proliferation of meanings, and is this plenitude or chaos?

- Can two interpreters traversing the same circle arrive at incompatible interpretations that are both valid, and if so, what becomes of the concept of correctness in interpretation?

- Does the hermeneutic circle presuppose that meaning is something to be found rather than made, and if meaning is made, is the circle simply a description of the manufacturing process?

- If meaning is manufactured, who is the manufacturer — the author, the reader, the language, the culture — and can the manufacturer control what is produced?

- Is there a hermeneutic circle for non-linguistic meaning — for the meaning of a gesture, a painting, a mathematical proof — and if so, does this reveal that the circle is about meaning as such, not just textual meaning?

- Does understanding require sympathy, and if so, can one understand a meaning one finds repugnant, or does repugnance close the circle?

- If understanding requires entering the circle, can one choose not to understand, and is the refusal of understanding itself meaningful?

- Can a machine enter the hermeneutic circle, and if not, is this what distinguishes human meaning from mechanical processing?

- Does the hermeneutic circle have an outside, and if it does, can one stand outside meaning and observe it, and if one cannot, is the study of meaning always from within?

---

- If prejudice is the condition of understanding rather than its obstacle, as Gadamer claimed, does this mean that objectivity about meaning is not merely difficult but incoherent?

- Can prejudice be identified without a standpoint free of prejudice, and if not, does the hermeneutic circle make the critique of prejudice impossible?

- If we cannot escape our prejudices, does meaning become whatever our prejudices allow us to see, and is this relativism or realism about the human condition?

- Does the historical distance between interpreter and text create meaning or destroy it, and can temporal distance be a productive rather than distorting force?

- If the fusion of horizons produces understanding, what exactly fuses, and is the fusion a kind of agreement, a compromise, or something more radical?

- Can horizons fuse without one absorbing the other, and if one horizon dominates, is the resulting understanding genuine or merely the victor's interpretation imposed on the vanquished?

- Does every act of understanding change both the understood and the understander, and if so, is the hermeneutic circle a process of mutual transformation?

- If meaning transforms through interpretation, is there an original meaning that is progressively distorted, or does transformation constitute the life of meaning?

## Sense and Reference

- If sense is the mode of presentation and reference is the object presented, can two senses present the same reference, and if so, is meaning located in the sense, the reference, or their relation?

- Can there be sense without reference — a meaning that presents nothing — and if so, is such meaning genuine or merely the appearance of meaning?

- If "the morning star" and "the evening star" have the same reference but different senses, where does the difference in meaning reside, and is the difference real or merely epistemic?

- Does reference determine sense, or does sense determine reference, and if neither determines the other, what coordinates them?

- Can the reference be unknown while the sense is grasped, and if so, does meaning float free of the world, or does it always implicitly reach toward a referent it may never find?

- If meaning is primarily sense, is a world without objects still a world full of meanings, and what would those meanings be about?

- If meaning is primarily reference, do synonyms have the same meaning despite different senses, and does this make the way we say things irrelevant to what we mean?

- Is the distinction between sense and reference itself meaningful, and if so, does its meaning have a sense, a reference, or both?

- Can the sense of an expression be exhaustively specified, or does every specification of sense require further specification in an endless regress?

- If sense cannot be exhaustively specified, is meaning essentially open-ended, exceeding any formulation we can give of it?

---

- Does reference require a causal chain connecting word to object, and if so, how did the first link in the chain get forged without prior language?

- If reference is causal, do mathematical terms refer, and if so, to what, given that mathematical objects are causally inert?

- Can a name refer to something that does not exist, and if it can, is the reference a relation to a nonexistent thing or a failure of reference disguised as success?

- If fictional names lack reference, do statements about fictional characters lack meaning, and if they have meaning, is reference inessential to meaning?

- Does reference presuppose the existence of the referent, and if so, can meaning about the future or the merely possible be referential?

- If reference cannot reach the nonexistent, the future, or the possible, must meaning rely on something other than reference for its scope, and what might that be?

- Can one refer to something one cannot identify, and if so, is such reference an empty gesture or a genuine act of meaning?

- Does the failure of reference destroy meaning or merely leave it unsatisfied, like a desire for something that does not exist?

- If meaning can survive the failure of reference, is meaning more fundamental than the relation to reality, and what sustains it in the absence of worldly objects?

---

- Is there a third element beyond sense and reference that meaning requires — perhaps force, or use, or context — and if so, does the sense-reference distinction capture only part of meaning?

- If force is the missing element, does the same sense with different forces produce different meanings, and is asking a question a different meaning from asserting the same content?

- Can meaning exist without any force — as a pure proposition uncommitted to truth, command, or question — and would such forceless meaning be meaning at all?

- If context determines which sense an expression has, does meaning exist apart from contexts of use, or is decontextualized meaning an abstraction that distorts the phenomenon?

- Can the sense of an expression be the same in every context, and if not, is there a common core of meaning that persists across contexts, or does context go all the way down?

- If context goes all the way down, can the same sentence ever mean the same thing twice, and if not, is communication a miracle or an illusion?

- Does the sense-reference distinction apply to non-declarative sentences — to imperatives, exclamations, greetings — and if not, are these sentences meaningful in a different way?

- If different sentence types have different kinds of meaning, is there a unified concept of meaning, or only a family of loosely related phenomena?

- Is Frege's puzzle — how identity statements can be informative — a puzzle about meaning, about knowledge, or about the relation between them, and does solving it require a theory of meaning or a theory of mind?

## The Problem of Rule-Following

- If meaning is determined by rules for use, what determines the correct application of a rule, and does the answer require another rule, generating an infinite regress?

- Can a rule determine its own application, or does every rule require interpretation, and does every interpretation require a further rule?

- If Wittgenstein was right that no course of action is determined by a rule because every course of action can be made out to accord with it, does this mean rules are meaningless or that meaning exceeds rules?

- Is following a rule the same as thinking one is following a rule, and if not, what makes the difference, and who adjudicates between genuine rule-following and its mere appearance?

- Can a private rule exist — a rule followed by only one person on one occasion — and if not, does meaning require a community of rule-followers?

- If meaning requires a community, does the community constitute the rule, and if so, can the community be wrong about its own rules?

- Is there a fact about what someone means by a word, or is meaning attribution always underdetermined by the totality of behavioral and mental evidence?

- If meaning is underdetermined by evidence, is meaning a kind of fact at all, and if it is not a fact, what is it?

- Does Kripke's skeptical paradox about rule-following show that meaning is impossible, or does it show that meaning is not the sort of thing that requires the kind of grounding we assumed?

- If meaning does not require grounding, can meaning be groundless without being arbitrary, and what would such groundless non-arbitrary meaning look like?

---

- If a rule can always be interpreted differently, does mastering a language require mastering not just rules but a way of life in which the rules are embedded?

- If a way of life is required, can someone outside the way of life understand the meanings it sustains, and is translation between ways of life possible?

- Does the regress of rules terminate in a practice that is simply acted upon, not interpreted, and if so, is the bedrock of meaning pre-rational?

- If the bedrock is pre-rational, does this mean meaning ultimately rests on something that is not itself meaningful, and can meaning be grounded in the non-meaningful?

- Is the non-meaningful bedrock of meaning biological, social, historical, or something else, and does the answer change what meaning is?

- Can the bedrock shift, and if so, does meaning shift with it, and are there revolutions in meaning analogous to revolutions in science?

- If meaning can undergo revolution, is there continuity of meaning through such revolutions, or does each revolution create an entirely new system of meanings?

- Does the possibility of meaning-revolution entail that current meanings might be fundamentally mistaken, and what would it mean for a meaning to be mistaken?

- If meanings can be mistaken, is there a standard of correctness for meaning, and where does that standard come from if not from the very meanings it is supposed to judge?

---

- Can a machine follow a rule, and if it can, does it mean anything by its rule-following, and if it does not mean anything, what distinguishes meaningful rule-following from mere mechanism?

- If meaningful rule-following requires understanding, what is understanding over and above correct performance, and can the additional element be specified without circularity?

- Is there something it is like to follow a rule meaningfully, and if so, is this phenomenal aspect essential to meaning or an accompaniment that could in principle be absent?

- Can a being follow a rule without knowing it is following a rule, and if so, is the rule-following unconscious, and does unconscious rule-following produce meaning?

- If unconscious rule-following produces meaning, does the speaker always know what they mean, or can they mean more or less than they think they mean?

- If speakers can mean more than they know, is meaning partly constituted by factors outside awareness, and does this challenge the authority of the speaker over their own meanings?

- Does the paradox of rule-following dissolve if we abandon the picture of meaning as determined by mental contents and replace it with meaning as constituted by normative practices?

- If normative practices constitute meaning, what constitutes the normativity of the practices — agreement, power, nature, or something else?

- Can normativity be naturalized, and if it can, does meaning become a natural phenomenon, and if it cannot, is meaning irreducibly sui generis?

- Does the rule-following problem show that meaning is essentially social, or does it show that meaning is essentially mysterious, or are these the same conclusion?

## Meaning and Use

- If meaning is use, does a word used in infinitely many ways have infinitely many meanings, and is the meaning then the totality of uses or something abstracted from them?

- Can use determine meaning if use itself is too varied and open-ended to constitute a determinate content?

- If we explain meaning by appeal to use, must we explain use by appeal to something else, and does the explanation ever terminate?

- Is use a matter of what people actually do with words, or what they should do, and if the latter, what determines the norms of use?

- Can a word be used incorrectly, and if meaning is use, does incorrect use change the meaning or fail to constitute meaning at all?

- If incorrect use fails to constitute meaning, is there always a fact about which uses are correct, and who or what determines this fact?

- Does the meaning-as-use thesis entail that a word used identically in two languages means the same thing, and if not, what besides use contributes to meaning?

- If meaning is use, do dead languages have meaning, and if no one uses Latin, does Latin still mean anything?

- Can a single use of a word on a single occasion constitute meaning, or does meaning require patterns of use across time and speakers?

- If patterns are required, how many uses constitute a pattern, and is there a precise threshold, and what is the status of meaning before the threshold is reached?

---

- If meaning is constituted by use within a language game, how many language games are there, and are they discrete or do they shade into one another?

- Can a word belong to multiple language games simultaneously, and if so, does it have multiple meanings or one meaning that spans games?

- What holds a language game together — rules, family resemblances, social practices, or something else — and is the cohesion itself meaningful?

- Can a language game be played by one person alone, or does every game require at least two players, and does the answer determine whether private meaning is possible?

- If language games require multiple players, does meaning emerge between persons rather than within them, and is meaning therefore irreducibly intersubjective?

- Can one enter a language game without understanding its meaning, and if so, can one come to understand meaning through playing, and does this make meaning a product of participation?

- If meaning is a product of participation, does the spectator who observes without participating grasp a different meaning, a lesser meaning, or no meaning at all?

- Can a language game end, and if it ends, does the meaning it sustained perish with it, or does it linger as a ghost in the language?

- Is the concept of a language game itself part of a language game, and if so, does this reflexivity undermine or enrich the concept?

---

- Does the identification of meaning with use commit one to behaviorism about meaning, and if so, can meaning survive the well-known objections to behaviorism?

- If meaning is not behavior but the pattern governing behavior, is the pattern something over and above the instances, and where does the pattern reside?

- Can the same pattern of use be constituted by radically different mental states, and if so, are the mental states irrelevant to meaning?

- If mental states are irrelevant to meaning, does it matter whether a speaker understands what they say, or is meaning fully external?

- Can meaning be fully external — entirely a matter of how words function in public practice — and if so, what is the relation between meaning and thought?

- If meaning is external but thought is internal, can one think without meaning and mean without thinking, and are these possibilities coherent?

- Does the use theory of meaning explain meaning or explain it away, replacing a rich phenomenon with a thin behavioral surrogate?

- If the use theory is reductive, does it lose something essential about meaning, and if so, what is the residue that use cannot capture?

- Is the meaning of "meaning" itself determined by use, and if so, does the use theory of meaning presuppose what it seeks to explain?

## Meaning and Intention

- If meaning is what the speaker intends, can a speaker intend a meaning they cannot articulate, and if so, is the unarticulated intention still a meaning?

- Does intention determine meaning, or does meaning determine what intentions are available, and if intentions are meaning-laden, can they serve as the ground of meaning?

- Can a speaker mean something other than what their words conventionally mean, and if so, does the intention override the convention, or do they produce two competing meanings?

- If intention and convention conflict, which is the real meaning, and is there a fact of the matter, or is meaning simply ambiguous between the two?

- Can a speaker intend a meaning without knowing they intend it, and if unconscious intentions shape meaning, is meaning ever fully transparent to the speaker?

- If meaning is never fully transparent, does the speaker have privileged access to their own meaning, or can others know what a speaker means better than the speaker does?

- Does Grice's analysis of meaning as speaker-intention successfully reduce meaning to psychology, or does it presuppose meaning in the very description of the relevant intentions?

- If Gricean intentions are already meaningful, does the analysis move in a circle, explaining meaning by appeal to meaningful intentions?

- Can an intention to mean something bring meaning into existence, or must there be a pre-existing system of meanings from which intentions draw?

- If meanings must pre-exist intentions, where do the original meanings come from — nature, convention, divine institution — and does this question have an answer?

---

- If a speaker utters words without any intention to communicate, do the words still mean something, and if so, whose meaning is it?

- Can the meaning of an utterance exceed the speaker's intention — can words mean more than intended — and if so, is the surplus meaning real meaning?

- If surplus meaning is real, does the text have a life of its own, and does the death of the author, as Barthes proclaimed, liberate meaning or orphan it?

- If the author dies, who or what constrains interpretation, and without constraint, is every interpretation equally valid?

- Can intention be recovered from a text, and if it can, is the recovered intention the original intention or a reconstruction, and does the difference matter?

- If intention cannot be recovered, is the original meaning lost forever, and does this make historical understanding impossible?

- Does the intention theory of meaning require a homunculus — a little thinker inside the speaker who means things — and does this generate a regress of meaning-intending agents?

- If the homunculus regress threatens, can intention be naturalized, and if so, does naturalized intention preserve the normative dimension of meaning?

- Can one intend to mean nothing, and if so, is the intention to mean nothing itself meaningful?

- Does the death of the speaker end the meaning of their utterance, or does meaning survive the intender, and if it survives, is it still the same meaning?

---

- If meaning requires intention, do natural signs — smoke meaning fire, clouds meaning rain — have genuine meaning, or only a metaphorical likeness to meaning?

- If natural signs have genuine meaning, does nature intend, and if nature does not intend, must we revise the claim that meaning requires intention?

- Can intention be attributed to collectives — does a language community intend meanings — and if so, is collective intention reducible to individual intentions?

- If collective intention is irreducible, is there a group mind that means things, and does this group mind have beliefs, desires, and understandings of its own?

- Can an institution mean something, and if meaning belongs to institutions, is individual meaning derivative from institutional meaning rather than the reverse?

- If institutional meaning is primary, does the individual speaker merely channel meanings that belong to the institution, and is personal expression an illusion?

- Does the priority of intention over convention or convention over intention depend on the kind of meaning in question, and are there different species of meaning with different structures?

- If there are different species of meaning, is there a genus that unites them, and what would the genus "meaning" mean?

- Can a god intend meanings that no created being can grasp, and if so, are those meanings real meanings or meanings in name only?

## Meaninglessness

- Is meaninglessness the absence of meaning or a kind of meaning — the meaning that there is no meaning — and can this question be answered without presupposing one answer?

- If meaninglessness is the absence of meaning, how can it be recognized, since recognizing requires understanding, and understanding seems to require meaning?

- Can the meaningless be stated, and if it can be stated, does the statement have meaning, and if it does, does it mean that meaninglessness has been expressed or that meaninglessness cannot be expressed?

- If the meaningless cannot be stated, does it follow that everything stateable is meaningful, and if so, are there no genuinely meaningless sentences?

- Does the positivist criterion of meaning — that a statement is meaningful only if verifiable — itself pass its own test, and if not, does the criterion self-destruct?

- If the verifiability criterion self-destructs, does this rehabilitate metaphysics, and does the failure of one criterion of meaning show that no criterion is possible?

- Is "colorless green ideas sleep furiously" meaningless, or does it have a meaning that resists paraphrase, and does the fact that we can identify it as meaningless show that we understand something about it?

- If we understand something about meaningless sentences, is our understanding a grasp of meaning or a grasp of something else — syntax, form, the ghost of meaning?

- Can a life be meaningless, and if so, is the meaninglessness of a life the same kind of meaninglessness as that of a sentence, or is life-meaning entirely different from linguistic meaning?

- If life-meaning and linguistic meaning are different, what connects them, and does the word "meaning" equivocate between them, or does it name a deep unity?

---

- Does meaninglessness require a backdrop of meaning against which to stand out, and if so, is total meaninglessness impossible, since it would destroy its own backdrop?

- If total meaninglessness is impossible, is the universe necessarily meaningful, and does this necessity arise from the universe itself or from the structure of thought?

- Can an experience be meaningless, or does the very having of an experience confer meaning, making meaningless experience a contradiction?

- If experience confers meaning, does a meaningless life require the absence of experience, and is death the only genuine meaninglessness?

- Is boredom the experience of meaninglessness, and if so, does the fact that boredom is experienced — felt — endow it with a meaning that undermines its meaninglessness?

- Can suffering be meaningless, and if it can, is meaningless suffering worse than meaningful suffering, and does this comparison itself give meaningless suffering a meaning?

- If even meaningless suffering has meaning qua meaningless, is meaninglessness a self-defeating concept that always reinstates what it denies?

- Does the threat of meaninglessness motivate the search for meaning, and if so, is meaninglessness the negative ground of meaning — the condition of its possibility?

- Can one choose meaninglessness, and if the choice is meaningful, is chosen meaninglessness a performative contradiction?

- Is the anxiety about meaninglessness itself meaningful, and does this mean that the human confrontation with meaninglessness is always already a meaning-making activity?

---

- If meaninglessness is always already meaningful, does this vindicate the optimist or merely show that the concept of meaninglessness is incoherent?

- Can something be partially meaningless — meaningful in some respects and meaningless in others — and if so, how are the meaningful and meaningless parts related?

- Does the meaningless have a function in the economy of meaning — does it serve as the waste product, the shadow, or the necessary contrast?

- If meaninglessness serves a function, is it instrumentally meaningful, and does instrumental meaning count as genuine meaning?

- Can the distinction between meaningful and meaningless itself become meaningless, and if so, what would thinking look like without this distinction?

- Is meaninglessness a modern invention — a product of the death of God, the disenchantment of nature, or the collapse of grand narratives — and did premodern people live without it?

- If meaninglessness is historically contingent, can it be historically overcome, and would a post-meaningless age be a return to premodern meaning or something entirely new?

- Can meaninglessness be cured, and if so, is the cure a restoration of lost meaning or the creation of new meaning, and does it matter which?

## Context and Determination

- If meaning is context-dependent, and context is indefinitely large, how can meaning ever be determinate, since the relevant context can always be expanded?

- Does context determine meaning, or does meaning determine what counts as relevant context, and if the latter, is meaning prior to context?

- Can the same words mean different things in different contexts, and if so, is there a core meaning that persists across contexts, or does context go all the way down?

- If context goes all the way down, is decontextualized meaning an impossibility, and are dictionaries and logic books engaged in a useful fiction?

- Can context be fully specified, or does every specification of context itself require a context, generating a regress of contexts?

- If the regress of contexts is real, does meaning rest on an ultimately unspecifiable background, and is this background itself meaningful or beyond meaning?

- Does the physical context matter for meaning — does a sentence mean something different on Earth than it would on Twin Earth — and if so, is meaning not in the head?

- If meaning is not in the head, is it in the world, in the community, or in the practice, and can it be in all three simultaneously?

- Can one person's context differ from another's for the same utterance, producing different meanings, and if so, which meaning is the meaning?

- If there is no single meaning, is communication the alignment of contexts, and how can contexts align if each person's context is irreducibly their own?

---

- Does temporal context affect meaning — does a sentence mean something different today than it did a century ago — and if so, do historical texts lose their original meaning?

- If texts lose their original meaning, is historical interpretation a recovery of the lost or an invention of the new, and is there a principled way to distinguish them?

- Does emotional context shape meaning — does the same sentence mean something different when spoken in anger than in jest — and if so, is emotion part of meaning or external to it?

- If emotion is part of meaning, can a purely dispassionate statement have full meaning, or does the absence of emotion impoverish meaning?

- Does social context — power relations, class, gender — affect meaning, and if so, is meaning political all the way down?

- If meaning is political, is there apolitical meaning, or is the claim of apoliticality itself a political move that masks its own meaning?

- Can a context be toxic to meaning — can certain contexts make meaning impossible — and if so, what makes a context hospitable or hostile to meaning?

- Does the context of utterance include the speaker's biography, and if so, does the same sentence spoken by different people mean different things?

- If biography affects meaning, does a speaker's meaning change as they age and acquire new experiences, and is the meaning of their past utterances retroactively altered?

- Can the context of reception override the context of production, and if so, does the reader's context determine meaning more than the writer's?

---

- If meaning requires context, can anything be meaningful in a vacuum, and is the idea of intrinsic meaning — meaning independent of all context — coherent?

## Meaning and Truth

- If meaning depends on truth, does a necessarily false statement have meaning, and if it does, what grounds its meaning if not its possible truth?

- Can a statement be meaningful and neither true nor false, and if so, is the meaningful domain larger than the true-or-false domain, and what occupies the gap?

- If meaning exceeds truth, is truth a special case of meaning, and if so, what additional element converts meaning into truth?

- Does understanding the meaning of a statement require knowing its truth conditions, and if so, can one know truth conditions without knowing whether the statement is true?

- If truth conditions are known without knowing truth, is meaning something like a recipe for verification, and does the recipe itself have meaning?

- Can a statement have meaning in a world where its truth conditions cannot obtain, and if so, is meaning more resilient than truth?

- If meaning is more resilient than truth, does meaning survive the collapse of all truths, and is a world without truths still a world of meanings?

- Does the truth of a statement change its meaning, and if so, does the discovery of truth alter what we meant all along, or does it add a new layer of meaning?

- If truth and meaning are independent, can a false statement mean exactly what a true statement means, differing only in truth value, or does falsity inflect meaning?

---

- Does Tarski's schema — "snow is white" is true if and only if snow is white — explain meaning, or does it presuppose meaning in the use of the same sentence on both sides?

- If truth-conditional semantics explains meaning, does it explain the meaning of the truth predicate itself, or does it take truth as primitive?

- Can a theory of meaning avoid appealing to truth altogether, and if so, what replaces truth in the explanation — assertibility, utility, coherence?

- If assertibility replaces truth, does meaning become relative to standards of assertibility, and are those standards themselves meaningful?

- Can a paradoxical statement — "this statement is false" — be meaningful, and if it is meaningful, what is its meaning, and does the paradox infect its meaning?

- If paradoxes are meaningful, is meaning broader than consistency, and can meaning encompass contradiction?

- Does poetic truth differ from propositional truth, and if so, does poetry mean in a different way than prose, or does it mean in the same way about different things?

- If poetic meaning is irreducible to propositional meaning, is there a kind of truth that belongs to poetry alone, and is this truth a species of meaning-truth?

- Can fiction be true, and if it can, does the truth of fiction show that meaning can create its own truth, independent of correspondence to fact?

- Does the relationship between meaning and truth change across domains — in science, religion, art, ordinary life — and if so, is there a unified theory of meaning-and-truth, or only local theories?

---

- If meaning is prior to truth, does this mean that the world is meaningful before it is true, and what would a meaningful-but-not-yet-true world look like?

- Can truth exist without meaning — can there be facts that no one means and no language captures — and if so, are such facts meaningful in themselves?

- If facts can exist without meaning, is meaning a human overlay on a meaningless factual substrate, and if so, does the overlay accurately reflect or fundamentally transform the substrate?

- Does the pursuit of truth generate new meanings, or does it discover meanings that were always there, and does the answer affect the value of truth-seeking?

- If truth-seeking generates meaning, is meaning a byproduct of the epistemic process, and can meaning be sought directly, or only indirectly through the pursuit of truth?

- Can a statement be true and meaningless, and if so, what would make it true if not something about its meaning?

- If a true statement must be meaningful, does truth supervene on meaning, and does the supervenience relation itself have a meaning?

- Does the interdependence of meaning and truth form a circle, and if so, is this circle vicious or virtuous?

## The Limits of Language

- Can there be meanings that no language can express, and if so, how can we know they exist if we cannot express them?

- If inexpressible meanings exist, do they exert a pressure on language from outside, and is the history of language a history of attempts to capture what resists capture?

- Does every language create meanings that other languages cannot replicate, and if so, does each language constitute a unique meaning-world?

- Does the attempt to say what cannot be said expand the limits of language or merely produce nonsense decorated with profundity?

- If silence communicates what language cannot, is silence itself a kind of language, and if so, does it too have limits?

- Can a language be complete — capable of expressing every possible meaning — and if not, is incompleteness a feature of language or of meaning?

---

- Does formalization overcome the limits of natural language, or does it create its own limits while discarding the meanings natural language could carry?

- If formal languages are more precise, are they also more meaningful, or does precision come at the cost of richness?

- Can a formal language capture the meaning of "I love you," and if it cannot, does this limitation show a defect of formal language or the irreducibility of certain meanings to formal expression?

- If some meanings resist formalization, are they irrational, and if they are not irrational, is rationality broader than formality?

- Does the fact that Goedel's incompleteness theorems apply to formal systems entail that meaning can never be fully formalized?

- If meaning exceeds every formal system, is meaning more like an ocean than a container, and does every formalization merely bottle a sample?

- Can the limits of language be stated in language, and if so, does language transcend its own limits in the act of stating them?

- If language can state its own limits, is the statement itself beyond the limits it describes, and does this self-transcendence reveal something essential about meaning?

- Is metaphor an attempt to circumvent the limits of literal language, and if so, does metaphor extend meaning or merely gesture at what remains unexpressed?

- If metaphor extends meaning, is literal meaning a special case of metaphorical meaning, and is all language ultimately figurative?

---

- Does the existence of untranslatable words prove that there are meanings unique to particular languages, or merely that languages carve up a universal meaning-space differently?

- If there is a universal meaning-space, what structures it, and is the structure logical, experiential, biological, or divine?

- Can new meanings be created, or are all meanings eternally available, merely awaiting the language that will express them?

- If meanings can be created, who or what creates them, and is meaning-creation a godlike act?

- Can meanings be destroyed, and if a meaning is destroyed, is the destruction irreversible, and does the destroyed meaning leave a trace?

- If meanings leave traces, is the history of meaning a palimpsest, with old meanings bleeding through into new ones?

- Does the evolution of language track the evolution of meaning, or can meaning change while language stays the same?

- If language stays the same while meaning changes, can speakers even detect the change, or does the illusion of stability mask a revolution in meaning?

- Is the limit of language the limit of thought, or can thought exceed language, and if it can, is such thought meaningful?

- If thought can exceed language, is there a form of meaning more fundamental than linguistic meaning, and what is its nature?

## Shared Meaning

- Can meaning be shared with a machine, and if a chatbot produces appropriate responses, does it share meaning with its interlocutor?

- If children acquire meaning by participating in practices, do they share the meanings of adults, or do they create their own meanings that gradually converge?

- Can cultural meanings be shared across cultures, or does every culture constitute its own meaning-system incommensurable with others?

## Interpretation and Indeterminacy

- If Quine's thesis of the indeterminacy of translation is correct, is there no fact about what a sentence means, and does this undermine the concept of meaning itself?

- Can there be multiple incompatible but equally correct translations of any sentence, and if so, is meaning multiply realized or indeterminate?

- If meaning is indeterminate, is communication a miracle, a fiction, or merely a practical success that does not require theoretical justification?

- Does the indeterminacy of translation infect all interpretation, including the interpretation of sentences in one's own language?

- If interpretation is always indeterminate, does the interpreter create meaning rather than discover it, and is the creation constrained by anything?

- Can an interpretation be wrong if meaning is indeterminate, and if no interpretation is wrong, has the concept of correctness in interpretation been abandoned?

- Does the indeterminacy of meaning make disagreement impossible, since disagreement requires that two parties assign different but determinate meanings to the same claim?

- If disagreement requires determinate meaning, does the possibility of disagreement prove that meaning is determinate, refuting the indeterminacy thesis?

- Can meaning be vague without being indeterminate, and is vagueness a kind of meaning or a kind of meaninglessness?

- If vagueness is a kind of meaning, does precise meaning eliminate vagueness, or merely replace one kind of meaning with another?

---

- Does every text have one correct interpretation, multiple correct interpretations, or no correct interpretation, and what determines which answer is right?

- If a text has multiple correct interpretations, are they compatible or incompatible, and can incompatible interpretations both be correct?

- If incompatible interpretations can both be correct, does the text contain contradictions, or does the notion of correctness work differently in interpretation than in logic?

- Can an interpretation be creative and correct simultaneously, and if creativity adds something to the text, is the addition part of the text's meaning or the interpreter's?

- If the interpreter adds to meaning, is interpretation always partly invention, and is there a line between interpretation and fabrication?

- Does the passage of time generate new interpretations that the original audience could not have entertained, and if so, are these interpretations discoveries or anachronisms?

- If new interpretations are discoveries, does the text have latent meanings that only future interpreters can access, and is the meaning of a text therefore never complete?

- Can a text mean something in a context its author could not have foreseen, and if so, is the meaning the author's, the context's, or the text's own?

- If the text generates its own meanings in new contexts, does it have a kind of agency, and is textual agency a metaphor or a literal truth?

---

- If all understanding is interpretation, is there a non-interpretive encounter with meaning, and if there is, is it more or less meaningful than interpretive understanding?

- Can one understand without interpreting — can understanding be immediate — and if so, is immediate understanding more trustworthy or more naive?

- If immediate understanding is possible, does it bypass the problems of indeterminacy, or does it merely conceal them?

- Does the fact that we can always ask "but what does that really mean?" show that interpretation is infinite, and is the infinity of interpretation a feature or a bug?

- If interpretation is infinite, does meaning recede before every attempt to grasp it, and is meaning therefore essentially elusive?

- Can the infinity of interpretation be embraced rather than lamented, and does such embrace constitute a mature relationship with meaning?

- Does the interpreter's identity — their gender, race, class, history — determine what meanings they can access, and is meaning therefore always situated?

- If meaning is situated, is there transcendent meaning — meaning that exceeds every situation — and if there is, how can situated beings access it?

- Can the indeterminacy of meaning coexist with the reality of meaning, or does indeterminacy entail that meaning is less than fully real?

## Literal and Figurative

- Can a figure of speech become so conventional that it ceases to be figurative, and if so, does it lose meaning in the transition or gain a new kind of meaning?

- Does sarcasm mean the opposite of what is said, and if so, how does the listener detect the inversion, and is the detection based on meaning or on something outside meaning?

- Can figurative language express truths that literal language cannot, and if so, are those truths less rigorous, or differently rigorous?

---

- Is a parable a kind of meaning that resists extraction into a literal moral, and if so, does the form of the parable contribute to its meaning in a way that cannot be separated from its content?

- If form contributes to meaning, is the distinction between form and content itself untenable, and does all meaning involve an inseparable fusion of what is said and how it is said?

- Can the how of saying change the what — can the same content mean something different when expressed differently — and if so, is there a content separable from expression?

- If content is inseparable from expression, is paraphrase impossible, and does every rephrasing alter meaning, no matter how faithful the paraphrase attempts to be?

- Does the failure of paraphrase show that meaning is essentially embodied in particular words, and if so, is meaning more like a physical object than an abstract idea?

- Does the playfulness of figurative language reveal something about meaning that serious discourse conceals — that meaning is ludic, creative, and irreducible to information?

- If consciousness is required for meaning, do unconscious mental processes that influence behavior operate without meaning, and is there such a thing as meaningless mentation?

- Can a pre-linguistic infant have meanings, and if so, what grounds those meanings if not words?

- If something must be added to syntax, is the addition understanding, consciousness, intentionality, or something else, and can the addition be specified without circularity?

---

- Is meaning a substance, a relation, a process, or an event, and does the ontological category of meaning affect our understanding of how meaning works?

- If meaning is a relation, what are the relata — mind and world, sign and object, speaker and hearer — and can the relation exist without both relata?

## Compositionality

- If thoughts have a compositional structure, does the language of thought precede natural language, and is natural language a translation of thought into sound?

- Can the meaning of an artwork be compositional — is the meaning of a painting determined by the meanings of its elements and their arrangement — and if so, what are the meaning-elements of visual art?

- If artistic meaning is not compositional, does this show that compositionality is a feature of language rather than meaning as such?

- Does compositionality explain how infinite meanings arise from finite means, and if it does, is the explanation sufficient, or is something else required?

- Can compositionality account for the meaning of sentences about meaning — sentences like "meaning is compositional" — or does self-reference create problems?

- If compositionality applies to itself, is the meaning of compositionality compositional, and does this self-application strengthen or complicate the principle?

---

- Does compositionality require that parts have meanings independently of the whole, and if so, can a word have meaning outside of any sentence?

- If words have meaning only in sentences, as Frege suggested, is the meaning of a word an abstraction from sentential meaning, and is the abstraction legitimate?

- Can the whole contribute meaning to its parts — can a sentence give a word a meaning it does not have in isolation — and if so, does meaning flow downward from whole to part as well as upward from part to whole?

- If meaning flows in both directions, is the relationship between part and whole a kind of mutual constitution, and does mutual constitution undermine compositionality?

- Are there holistic meanings — meanings that belong to the whole but to none of its parts — and if so, is holism the death of compositionality?

- If holism replaces compositionality, how is language learnable, and does learnability require at least a minimal form of compositionality?

- Can a word change meaning when placed in a new sentence, and if it can, does it have a stable meaning at all, or is its meaning always sentence-relative?

- If word-meaning is sentence-relative, is sentence-meaning discourse-relative, and is discourse-meaning context-relative, and does the chain of relativity terminate?

- Does the failure of compositionality for certain expressions — metaphors, idioms, poetry — show that these expressions achieve meaning by different means, and are those means inferior or superior to compositional means?

## The Origin of Meaning

- How did meaning first arise — from nature, from convention, from the structure of mind, or from something else entirely — and is the question of origin a meaningful question?

- If meaning arose from nature, is it an evolved adaptation, and if so, what survival advantage does meaning confer?

- If meaning confers survival advantage, is meaning reducible to its evolutionary function, and does the reduction preserve or destroy what meaning means?

- Can meaning have arisen gradually, and if so, was there a stage at which something almost had meaning but did not quite, and what would almost-meaning be?

- Is the origin of meaning the same as the origin of language, or could meaning have existed before language in some non-linguistic form?

- If meaning existed before language, in what medium did it exist — gesture, perception, emotion, social practice — and is this non-linguistic meaning still operative in linguistic beings?

- Did the first meaning require a community, or could a solitary being have generated meaning, and does the answer reveal something about meaning's social or individual nature?

- If meaning required a community, how did the community coordinate on meanings before meanings existed with which to coordinate?

- Is the origin of meaning a chicken-and-egg problem — do meanings require language that requires meanings that require language — and if so, does the circle have a solution?

- Does the mystery of meaning's origin show that meaning is not the kind of thing that can originate, but must have always existed in some form?

---

- If meaning always existed, did it exist before minds, and if so, was there unthought meaning, and what sense does unthought meaning make?

- If meaning could not exist before minds, did minds create meaning, and if so, how did mindless processes give rise to meaning-bearing minds?

- Is the emergence of meaning from non-meaning a genuine emergence, or does it require a hidden meaning in the non-meaning that makes the emergence possible?

- If there is hidden meaning in the non-meaning, is the distinction between meaningful and meaningless absolute, or is it a matter of degree?

- Can meaning arise spontaneously, without intention or design, and if so, is such spontaneous meaning an accident or a natural development?

- If meaning arises spontaneously, does this undermine the idea that meaning requires a meaner — an agent who intends meaning?

- Does the question of origin presuppose that meaning is the kind of thing that began at a point in time, and could meaning be atemporal?

- If meaning is atemporal, does it exist outside time, and what does it mean for something to exist outside time?

- Can evolutionary theory explain the origin of meaning, or does it only explain the origin of meaning-behavior, leaving meaning itself unexplained?

- Is the gap between explaining meaning-behavior and explaining meaning itself the same gap that separates the physical from the mental, and is meaning therefore part of the mind-body problem?

---

- Does the origin of meaning matter for understanding what meaning is, or can meaning be understood without knowing its origin?

- If meaning can be understood without knowing its origin, is the question of origin a genetic fallacy — confusing how something came to be with what it is?

- If the genetic question is fallacious, does meaning have an essence independent of its history, and can that essence be specified?

- If meaning has an essence, is the essence fixed or evolving, and can an evolving essence remain the same essence?

- Does the origin of meaning differ for different kinds of meaning — linguistic, existential, mathematical, aesthetic — and if so, is meaning a plurality rather than a unity?

- If meaning is a plurality, what holds the plurality together, and is the unity we attribute to meaning a genuine unity or a projection?

- Can meaning have multiple origins — arising independently in different contexts — and if so, do the different origins produce the same meaning or merely similar meanings?

- If meaning has multiple origins, does this make meaning more robust or more fragmented?

- Is the search for the origin of meaning itself a meaningful activity, and if so, does the meaningfulness of the search presuppose what it seeks?

## Meaning and Value

- Is meaning the same as value, or does meaning differ from value, and if they differ, can something be meaningful but valueless, or valuable but meaningless?

- If meaning and value coincide, does the meaningless lack all value, and does the valueless lack all meaning?

- Can a meaningless life have value — can activities without meaning still be worth pursuing — and if so, is value more fundamental than meaning?

- If value is more fundamental than meaning, is meaning a species of value, and what differentiates meaning from other species?

- Does meaning give value, or does value give meaning, and if the determination runs in both directions, what grounds the circle?

- Can meaning be negative — can something mean something bad — and if so, is negative meaning meaningful in the same way that positive meaning is?

- If negative meaning is real, does the desire for meaning risk producing negative meaning, and is the absence of meaning sometimes preferable to its presence?

- Does the meaning of life depend on the meaning of "meaning" in the phrase "meaning of life," and does the phrase use "meaning" in a special way?

- If "meaning of life" uses "meaning" differently from "meaning of a sentence," are there two concepts of meaning, and which is more fundamental?

- Can the meaning of life be linguistic — can a life mean something the way a sentence means something — and if so, who reads the life?

---

- Does suffering have meaning, and if it does, does the meaning justify the suffering, and is meaning that justifies suffering a consolation or a mystification?

- If suffering is meaningless, does its meaninglessness make it worse, and if so, does the worsening itself constitute a kind of meaning?

- Can meaning be imposed on suffering retrospectively, and if so, is the retrospective meaning genuine or a coping mechanism?

- If coping mechanisms produce meaning, is coped-with meaning less real than meaning that arises spontaneously, and does the distinction matter?

- Does the meaning of death determine the meaning of life, or does the meaning of life determine the meaning of death, and can either be determined independently?

- If death is the end of meaning, does the finitude of life give meaning urgency, and is urgent meaning more meaningful than meaning that has all the time in the world?

- If meaning requires finitude, can an immortal being find anything meaningful, and does this suggest that meaning is essentially related to limitation?

- Can an absurd universe contain meaning, and if it can, is the meaning within an absurd universe itself absurd, or does it constitute a local island of sense in a senseless sea?

- If meaning is an island in absurdity, what sustains the island, and can the island grow or must it eventually be swallowed by the surrounding meaninglessness?

- Does the creation of meaning in an absurd universe constitute defiance, resignation, or something else entirely, and does the attitude of the meaning-maker affect the meaning made?

---

- Does meaning require purpose, and if life has no cosmic purpose, can it have meaning, and if it can, is the meaning self-generated or found?

- If meaning is self-generated, is it arbitrary — could any meaning be self-generated — and if not, what constrains self-generation?

- If meaning is found, where is it found — in relationships, achievements, experiences, understanding — and is the finding a discovery or a creation?

- Does the question "what is the meaning of life?" presuppose that life is the kind of thing that has a meaning, and is this presupposition warranted?

- If life does not have a meaning, does it follow that life is meaningless, or could life be beyond meaning — neither meaningful nor meaningless?

- If life is beyond meaning, does this transcendence liberate or terrify, and is the response itself a form of meaning-making?

- Can two people disagree about the meaning of life, and if so, is there a fact of the matter, and if there is not, does "meaning of life" name anything?

- Is the meaning of life the same for everyone, or does each person have their own meaning, and if each person has their own, does the phrase "meaning of life" equivocate?

- Does the loss of meaning — the experience of meaninglessness — reveal something about meaning that the presence of meaning conceals?

## Meaning and Time

- Does meaning change over time, and if it does, is there an identity of meaning across change, or does changed meaning constitute new meaning?

- If meaning changes, what changes — the content, the force, the scope — and can one element change while others remain constant?

- Does the meaning of a historical event change as time passes, and if so, does the event come to mean something it did not originally mean?

- If events acquire new meanings, are the new meanings real or projected, and who has the authority to determine the meaning of a past event?

- Does the future affect the meaning of the present — does what will happen change what is happening now means — and if so, is present meaning always provisional?

- If present meaning is provisional, is meaning ever settled, and if it is never settled, is meaning essentially incomplete?

- Does memory preserve meaning, and if it does, is remembered meaning the same as experienced meaning, or does memory transform meaning?

- If memory transforms meaning, does every act of recall create a new meaning, and is the past therefore a reservoir of ever-changing meanings?

- Can a moment be meaningful in itself — meaningful apart from its relation to past and future — and if so, is momentary meaning the purest form of meaning?

- If momentary meaning exists, does it vanish with the moment, and is the attempt to preserve meaning an attempt to stop time?

---

- Does anticipation create meaning — does the expectation of what is to come give meaning to what is now — and if so, is meaning essentially forward-looking?

- If meaning is forward-looking, does the present have meaning only as a means to the future, and does this instrumentalize the present?

- Can the past have meaning for beings who no longer remember it, and if not, does forgotten meaning cease to be meaning?

- If forgotten meaning ceases to exist, is meaning dependent on consciousness, and does unconscious meaning make sense?

- Does the duration of meaning affect its value — is a meaning that lasts a thousand years more meaningful than one that lasts a moment?

- If duration affects value, is eternal meaning the most meaningful, and does the desire for eternal meaning drive the construction of monuments, texts, and institutions?

- Can meaning be instantaneous — can a flash of insight be fully meaningful in a single moment — and if so, does the instantaneity challenge the idea that meaning requires temporal development?

- If meaning can be instantaneous, is time inessential to meaning, and can there be meaning outside of time altogether?

- Does the arrow of time affect meaning — does meaning have a direction — and if so, can meaning be reversed?

- If meaning cannot be reversed, is there an irreversibility to meaning that parallels the irreversibility of time, and does this make meaning a temporal phenomenon in its essence?

---

- Does the impermanence of all things make meaning impermanent, and if so, is the search for permanent meaning a refusal to accept the nature of reality?

- If meaning is impermanent, should one mourn its passing or celebrate its arising, and does the proper attitude toward impermanent meaning affect the meaning itself?

- Can meaning accumulate — can a life become more meaningful over time — and if so, does meaning have a trajectory?

- If meaning has a trajectory, can the trajectory be predicted, and does the predictability of meaning diminish its meaningfulness?

- Does repetition create meaning or destroy it — does doing the same thing again mean the same thing, or does repetition drain meaning?

- If repetition drains meaning, does novelty restore it, and is the human need for novelty a need for new meaning?

- Can a single event have different meanings at different times in one's life, and if so, is meaning subjective, or does the event objectively contain multiple meanings that are accessed at different times?

- If events contain multiple meanings accessed at different times, is the event richer than any single interpretation, and is exhaustive interpretation impossible?

- Does the end of time entail the end of meaning, and if the universe eventually undergoes heat death, does all meaning retroactively become meaningless?

## Meaning and Absence

- Does meaning depend on what is absent — does a sign mean by virtue of what it is not — and if so, is meaning constituted by a system of differences?

- If meaning is constituted by differences, does the meaning of a word depend on every other word in the language, and can any word be understood in isolation?

- If no word can be understood in isolation, does the learner who knows only one word know nothing, and if so, how does the learning of a second word create meaning for both?

- Does the unsaid contribute to meaning — does what is left unsaid in an utterance affect what is meant — and if so, is the meaning of any utterance partly constituted by its silences?

- If silence contributes to meaning, can silence itself be meaningful, and if it can, is meaningful silence a kind of speech?

- If silence is a kind of speech, can one ever be truly silent, and is the impossibility of pure silence related to the impossibility of pure meaninglessness?

- Does absence of meaning differ from presence of meaninglessness, and if so, is the difference itself meaningful?

- Can a gap in a text mean something — can what is missing be as meaningful as what is present — and if so, how do we read what is not there?

- Does the experience of something missing presuppose a sense of what should be there, and if so, does the absence have meaning only in relation to an expected presence?

- If absence has meaning only in relation to expected presence, is all meaning ultimately about presence, with absence serving merely as a foil?

---

- Does the negation of a meaning produce a new meaning, and if so, is the new meaning the opposite of the original or something more complex?

- If negation produces meaning, can anything be said that does not mean something, and is the meaningless truly unsayable?

- Does the void have meaning — does nothingness mean something — and if it does, is this the most fundamental meaning or the most derivative?

- If nothingness has meaning, does this meaning arise from nothingness itself or from the mind that contemplates nothingness?

- Can the absence of a speaker — the dead author, the anonymous text — affect the meaning of what was said, and if so, does the identity of the speaker contribute to meaning?

- If the speaker's identity contributes to meaning, does the same sentence mean something different depending on who says it, and is this a feature of meaning or of interpretation?

- Does the loss of context destroy meaning — if we lose all knowledge of when, where, and why something was said, is the meaning lost — and if so, is meaning fragile?

- If meaning is fragile, what preserves it, and is the institution of meaning-preservation — libraries, schools, traditions — itself a meaningful activity?

- Can something that was once meaningful become meaningless, and if so, does the loss of meaning destroy the thing itself or merely its significance?

- If meaning can be lost, can it be recovered, and is recovered meaning the same as original meaning or a new creation?

---

- Does the trace — the mark of something no longer present — carry meaning, and if it does, is the trace's meaning the meaning of what was present or a new meaning generated by the absence?

- If traces generate new meaning, does every present meaning carry within it traces of absent meanings, and is the life of meaning a constant play of presence and absence?

- Can meaning survive total absence — can a meaning persist when every bearer of the meaning has been destroyed — and if not, is meaning essentially embodied?

- If meaning is essentially embodied, does the destruction of all physical bearers of a meaning extinguish the meaning forever?

- Does the experience of loss generate meaning, and if so, is loss a source of meaning as well as a destruction of it?

- If loss generates meaning, is the fear of loss also a meaning-generating force, and does anxiety about meaninglessness itself produce meaning?

- Can one anticipate the absence of meaning, and if so, is the anticipation a kind of pre-mourning, and does it affect the meaning that is still present?

- Does the fact that all meaning will eventually be absent — that entropy will erase every sign — make present meaning more precious or more futile?

## The Paradox of Self-Referential Meaning

- Can a statement mean itself — can "this sentence is meaningful" be about its own meaning — and if so, does self-referential meaning differ from other-referential meaning?

- If self-referential meaning differs, is it more or less reliable than other-referential meaning, and does self-reference create paradoxes for meaning as it does for truth?

- Does "this sentence is meaningless" have meaning, and if it does, is it true, and if it is true, is it meaningless, and does this regress terminate?

- If the regress does not terminate, is the sentence genuinely paradoxical, and does the paradox reveal a limit of meaning or a feature of meaning?

- Can meaning refer to itself without paradox, and if it can, under what conditions does self-reference become paradoxical?

- If some self-reference is paradoxical and some is not, what distinguishes the two, and is the distinction itself meaningful?

- Does the meaning of "meaning" include itself, and if so, does "meaning" mean more than any other word, since it refers to the very phenomenon it exemplifies?

- If "meaning" means more than other words, is there a hierarchy of meanings, and is meaning itself at the top of the hierarchy?

- Can the theory of meaning be stated in meaningful terms, or does every theory of meaning presuppose what it seeks to explain?

- If every theory of meaning presupposes meaning, is a non-circular theory of meaning impossible, and does this impossibility show that meaning is a fundamental that cannot be further analyzed?

---

- Does the attempt to define meaning encounter the same problems as the attempt to define truth — circularity, regress, paradox — and are meaning and truth structurally analogous?

- If meaning and truth are structurally analogous, does solving the problems of one solve the problems of the other, or are the analogies superficial?

- Can meaning be axiomatized, and if it can, are the axioms themselves meaningful, and does their meaningfulness require a meta-theory of meaning?

- If a meta-theory is required, does it require a meta-meta-theory, and does the hierarchy of theories ever reach a level that is self-grounding?

- Is the impossibility of fully explaining meaning a deficiency in human thought or a sign that meaning is richer than any explanation?

- If meaning exceeds explanation, does this mean that meaning is beyond reason, or does it mean that reason is only one way of engaging with meaning?

- Does the self-referential character of meaning-investigation make it fundamentally different from the investigation of other phenomena, and if so, is the study of meaning a unique discipline?

- If the study of meaning is unique, does it require unique methods, and what would those methods be?

- Can one step outside meaning to study it, and if one cannot, is all study of meaning conducted from within meaning, and does this inside-ness compromise or enrich the study?

---

- If meaning cannot be defined without circularity, must we accept meaning as a primitive concept, and is acceptance of primitives a philosophical failure or a philosophical achievement?

- If meaning is primitive, is it indefinable, and if it is indefinable, can it be communicated, and if it can be communicated without definition, what does this show about the nature of communication?

- Does the primitiveness of meaning entail that we understand meaning better than we understand anything else, since understanding anything requires meaning?

- If we understand meaning best, why does it seem so elusive, and is the elusiveness an illusion created by the attempt to make the familiar strange?

- Can the self-referential nature of meaning-inquiry be a source of insight rather than a source of paradox, and if so, what does the self-reference reveal?

- If self-reference reveals that meaning is always already operative in every attempt to think about meaning, does this show that meaning is the condition of thought?

- If meaning is the condition of thought, can there be thought without meaning, and if there cannot, is meaningless thought a contradiction in terms?

- Does the impossibility of meaningless thought entail that all thought is meaningful, and if all thought is meaningful, is the fear of meaninglessness based on a misunderstanding?

## Semantic Holism

- If the meaning of any expression depends on its relations to all other expressions in the language, can any single expression be understood in isolation?

- If no expression can be understood in isolation, does learning a language require learning it all at once, and if not, how does partial knowledge of a language yield partial understanding of meanings?

- Does semantic holism entail that two speakers who disagree about anything disagree about everything, and if so, is this consequence acceptable?

- If the consequence is unacceptable, must holism be modified, and can holism be modified without collapsing into atomism?

- Is there a middle ground between semantic holism and semantic atomism — a molecular theory of meaning — and if so, what determines the size of the meaning-molecules?

- If meaning comes in molecules, do the molecules overlap, and if they do, does every meaning share some content with every other meaning, and is this holism in disguise?

- Does holism make translation impossible, since no two languages have the same total web of meanings?

- If holism makes translation impossible in theory, why does translation work in practice, and does the practical success of translation refute holism or merely show that practice does not require theoretical perfection?

- Can holism account for meaning change — if a word changes meaning, does the entire web shift — and if so, does every change in meaning change all meanings?

- If every change changes all, is meaning in constant flux, and does the flux make meaning unstable or dynamic?

---

- Does the web of meanings have a structure — are some connections stronger than others — and if so, is the structure itself meaningful?

- If the web has a center, what occupies the center — logic, experience, convention — and is the center more meaningful than the periphery?

- Can the web be reorganized without loss of meaning, and if it can, does this show that meaning is structural rather than contentful?

- If meaning is structural, does the same structure realized in different materials produce the same meaning, and is meaning therefore a kind of form?

- If meaning is form, is it Platonic — existing independently of any particular realization — and does this make meaning eternal?

- Can the web of meanings be infinite, and if it can, does any finite being grasp only a fragment, and is the fragment sufficient for understanding?

- If a fragment is sufficient, does holism lose its force, and does the sufficiency of fragments show that meaning is not truly holistic?

- Can two webs be isomorphic — having the same structure but different contents — and if they can, do they have the same meanings or different meanings?

- Does semantic holism apply to non-linguistic systems of meaning — to cultures, religions, mythologies — and if so, is intercultural understanding as problematic as interlinguistic translation?

---

- If holism is true, does the addition of a new word to a language change the meanings of all existing words, and if so, is the pre-word language a different language from the post-word language?

- If every addition creates a new language, how many languages does a person speak in a lifetime, and is the notion of a single language a useful fiction?

- Does holism entail that the meaning of a word is its role in the whole language, and if so, is the role specifiable without specifying the whole language?

- If the role is not specifiable without the whole, is meaning essentially unspecifiable, and does this unspecifiability frustrate or liberate semantics?

- Can holism be reconciled with compositionality — can meaning be both holistic and compositional — and if so, how do the two principles interact?

- If they cannot be reconciled, which should be abandoned, and does the choice affect what meaning can be?

- Does semantic holism make the concept of a definition incoherent, since a definition attempts to fix meaning by isolating it from the web?

- If definitions are incoherent under holism, what replaces them, and can meaning be conveyed without definitions?

- Is holism a theory about meaning or a theory about knowledge of meaning, and does the distinction matter?

## Meaning and Death

- Does death give life meaning by imposing a limit, and if so, would an unlimited life be meaningless?

- If death gives meaning, does the meaning-giving require awareness of death, and can a being unaware of its mortality find meaning?

- Does the fear of death motivate the search for meaning, and if so, is all meaning-seeking ultimately a response to mortality?

- If meaning-seeking is a response to mortality, does this make meaning a coping mechanism, and is coped-meaning real meaning?

- Can the dead have meanings — can a dead person's life mean something — and if so, who holds the meaning?

- Does the meaning of death itself change depending on the meaning of the life that ends, and if so, does a meaningful death require a meaningful life?

- If a meaningful death requires a meaningful life, can death ever be more meaningful than the life it ends, and if so, does the death retroactively bestow meaning on the life?

- Can one face death meaningfully without knowing the meaning of life, and if so, does the confrontation with death create meaning rather than presuppose it?

- Does the universality of death create a shared meaning — a meaning common to all mortal beings — and if so, is this shared meaning the foundation of all other meanings?

---

- If death is the end of all experience, does the end of experience entail the end of meaning for the dead, and is posthumous meaning meaning without a subject?

- Can subjectless meaning exist, and if the dead have no experience, can meaning pertain to them in any sense other than the meanings the living attribute to them?

- Does the legacy of the dead constitute their meaning, and if so, is meaning ultimately a social phenomenon — something that exists between persons rather than within them?

- If meaning is social, does the last survivor hold all remaining meaning, and does the death of the last survivor extinguish meaning entirely?

- Can a species face extinction meaningfully, and if so, does the meaning of the species' existence depend on someone or something that survives it?

- If no witness survives, is the species' meaning erased, and does erased meaning count as meaning that once was or meaning that never truly was?

- Does the finality of death make the meanings it delimits more intense, and is meaningful intensity a compensation for meaningful brevity?

- If intensity compensates for brevity, does this imply that an infinitely long but low-intensity existence could be less meaningful than a brief but intense one?

- Can one choose death for the sake of meaning, and if so, is the sacrifice of life the ultimate meaning-making act?

- Is the martyrdom that gives meaning to a cause an example of death creating meaning, or is it the cause that gives meaning to the death?

---

- Does the prospect of one's own death make present meaning more vivid, and if so, does the being-toward-death that Heidegger described constitute a privileged access to meaning?

- If being-toward-death reveals meaning, does the denial of death conceal meaning, and are those who suppress their mortality living less meaningfully?

- Can the meaning of death be communicated to someone who has not confronted their own mortality, and if not, is the meaning of death essentially first-personal?

- If the meaning of death is first-personal, does it differ for every individual, and is there a universal meaning of death or only particular meanings?

- Does the manner of death affect its meaning — does a peaceful death mean something different from a violent one — and if so, is meaning sensitive to the form of its ending?

- If meaning is sensitive to endings, does the way a story ends change the meaning of everything that came before, and is life a story whose meaning depends on its conclusion?

- Can meaning survive death through art, and if art preserves meaning, does it preserve the original meaning or create a new one?

- If art creates new meaning from old, is the preservation a transformation, and is transformed meaning still the meaning that was intended to survive?

- Does the awareness that all things die make meaning tragic, and if so, is the tragic nature of meaning essential to its depth?

## Meaning as Gift

- Is meaning something given or something taken, and if given, who or what gives it, and if taken, from whom or what is it taken?

- If meaning is given, is it a gift that imposes an obligation, and does the obligation to respond to meaning constrain the recipient?

- Can meaning be given without a giver, and if so, is impersonal meaning more or less valuable than personally bestowed meaning?

- If meaning is a gift, can it be refused, and if the refusal is itself meaningful, does meaning impose itself regardless of the recipient's will?

- Does the reception of meaning change the receiver, and if so, is understanding a kind of transformation?

- If understanding transforms, can one choose not to be transformed by meaning, and does resistance to transformation resist meaning itself?

- Can meaning be given in excess — can one receive more meaning than one can bear — and if so, is the overwhelming of the subject by meaning a violence or a grace?

- If meaning can overwhelm, does this suggest that meaning has a force independent of the subject, and is this force benign or indifferent?

- Does the gift of meaning create a debt — does receiving meaning obligate one to pass it on — and if so, is the circulation of meaning an economy?

- If meaning circulates as an economy, what counts as meaning-wealth, and can meaning be hoarded, and if it can, does hoarding diminish or increase it?

---

- Is gratitude the appropriate response to meaning, and if so, toward whom is the gratitude directed when the source of meaning is unknown?

- Can meaning be given in words alone, or does it require presence, gesture, tone — the full embodied encounter — and if so, is written meaning always a diminished gift?

- If written meaning is diminished, does the reader restore what was lost, and is the act of reading a kind of meaning-resurrection?

- Can one give meaning to another's suffering, and if so, is this giving an act of compassion or an imposition?

- If giving meaning to suffering is an imposition, should suffering be left meaningless, and is meaningless suffering more respectful of the sufferer's experience?

- Does the gift of meaning presuppose a shared world, and if meaning is given across worlds — across cultures, ages, languages — does the gift create the shared world rather than presuppose it?

- If gifts of meaning create shared worlds, is meaning the foundation of community, and is a community without shared meaning merely a collection of individuals?

- Can meaning be given anonymously, and if it can, does the anonymity affect the meaning — does knowing the giver change the gift?

- Is the most profound meaning the meaning one discovers for oneself, and if so, is the gift of meaning always less than the meaning one earns?

- Does the distinction between given and earned meaning map onto the distinction between grace and works, and is meaning theological in its structure?

## The Weight of Meaning

- Does meaning have weight — does it press upon the one who bears it — and if so, is the weight of meaning a burden or a ground?

- If meaning is a burden, can one be crushed by too much meaning, and is the desire for lightness a desire for meaninglessness?

- If meaning grounds, does the absence of meaning produce a kind of weightlessness, and is the weightlessness of meaninglessness a freedom or a terror?

- Can meaning be too heavy for words — can the weight of what is meant exceed the capacity of language to bear it — and if so, what happens to the excess?

- Does the weight of meaning accumulate with time, and if so, do older meanings weigh more than newer ones?

- If older meanings weigh more, does tradition exert a gravitational pull on present meaning, bending it toward the past?

- Can one choose how much meaning to bear, or is the weight of meaning imposed by circumstances beyond one's control?

- If the weight is imposed, is there an ethics of meaning — should one accept or resist the meanings imposed upon one?

- Does the weight of meaning differ from person to person — can the same meaning weigh heavily on one and lightly on another — and if so, is weight a property of meaning or of the bearer?

- If weight is a property of the bearer, does meaning itself weigh nothing, and is what we call the weight of meaning actually the weight of mattering?

---

- Can institutions bear meaning that individuals cannot, and if so, do institutions have a capacity for meaning that exceeds any individual's?

- If institutions bear meaning, does the collapse of an institution destroy the meaning it bore, or does the meaning seek a new bearer?

- Can meaning be received passively, or does the reception of meaning always involve an active contribution from the receiver?

---

- If meaning belongs to the commons, is the privatization of meaning — through intellectual property, jargon, esoteric discourse — a form of enclosure?

- Does the givenness of meaning impose a responsibility — if meaning is given, must it be received, and is the refusal of given meaning an ethical failure?

- Does the partiality of human meaning indicate that there is more meaning than any human can grasp, and does this more constitute a horizon that beckons understanding forward?

## Meaning and Repetition

## Meaning and Translation

- If desire can be mistaken, are there things that seem meaningful but are not, and does the disappointed desire reveal the illusoriness of the seeming meaning?

- Can meaning exist without any being that desires it, and if it can, is meaning independent of desire, and is the connection between meaning and desire contingent?

- If the connection is contingent, can a being without desires grasp meaning, and is such meaningfulness cold or pure?

- Does the intensity of desire correlate with the intensity of meaning, and if so, is passionate meaning the most meaningful?

---

- Does Freudian desire — the unconscious wish — create meanings that the conscious mind does not intend, and if so, is unconscious meaning real meaning?

- If unconscious meaning is real, does the speaker always mean more than they know, and is full self-transparency about meaning impossible?

- Can desire distort meaning — can the wish for something to be meaningful make it appear meaningful when it is not?

- If desire distorts, is dispassionate inquiry the best path to meaning, or does dispassion flatten meaning by removing the desire that animates it?

- Does the death of desire entail the death of meaning, and if the Buddhist cessation of desire cessates meaning, is nirvana meaningful or meaningless?

- If nirvana is meaningful, is there a meaning beyond desire, and does this beyond-desire meaning constitute the highest meaning?

- Can erotic desire create meaning — is the meaning of a kiss reducible to its physiology, or does the eroticism constitute a meaning-surplus?

- If eroticism creates meaning-surplus, is embodied desire a uniquely powerful meaning-making force, and does the body know meanings the mind does not?

- Does unrequited desire mean more than requited desire, and if so, does the impossibility of fulfillment enhance meaning?

- Is the search for meaning itself a desire, and if the desire for meaning is one desire among many, how does it relate to the desire for happiness, for truth, for love?

---

- Does the frustration of desire generate meaning — does the inability to get what one wants force the creation of meaning as compensation?

- If frustration generates meaning, is civilization — Freud's sublimation of desire — a vast meaning-generating machine powered by frustration?

- Can the desire for meaning be manipulated — can others direct one's meaning-seeking toward their ends — and is such manipulation a form of enslavement?

- If meaning-desire can be manipulated, is the autonomy of meaning-seeking a moral imperative, and does the right to seek one's own meaning constitute a fundamental right?

- Does advertising exploit the desire for meaning by attaching meaning to commodities, and if so, does consumerism constitute a corruption of meaning?

- If consumerism corrupts meaning, can the corruption be reversed, and does the reversal require rejecting commodities or reinterpreting them?

- Can one desire to be free from the desire for meaning, and is this meta-desire itself a form of meaning-seeking?

- If the desire to be free from meaning is itself meaningful, is escape from meaning impossible, and does every escape route circle back to meaning?

- Does the plurality of desires produce a plurality of meanings, and if each desire generates its own meaning, is the self a constellation of competing meanings?

- If the self is a constellation, is integration — the harmonization of competing meanings — the task of a life, and is a meaningful life one in which the constellation forms a pattern?

## Meaning and Attention

- Does attention create meaning — does focusing on something make it meaningful — and if so, is the inattentive life the meaningless life?

- If attention creates meaning, is distraction the enemy of meaning, and does the age of distraction threaten meaning itself?

- Can something be meaningful without being attended to, and if it can, is unattended meaning potential meaning waiting for attention to actualize it?

- If unattended meaning is potential, is the world full of potential meanings that attention selectively actualizes, and does the selection determine the meaningful world?

- Does the quality of attention affect the quality of meaning — does deep attention produce deep meaning — and if so, is the shallow attention of skimming a degradation of meaning?

- If deep attention produces deep meaning, is contemplation the highest form of meaning-engagement, and does the contemplative life access meanings unavailable to the active life?

- Can attention be misdirected — can one attend to the wrong things — and if so, does misdirected attention produce false meaning?

- If misdirected attention produces false meaning, is there a correct direction of attention, and what determines the correct direction?

- Does the attention of others confer meaning — does being noticed make one meaningful — and if so, is meaning a social phenomenon dependent on the gaze of the other?

- If the gaze of the other confers meaning, does invisibility produce meaninglessness, and is the unseen life the meaningless life?

---

- Does meditation — the practice of purified attention — reveal meaning or create it, and if it reveals, was the meaning always there, hidden by inattention?

- If meditation reveals hidden meaning, is the ordinary state of consciousness a state of meaning-blindness, and does the meditator see what others miss?

- Can one attend to absence — can the attention to what is not there be meaningful — and if so, is attentive absence a paradoxical form of meaning?

- If attention to absence is meaningful, does the artist who attends to negative space create meaning from emptiness, and is such creation the purest form of meaning-making?

- Does the span of attention determine the scope of meaning — does the being who can attend to more grasp more meaning — and is the expansion of attention an expansion of meaning?

- If the expansion of attention expands meaning, is the cultivation of attention a moral duty, and does the undeveloped attention miss moral meanings?

- Can collective attention create collective meaning — does a group attending to the same thing share a meaning — and is the shared attention of a congregation or audience a meaning-making event?

- If collective attention makes meaning, does the fragmentation of collective attention fragment collective meaning, and is the loss of shared attention a crisis of shared meaning?

- Does boredom result from the failure of attention, and if boredom is the experience of meaninglessness, does the renewal of attention renew meaning?

- Is the gift of attention the most basic gift of meaning — does attending to another person give their existence meaning — and is love a form of sustained attention?

---

- Does the object of attention shape the attention as much as attention shapes the object, and if so, is meaning a mutual determination of subject and object?

- If meaning is mutually determined, does the object contribute to its own meaning, and is the contribution the object's resistance to being reduced to what the subject projects?

- Can compulsive attention — fixation — distort meaning, and if so, does healthy meaning require a balance of attention and release?

- If fixation distorts, does obsession produce a surplus of meaning — too much meaning concentrated in one place — and is the surplus pathological?

- If surplus meaning is pathological, is the even distribution of attention across meanings the healthy condition, and is equanimity the proper relation to meaning?

- Does the modern economy of attention commodify meaning by trading attention for engagement, and if so, is the attention economy a meaning economy?

- If attention is commodified, is meaning commodified along with it, and does the pricing of attention assign a price to meaning?

- Can one attend without meaning — can pure attention exist without semantic content — and if it can, is such attention a state beyond meaning?

- If a state beyond meaning exists, is it accessible through disciplined practice, and does reaching it constitute enlightenment?

- Does the fact that attention can shift — that one can attend to one thing and then another — show that meaning is mobile, and does the mobility of meaning reflect the mobility of attention?

## Meaning and Memory

- Does memory preserve meaning, and if it does, is the preserved meaning identical to the original or a reconstruction?

- If memory reconstructs meaning, does every act of remembering create a new meaning, and is the past a source of ever-renewed meaning?

- Can a forgotten meaning be recovered, and if it can, is the recovered meaning the same as the original, and who decides?

- If forgotten meanings cannot be recovered, is the past a graveyard of meanings, and does the accumulation of forgotten meanings constitute a loss?

- Does collective memory — the shared memory of a community — carry shared meanings, and if it does, is the erosion of collective memory an erosion of shared meaning?

- If collective memory carries meaning, do monuments serve as meaning-bearers, and does the destruction of a monument destroy a meaning?

- Can false memory carry genuine meaning — can a memory of something that did not happen mean something real — and if so, is the reality of the event irrelevant to the reality of the meaning?

- If false memories carry genuine meaning, does the distinction between memory and imagination collapse for the purposes of meaning, and is imagined meaning as real as remembered meaning?

- Does the involuntary memory — the sudden, unbidden recollection — carry a deeper meaning than the voluntary memory, and if so, does the depth come from its spontaneity?

- If involuntary memory carries deeper meaning, does the Proustian madeleine reveal that the deepest meanings are those that bypass conscious intention?

---

- Does the narrative structuring of memory create meaning that the raw experience lacked, and if so, is narrative the origin of meaning in personal life?

- If narrative creates meaning, does the life without narrative lack meaning, and can animals and infants whose lives lack narrative structure have meaningful experiences?

- Can the meaning of a memory change as one ages, and if so, does the same event have an indefinite number of meanings, one for each stage of life?

- If memories have indefinite meanings, is the meaning of a life never fixed, and is the final meaning determined only at death?

- Does trauma destroy the meaning of memories, and if so, is therapeutic recovery a meaning-restoration?

- If therapy restores meaning, does it restore the original meaning or create a new one, and does the patient recognize the difference?

- Can one inherit memories — can the meanings of ancestors' experiences pass to descendants — and if so, is inherited meaning less vivid but more foundational?

- If inherited meaning is foundational, does personal meaning rest on a substrate of ancestral meaning, and does the substrate constrain what personal meanings are available?

- Does the digitization of memory — the storage of experience in photographs, videos, and files — preserve or alter meaning?

- If digital memory alters meaning, does it add a layer of mediation that distances the experiencer from the experience, and does the distance diminish or enrich meaning?

---

- Does the selectivity of memory — the fact that we remember some things and forget others — shape the meaning of a life, and is the shape accidental or purposive?

- If the shape is purposive, who or what determines the purpose — the unconscious, the self, the brain, or the demand for coherence?

- Can the repression of memory serve meaning — can forgetting be necessary for meaning-making — and if so, is forgetting as meaningful as remembering?

- If forgetting is meaningful, does total recall — the perfect memory of everything — overwhelm meaning by eliminating the selectivity that shapes it?

- Does nostalgia create meaning that the present cannot match, and if so, is nostalgia a meaning-enhancer or a meaning-distorter?

- If nostalgia distorts, does it distort toward something — toward a wished-for meaning — and is the wished-for meaning the meaning of desire?

- Can one remember the meaning of an experience without remembering the experience itself, and if so, is meaning detachable from its experiential substrate?

- If meaning is detachable, can it circulate independently of the events that gave rise to it, and is such circulating meaning the same as cultural meaning?

- Does the aging of memory — the fading of detail over time — concentrate meaning or dissolve it?

- If aging concentrates meaning, does the oldest memory carry the most concentrated meaning, and is the earliest remembered meaning the deepest?

## The Circulation of Meaning

- Does meaning circulate — does it move between persons, texts, cultures, and epochs — and if it does, does circulation change meaning or merely transport it?

- If circulation changes meaning, does every reading of a text alter its meaning, and is the text a different text for every reader?

- Can meaning accumulate through circulation — can a text become more meaningful as more people read it — and if so, is widely read meaning richer than privately held meaning?

- If wide circulation enriches, is the obscure text impoverished, and does the justice of readership affect the justice of meaning?

- Does the translation of a text into multiple languages multiply its meaning, and if so, does the original text gain meaning from its translations?

- If the original gains from translations, do languages enrich each other through the circulation of meaning, and is the totality of meaning larger than any single language's share?

- Can meaning circulate without a medium — can meaning move from mind to mind without signs — and if it cannot, is meaning essentially mediated?

- If meaning is essentially mediated, is the medium transparent or opaque, and does the opacity of the medium add its own meaning to the meaning it carries?

- Does the speed of circulation affect meaning — does quickly circulated meaning differ from slowly circulated meaning — and is the meaning of a rumor different from the meaning of a scholarly argument?

- If speed affects meaning, does the instantaneous circulation of digital meaning produce a new kind of meaning, and is viral meaning a genuine category?

---

- Does the economy of meaning resemble the economy of goods — is there scarcity of meaning, competition for meaning, markets in meaning?

- If meaning is scarce, can it be monopolized, and does the monopolization of meaning constitute tyranny?

- Can meaning be gifted, traded, stolen, or destroyed, and do these different modes of transfer affect the meaning transferred?

- If meaning can be stolen, is plagiarism a theft of meaning, and does the stolen meaning retain its original meaning or become something else in the thief's hands?

- Does the democratization of meaning — the widening of access to meaning-making — enrich the total meaning or dilute it?

- If democratization enriches, is the aristocratic restriction of meaning — the esoteric tradition — an impoverishment, and should all meaning be made public?

- Can meaning be exported — can the meanings of one culture be sent to another — and if so, does the export succeed or does the meaning arrive transformed beyond recognition?

- If meanings arrive transformed, does cultural exchange produce hybrid meanings, and are hybrid meanings richer than pure ones?

- Does the global circulation of meaning homogenize culture, and if so, is the homogenization a loss of meaning-diversity?

- If meaning-diversity is valuable, should it be protected, and does the protection of meaning-diversity require the protection of the conditions that produce it — languages, cultures, ways of life?

---

- Does the circulation of meaning create a semantic commons — a shared reservoir of meanings that all can draw from — and if so, who maintains the commons?

- If the commons is maintained by collective effort, does the neglect of the commons deplete meaning, and is the depletion reversible?

- Can meaning be inherited, and if it can, is the inheritance a gift or a burden, and does the inheritor have the right to refuse?

- If the inheritor can refuse, does refusal diminish the stock of meaning, and is the preservation of inherited meaning a duty?

- Does the circulation of meaning create feedback loops — does the meaning of a text change as its reception changes, and does the changed reception change the text's meaning again?

- If feedback loops exist, is meaning always in motion, never settling into a fixed state, and does the constant motion energize or exhaust meaning?

- Can a meaning outlive all its original bearers — can a meaning survive when no one who originally understood it remains — and is such surviving meaning a zombie meaning or a living tradition?

- If a meaning can survive its original bearers, does the survival depend on continuous interpretation, and is uninterpreted meaning dead meaning?

- Does the circulation of meaning across time constitute a tradition, and is tradition the primary vehicle of meaning's survival?

- If tradition carries meaning through time, does the break with tradition sever the connection to past meaning, and is the avant-garde a meaning-destroying or meaning-creating force?

## Meaning and Finitude

- Does finitude — the condition of being limited — make meaning possible, and would an infinite being find nothing meaningful?

- If finitude enables meaning, does the specific form of human finitude — bounded by birth and death, limited in knowledge and power — determine the specific character of human meaning?

- Can a finite being grasp infinite meaning, and if it cannot, is every human meaning a fragment of a larger meaning that exceeds comprehension?

- If human meaning is always fragmentary, does the fragment point beyond itself to the whole, and is the whole itself meaningful or merely the sum of its fragments?

- Does the awareness of finitude intensify meaning, and if so, is the denial of finitude a flight from meaning?

- If the denial of finitude is a flight from meaning, does the modern project of extending human life threaten meaning by diminishing the awareness of limits?

- Can the finite be completed — can a human life reach a point where its meaning is fully achieved — and if it can, does completion end the need for further meaning?

- If completion is possible, does it require death, and is death the completion of meaning or its interruption?

- Does the finitude of language — the finite vocabulary, the finite rules — limit the meanings expressible, and are there meanings that finitude excludes?

- If finitude excludes meanings, are the excluded meanings the most important ones — the ones that would answer the deepest questions — and does finitude consign us to superficial meaning?

---

- Does the finitude of attention mean that every act of meaning-grasping excludes other meanings, and is the selective character of meaning a consequence of finitude?

- If selection is necessary, does every meaning-choice involve a meaning-sacrifice, and is the sacrificed meaning lost forever or recoverable?

- Can finite beings share meaning across their separate finitudes, and if they can, does the sharing create a meaning that transcends any individual's finitude?

- If shared meaning transcends individual finitude, does community expand the horizon of meaning, and is the isolated individual impoverished in meaning?

- Does the finitude of knowledge mean that meaning always outstrips understanding, and is the excess of meaning over understanding the source of wonder?

- If the excess produces wonder, is wonder the proper response to meaning, and does the being that ceases to wonder cease to find meaning?

- Can finitude itself be meaningful — can the sheer fact of being limited mean something — and if it can, what does it mean?

- If finitude means something, does its meaning differ for different kinds of finite beings — for mayflies and for humans — and is the meaning proportional to the awareness of finitude?

- Does the acceptance of finitude constitute a mature relation to meaning, and is the demand for infinite meaning a refusal to accept the human condition?

- If acceptance is maturity, does the mature person find meaning in finitude itself, and is finitude not the enemy of meaning but its ground?

---

- Does the project of creating lasting meaning — monuments, texts, institutions — represent a rebellion against finitude, and is the rebellion itself meaningful?

- If the rebellion is meaningful, does it succeed on its own terms — does lasting meaning endure — or does it too eventually succumb to finitude?

- Can the ephemeral be meaningful — can a sandcastle, a conversation, a glance be fully meaningful despite leaving no trace?

- If the ephemeral is meaningful, is the demand for permanence a misunderstanding of meaning, and does meaning not require duration?

- Does the experience of running out of time intensify meaning, and if so, is deadline-meaning more vivid than leisure-meaning?

- If deadlines intensify meaning, does the absence of deadlines diminish meaning, and is the life without urgency the life without meaning?

- Can a being that is aware of its own finitude create meanings that outlast it, and if so, is the creation of outlasting meaning the most ambitious act of a finite being?

- If the ambition to outlast oneself drives meaning-creation, is the desire for legacy a desire for meaning-transcendence?

- Does the inevitable loss of all human meaning in the heat death of the universe make present meaning-creation absurd, or does the absurdity intensify the preciousness of present meaning?

- If present meaning is precious despite its ultimate futility, does preciousness constitute a kind of meaning that futility cannot touch, and is the most resilient meaning the meaning of the precious?

## Meaning and Freedom

- Does meaning constrain freedom — does what something means limit what can be done with it — and if so, is meaninglessness a form of liberation?

- If meaninglessness liberates, does the existentialist's claim that we are condemned to be free entail that we are condemned to meaninglessness before we are condemned to meaning?

- Can one freely choose what to mean, or are meanings given by language, culture, and biology, leaving only the illusion of semantic freedom?

- If meanings are given, is the speaker a ventriloquist's dummy — mouthing meanings that belong to the language rather than to the self?

- Does the freedom to create new meanings — neologism, metaphor, poetry — constitute the deepest form of human freedom?

- If meaning-creation is the deepest freedom, is the censorship of meaning the deepest oppression, and does the control of meaning constitute the control of freedom itself?

- Can one be free from meaning — can one exist in a state of pure meaninglessness — and if one can, is the state ecstatic or terrifying?

- If freedom from meaning is terrifying, does this terror reveal that humans are meaning-dependent beings, and is meaning a kind of existential necessity?

- Does the proliferation of choices in modern life produce a proliferation of meanings or a paralysis of meaning, and is choice the friend or enemy of meaning?

- If too many choices paralyze meaning, does the reduction of options clarify meaning, and is constraint paradoxically enabling for meaning?

---

- Does the freedom to interpret constitute a freedom to create meaning, and if it does, is the reader freer than the writer?

- If the reader is freer, does this freedom make the reader responsible for the meanings they produce, and is the reader answerable for their interpretations?

- Can one be forced to mean something — can coercion produce genuine meaning — or does coerced meaning lack the voluntariness that meaning requires?

- If coerced meaning is not genuine, does genuine meaning require consent, and is the absence of consent the absence of meaning?

- Does the freedom of the artist lie in their power to create meanings that did not exist before, and is artistic creation the purest form of meaning-creation?

- If artistic creation is the purest meaning-creation, does the artist who creates repugnant meaning exercise a freedom that should be protected even at a cost?

- Can freedom undermine meaning — can the unfettered exercise of freedom produce nihilism — and if so, does meaning require the voluntary acceptance of constraints?

- If meaning requires constraints, is the totally free being the totally meaningless being, and does absolute freedom negate meaning?

- Does the struggle for freedom generate meaning — does the fight against oppression produce meanings that peace cannot — and if so, is conflict paradoxically a source of meaning?

- If conflict generates meaning, does permanent peace threaten permanent meaninglessness, and is the end of history the end of meaning?

---

- Does the freedom to choose one's meaning entail responsibility for one's choice, and is the evasion of this responsibility bad faith?

- If bad faith about meaning is possible, does it involve pretending that meaning is given rather than chosen, and is the comforting illusion of given meaning a form of self-deception?

- Can one authentically accept given meaning — can the choice to accept tradition be a genuine free choice — or does authenticity require creating one's own meaning?

- If authenticity requires self-creation, is the life lived according to inherited meaning inauthentic, and does this judgment condemn most human lives?

- Does the concept of freedom of speech presuppose freedom of meaning, and if meaning is controlled while speech is free, is the freedom hollow?

- If freedom of meaning is more fundamental than freedom of speech, does the defense of free expression need to be reformulated as a defense of free meaning?

- Can one freely give one's meaning to another — can the gift of meaning be an act of freedom — and if so, is the economy of meaning a gift economy?

- If meaning circulates as gift, does the commodification of meaning destroy its character as gift, and is bought meaning less meaningful than given meaning?

- Does the exercise of freedom always create meaning, or can freedom be exercised meaninglessly, and is meaningless freedom a waste of freedom?

- If meaningless freedom is a waste, does the good use of freedom require meaning, and does this make meaning the purpose of freedom?

## Meaning and Otherness

- Does the encounter with the other — the radically different — generate meaning, and is otherness a condition for meaning-production?

- If otherness generates meaning, does the assimilation of the other destroy the source of meaning, and is the preservation of otherness a preservation of meaning?

- Can one understand the meaning of the other without reducing it to the same, and if understanding always assimilates, is the meaning of the other always lost in the grasping?

- If the other's meaning is lost in understanding, does this loss constitute a violence inherent in all comprehension, and is meaning-grasping always a meaning-appropriation?

- Does the face of the other, as Levinas argued, communicate a meaning that precedes all language, and if it does, is this pre-linguistic meaning the most fundamental meaning?

- If the face communicates pre-linguistic meaning, does this meaning consist in a command — "do not kill me" — and is the ethical dimension prior to the semantic dimension?

- Can the other mean something that I cannot mean — can the other's meaning be radically inaccessible — and if so, does the inaccessibility enrich or impoverish the world of meaning?

- If inaccessible meaning enriches, does the acknowledgment of what I cannot understand constitute a form of meaning-engagement superior to comprehension?

- Does the otherness of the past constitute a meaning-barrier, and if so, is historical understanding always a domestication of the past's radical otherness?

- If historical understanding domesticates, does it destroy the past's meaning in the act of grasping it, and is the past most meaningful when it remains strange?

---

- Does the other's perspective reveal meanings invisible from one's own standpoint, and if so, is the multiplication of perspectives the multiplication of meaning?

- If perspectives multiply meaning, is the limitation to a single perspective a limitation of meaning, and does empathy expand meaning by incorporating other perspectives?

- Can machines constitute genuine others, and if they can, does the encounter with artificial intelligence generate new meanings?

- If AI generates new meanings, are those meanings human meanings, machine meanings, or hybrid meanings, and does the emergence of hybrid meaning represent a new epoch in the history of meaning?

- Does the other resist my meanings — does the other refuse to be what I mean them to be — and if so, is the resistance of the other a check on the tyranny of meaning?

- If the other resists meaning, does the resistance itself mean something, and is the meaning of resistance different from the meaning that was resisted?

- Can strangers share meaning, and if meaning requires familiarity, does the stranger remain outside the circle of meaning?

- If the stranger is outside, does the inclusion of the stranger expand the circle, and is the expansion of meaning-circles a moral imperative?

- Does the experience of alienation — of being other to oneself — generate self-meaning, and is the alienated self more meaningful to itself than the comfortable self?

- If alienation generates meaning, does the overcoming of alienation destroy the meaning it produced, and is reconciliation a loss as well as a gain?

---

- Does the otherness of nature — its indifference to human concerns — constitute a meaning, and if so, is natural meaning alien to human meaning?

- If natural meaning is alien, can humans access it, and does the attempt to access it require abandoning human frameworks of meaning?

- Does the encounter with death — the ultimate other — strip away all conventional meanings, revealing either nothing or everything?

- If death reveals nothing, is the revelation itself a meaning — the meaning of nothingness — and does the encounter transform all subsequent meaning?

- If death reveals everything, does the revelation exceed comprehension, and is the near-death report a translation of untranslatable meaning?

- Can the nonhuman — the animal, the plant, the mineral — have meanings, and if so, are these meanings continuous with or discontinuous from human meaning?

- If nonhuman meanings exist, does the destruction of biodiversity destroy meanings, and is ecological loss a semantic loss?

- Does the vastness of the cosmos constitute a meaning — does the incomprehensible scale of the universe mean something — or is cosmic scale meaningless to finite minds?

- If the cosmos is meaningful, is its meaning accessible to human cognition, or does it require a cognition proportionate to its scale?

- Does the otherness of the divine — the wholly other — constitute the ultimate source of meaning, and if it does, is secular meaning a diminished echo of sacred meaning?

## Meaning and Depth

- Is there a surface of meaning and a depth of meaning, and if so, what separates them, and is the depth more real than the surface?

- If depth is more real, does the surface deceive, and is the interpretation that stays at the surface a failure to understand?

- Can surface meaning be genuine — can what is obvious be the whole truth — and if so, is the demand for depth a prejudice?

- If the demand for depth is a prejudice, does the hermeneutics of suspicion — the assumption that things mean other than what they seem — create the very depth it claims to discover?

- Does depth require hiddenness — must deep meaning be concealed — and if so, is the most meaningful content the most hidden?

- If the most meaningful is the most hidden, does revelation destroy meaning by removing the concealment, and is mystery more meaningful than clarity?

- Can depth be infinite — can meaning go down forever — and if it can, does the infinite depth make complete understanding impossible?

- If complete understanding is impossible, is the pursuit of depth an asymptotic endeavor, approaching but never reaching the bottom?

- Does the experience of profundity — the feeling that something is deeply meaningful — track real depth, or can the feeling be produced by shallow content dressed in deep clothing?

- If the feeling of profundity can be manufactured, is depth a property of meaning or a property of the meaning-experience, and can the two come apart?

---

- Does the depth of meaning correspond to the depth of the subject — does a deep person find deeper meanings than a shallow one?

- If the depth of the person matters, is the cultivation of personal depth a prerequisite for the discovery of deep meaning?

- Can depth be taught, or is it the fruit of experience, suffering, and reflection, and does the shortcut to depth produce only pseudo-depth?

- If pseudo-depth exists, how is it distinguished from genuine depth, and is the distinction itself deep or superficial?

- Does philosophical analysis add depth or merely expose the depth that was already there, and is the philosopher a depth-creator or a depth-detector?

- If philosophy detects depth, was the depth present before the philosopher arrived, and does pre-philosophical depth exist in the same way as analyzed depth?

- Can something be too deep — can meaning be so deep that it becomes inaccessible — and if so, is inaccessible meaning meaningless to the one who cannot access it?

- If inaccessible meaning is meaningless to the locked-out subject, is meaning relative to the depth-capacity of the receiver?

- Does the history of a concept add depth — does the word "justice" mean more after two thousand years of reflection than it did when first coined?

- If historical accumulation adds depth, is the newest word the shallowest, and does a word gain meaning simply by enduring?

---

- Does tragedy achieve the deepest meaning — does the tragic vision penetrate further than the comic, the ironic, or the didactic?

- If tragedy is deepest, does the avoidance of tragedy in art and life constitute an avoidance of meaning?

- Can comedy achieve depth — can the laughable be as profound as the weepable — and if it can, does comedy access a depth that tragedy misses?

- If comedy and tragedy both access depth, do they access the same depth from different angles or different depths entirely?

- Does suffering deepen meaning, and if it does, is the sheltered life the shallow life?

- If suffering deepens, does the deliberate seeking of suffering for the sake of meaning constitute a perversion, and is the masochism of meaning-seeking a recognizable phenomenon?

- Can joy deepen meaning as effectively as suffering, and if it can, is the equation of depth with suffering a morbid prejudice?

- If joy deepens, does the deepest meaning arise from the alternation of joy and suffering, and is the rhythm of life the rhythm of meaning?

- Does the depth of meaning have a floor — is there a deepest possible meaning — or is depth inexhaustible?

- If depth is inexhaustible, is the search for the deepest meaning an infinite task, and is the infinity of the task itself meaningful?

## The Mystery of Meaning

- Is meaning ultimately mysterious — does every explanation of meaning leave something unexplained — and is the residue the essential part?

- If meaning is mysterious, does the mystery reside in what meaning is, how it works, or why there is meaning at all?

- Can the mystery of meaning be dissolved by a sufficiently powerful theory, or does the mystery survive every theoretical advance?

- If the mystery survives every theory, is meaning inherently resistant to explanation, and is the resistance a feature of meaning or a limitation of theory?

- Does the mystery of meaning parallel the mystery of consciousness — are they the same mystery or different ones, and does solving one solve the other?

- If meaning and consciousness share a mystery, is the mystery the mystery of subjectivity — of how anything can be about, directed toward, or significant to a subject?

- Can the mystery of meaning be experienced directly — can one confront the sheer strangeness of the fact that things mean — and if so, is this confrontation philosophical, religious, or neither?

- If the mystery can be experienced, does the experience add meaning to the mystery, and does the mystery of meaning itself become meaningful?

- Does the wonder at meaning constitute philosophy's beginning, and does philosophy end when the wonder is satisfied or when it is acknowledged as unsatisfiable?

- If wonder is unsatisfiable, is philosophy an infinite conversation with the mystery of meaning, and is the conversation itself the highest meaning?

---

- Does the mystery of meaning reduce to the mystery of why there is something rather than nothing, and is meaning the something that exists rather than nothing?

- If meaning is the something, is the question "why is there meaning?" the most fundamental question, and does its unanswerable character define the limits of inquiry?

- Can the mystery of meaning be appreciated without being solved, and is appreciation a relation to meaning different from and perhaps superior to understanding?

- If appreciation is superior, does the proper response to meaning combine understanding with wonder, analysis with awe?

- Does the mystery deepen the more one studies meaning, and if so, is the expert on meaning the one most aware of what they do not know?

- If expertise increases mystery, does the accumulation of knowledge about meaning paradoxically increase ignorance about meaning?

- Can the mystery of meaning be communicated, and if it can, does the communication itself exemplify the mystery it describes?

## The Impossibility of Complete Meaning

- If partial understanding is genuine, does the partiality define the human relation to meaning, and is the dream of complete understanding a dream of becoming more than human?

- Can the incompleteness of meaning be proved — can it be demonstrated that no interpretation exhausts a text — or is incompleteness an article of faith?

- Can the partial be complete in its partiality — can a fragment be perfect as a fragment — and if so, is the well-crafted fragment the highest achievement of meaning?

- If unthinkable meanings exist in other languages, does the learning of a new language unlock new meanings, and is polyglotism an expansion of meaningful existence?

- Does the contingency of human evolution mean that meaning is a biological accident, and if it is, does the accidental origin diminish meaning's authority?

- If accidental origin diminishes authority, does this apply to all evolved capacities — to vision, to reason, to love — and does the evolutionary debunking argument generalize?

- Can contingent meaning be as binding as necessary meaning — can an accident be as authoritative as a necessity — and if it can, what gives the contingent its authority?

- If the contingent has authority, does the authority come from commitment — from the choice to treat the contingent as binding — and is the commitment itself contingent?

- Does the contingency of cultural meaning — the fact that one's culture could have had different meanings — relativize meaning, and is cultural relativism the correct conclusion?

- If relativism follows, can one still criticize the meanings of another culture, and if one can, from what standpoint does the criticism proceed?

- Does the contingency of the encounter — the accident of meeting this person, reading this book, visiting this place — generate meaning, and is the most meaningful content of a life its accidents?

- If accidents generate meaning, does planning reduce meaning by reducing the role of the accidental, and is the overly planned life the under-meaningful life?

- Can contingency itself be meaningful — can the sheer fact that things could have been otherwise mean something — and if it can, what does contingency mean?

- If contingency means something, does its meaning include the awareness of fragility — the knowledge that what is meaningful could easily not have been — and is the fragility of meaning the most meaningful thing about it?

---

- Does the recognition of contingency produce humility about meaning — does the knowledge that one's meanings are contingent make one less certain and more open?

- If contingency produces humility, is the dogmatist about meaning the one who has failed to appreciate contingency, and is semantic dogmatism a failure of imagination?

- Can one embrace the contingency of meaning without falling into nihilism — can the contingent be fully meaningful — and does the embrace require a particular philosophical disposition?

- If the embrace requires a disposition, is the disposition Nietzschean amor fati — the love of fate as it is — and does the love of contingent meaning constitute an affirmation of existence?

- Does the awareness of contingency make meaning more precious — does the knowledge that meaning is fragile make one value it more?

- If fragility enhances value, is the most fragile meaning the most precious, and does the precariousness of meaning constitute part of its worth?

- Can the contingent become necessary — can a contingent meaning become so deeply embedded that it functions as if it were necessary?

- If contingent meanings become functionally necessary, does the distinction between contingent and necessary collapse in practice, and is all practical meaning functionally necessary?

- Does the final contingency — the contingency of one's own existence — ground all other contingencies of meaning, and is the meaning of existence itself the most contingent meaning of all?

- If the meaning of existence is the most contingent, is the search for the meaning of life the search for significance within total contingency, and is the discovery of meaning in contingency the deepest philosophical achievement?

## Meaning and Wonder

- Does wonder precede meaning — is the experience of astonishment the first step toward meaning — and if so, is the being incapable of wonder incapable of meaning?

- If wonder precedes meaning, does it also exceed meaning — does wonder persist after meaning has been found — and is the most meaningful experience one that retains its capacity to astonish?

- Can wonder be directed — can one wonder at a specific thing — and if directed wonder produces specific meaning, does undirected wonder produce universal meaning?

- If undirected wonder produces universal meaning, is the child's unfocused astonishment a more fundamental relation to meaning than the philosopher's targeted inquiry?

- Does the loss of wonder — the jading effect of familiarity — diminish meaning, and is the restoration of wonder a restoration of meaning?

- If familiarity diminishes wonder, does every gain in knowledge come at a cost to wonder, and does the most knowledgeable being live in the least wondrous world?

- Can wonder be cultivated, and if it can, is the cultivation of wonder a cultivation of the capacity for meaning?

- If wonder can be cultivated, does philosophy — which begins in wonder — have an obligation to sustain wonder rather than resolve it?

- Does the wonder at existence — the astonishment that anything exists at all — constitute the deepest meaning, and is this deepest meaning available to anyone who pauses to notice?

- If the deepest meaning is available to anyone, is meaning democratic in its distribution, and is the philosopher no closer to meaning than the child who stares at the sky?

---

- Does wonder at the fact of meaning constitute a meta-wonder — a wonder about why there is meaning at all — and is this meta-wonder the beginning of philosophy of meaning?

- If meta-wonder begins philosophy of meaning, does the philosophy ever resolve the wonder, or does it deepen it with each advance?

- Can the experience of wonder be communicated, and if the communication succeeds, does the communicated wonder carry meaning, and is the meaning the wonder itself?

- If wonder is communicable, does the shared experience of wonder create a community of meaning, and is this community the deepest form of human togetherness?

- Does the wonder at beauty, at truth, at goodness constitute three species of a single genus, and is the genus the wonder at meaning?

- If wonder at meaning is the genus, are all intellectual and aesthetic experiences species of meaning-wonder, and is the diversity of human culture a diversity of ways of wondering at meaning?

- Can wonder be exhausted — can one wonder too much — and if so, does the exhaustion of wonder extinguish meaning?

- If wonder can be exhausted, is the preservation of wonder a finite resource management problem, and does the wise person ration their wonder?

- Does the universe reward wonder with meaning — does the wondering gaze discover more meaning than the incurious glance — and if so, is meaning responsive to the attitude of the seeker?

- If meaning is responsive, does it hide from the indifferent and reveal itself to the attentive, and is meaning not a static property of things but a dynamic relation between seeker and sought?

---

- Does technological wonder — the awe at what human ingenuity has produced — carry the same meaning as natural wonder, or is it a different species?

- If technological wonder is different, does it produce a shallower meaning, and does the diminishment come from the absence of the natural or from the presence of the human-made?

- Can philosophical wonder be sustained across an entire life, and if it can, is the philosophical life the most meaningful life?

- If the philosophical life is most meaningful, does this privilege a particular form of engagement with the world, and is the privilege justified?

- Does the child's wonder and the philosopher's wonder meet at some deep level, and is the trajectory from childhood wonder through adult seriousness back to philosophical wonder the trajectory of a meaningful life?

- If the trajectory describes a meaningful life, does the adult who never returns to wonder live an incomplete life, and is the incompleteness a lack of meaning?

- Can wonder coexist with knowledge — can one know everything about a sunset and still wonder at it — and if it can, does the coexistence show that meaning is not reducible to knowledge?

- If meaning exceeds knowledge, is the most meaningful moment one in which knowledge and wonder converge — in which one knows what one sees and is still astonished?

- Does the final wonder — the wonder at the fact that wondering is possible — close the circle of meaning, returning the inquirer to the beginning with deeper eyes?

- If the circle closes, does meaning complete itself in the act of wondering at its own existence, and is this self-completing wonder the closest a finite being can come to grasping the infinite?

---

- Does the wonder at language — the astonishment that sounds and marks can carry meaning — reveal something about the nature of meaning that analysis obscures?

- If wonder at language reveals, does the revelation come from the pre-theoretical encounter with language's strangeness, and is the theorist who has lost the strangeness worse off?

- Can one wonder at one's own capacity for meaning — can the meaning-making being be astonished by its own meaning-making — and is this reflexive wonder the ground of self-awareness?

- If reflexive wonder grounds self-awareness, does the self know itself through its capacity for meaning, and is the self fundamentally a meaning-making being?

- Does the extinction of wonder in a culture portend the extinction of meaning, and is the death of wonder the premonition of a civilization's semantic death?

- If the death of wonder is a premonition, can the premonition serve as a warning, and is the revival of wonder the revival of meaning?

- Can artificial intelligence wonder, and if it cannot, does this show that meaning requires the capacity for astonishment, and is meaning therefore essentially tied to the capacity to be surprised by existence?

- If meaning requires the capacity for surprise, is the perfectly predictive intelligence the perfectly meaningless intelligence, and does the price of omniscience include the loss of meaning?

- Does the wonder that began this inquiry persist at its end, or has the asking of questions transformed the wonder into something else — something richer, deeper, more awake?

- If the wonder has transformed, has the transformation been a journey through meaning or a journey of meaning, and is the distinction between traveling through and traveling as the final question meaning asks of itself?

---

- Does the question that ends an inquiry carry more meaning than the question that begins it, and is the terminal question the fruit of everything that preceded it?

- If terminal questions carry more meaning, does the depth of an inquiry determine the depth of its final question, and is the deepest question the one that can only be asked after exhaustive exploration?

- Can a question be so deep that it cannot be answered, only inhabited, and is the inhabitation of unanswerable questions the philosophical life?

- If the philosophical life is the inhabitation of questions, does the philosopher live in meaning rather than possess it, and is living in meaning different from having meaning?

- Does the question "what does meaning mean?" exhaust itself in being asked, or does it renew itself with each asking, and is the renewal the proof that meaning is inexhaustible?

- If meaning is inexhaustible, does every question about meaning open onto further questions, and is the infinity of questions the infinity of meaning itself?

- Can the final question about meaning be formulated, or does every formulation prove to be penultimate, succeeded by a question it did not anticipate?

- If no question is truly final, does the inquiry into meaning have no end, and is the endlessness a consolation — the assurance that meaning will always be there to wonder about?

- Does the recursive structure of meaning-inquiry — questions generating questions generating questions — mirror the recursive structure of meaning itself, and is the inquiry the phenomenon?

- If the inquiry is the phenomenon, does the question of meaning answer itself in the very act of being asked, and is the asking of questions the deepest meaning of all?
