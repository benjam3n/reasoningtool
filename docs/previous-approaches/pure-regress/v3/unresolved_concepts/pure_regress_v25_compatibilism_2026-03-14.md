# Recursive Causal Interrogation: Compatibilism

## The Nature of Compatibility

What does it mean to claim that two seemingly contradictory propositions can both be true?

If determinism holds that every event is necessitated by prior causes, and free will holds that agents could have done otherwise, how can both be affirmed without contradiction?

Is compatibilism a philosophical discovery or a linguistic maneuver?

Does calling free will and determinism "compatible" change the nature of either concept, or only the relationship we assert between them?

What precisely is being made compatible with what — and who decides the terms of compatibility?

If compatibilism redefines free will to mean something other than the ability to have done otherwise, is it still talking about free will at all?

Can a redefinition count as a solution, or does it merely dissolve the problem by changing the subject?

Is the feeling that compatibilism "cheats" a philosophical objection or a psychological resistance to an unfamiliar framework?

When we say free will is "compatible" with determinism, are we describing a logical relationship or expressing a preference for how to use words?

Does the compatibility claim rest on metaphysics, semantics, or pragmatics?

---

Can two things be compatible in theory but incompatible in practice?

If compatibilism works as a philosophical framework but fails to capture what ordinary people mean by "free will," does that matter?

Is the ordinary intuition about free will — that one genuinely could have done otherwise in exactly the same circumstances — philosophically naive, or is it pointing at something real that compatibilism abandons?

Does compatibilism owe an account of why the incompatibilist intuition is so persistent if it is ultimately mistaken?

Can a philosophical position be correct even if almost no one pre-theoretically holds it?

Is compatibilism revisionary or descriptive — does it tell us what free will really is, or what it should be taken to mean?

What is the difference between saying "free will is compatible with determinism" and saying "what I call free will is compatible with determinism"?

Does the compatibilist owe us an argument that their notion of free will is the one worth caring about?

If multiple conceptions of free will are available, what makes one more legitimate than another?

Can the question of which conception of free will is "correct" itself be answered, or is it a choice that precedes argument?
